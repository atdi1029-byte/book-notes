Walk through how RSI is built, step by step, like you're explaining to a friend.	Look at the last 14 candles. For each one, check: did price go up or down from the previous close? If it went up, write down how much. If it went down, write down how much. Now you have two lists — your "up days" and your "down days." Average the up days. Average the down days. Divide the up average by the down average — that's your RS number. If up average is twice the down average, RS = 2. Then plug RS into: 100 - (100 / (1 + RS)). That spits out a number between 0 and 100. Done. That's RSI.	RSI::Ch2_HowItWorks
What is RS (Relative Strength) in the RSI formula, in simple terms?	RS is just: average gain divided by average loss. If over the last 14 days, the average up-move was $1.50 and the average down-move was $0.75, then RS = 1.50 / 0.75 = 2. That means up-moves have been twice as big as down-moves. The bigger RS is, the more dominant the buyers have been.	RSI::Ch2_HowItWorks
What does the RSI formula (100 - 100/(1+RS)) actually do to the RS number?	It just squishes RS onto a 0-to-100 scale so you can compare anything. RS can be any number from 0 to infinity, which is hard to read. The formula converts it: RS=0 becomes RSI=0, RS=1 becomes RSI=50, RS=2.33 becomes RSI=70, RS approaching infinity becomes RSI approaching 100. It's just a normalizer.	RSI::Ch2_HowItWorks
If a stock went up 7 out of the last 14 days and down 7, and the average gain equaled the average loss, what's the RSI?	RSI = 50. Because RS = average gain / average loss = 1, and plugging in: 100 - (100 / (1 + 1)) = 100 - 50 = 50. RSI of 50 means the bulls and bears are perfectly balanced.	RSI::Ch2_HowItWorks
If a stock went up every single day for the last 14 days, what's the RSI?	RSI approaches 100. Average loss = 0, so RS = average gain / 0 = infinity. 100 - (100 / (1 + infinity)) = 100 - 0 = 100. All gains, no losses, maximum RSI.	RSI::Ch2_HowItWorks
If a stock went down every single day for the last 14 days, what's the RSI?	RSI = 0. Average gain = 0, so RS = 0 / average loss = 0. 100 - (100 / (1 + 0)) = 100 - 100 = 0. All losses, no gains, minimum RSI.	RSI::Ch2_HowItWorks
What does RSI 70 actually mean in terms of gains vs losses?	RS = 2.33, meaning the average gain over the period is 2.3 times bigger than the average loss. Buyers have been dominating by more than a 2-to-1 ratio. That's why it's considered "overbought" — the up-moves have been way bigger than the down-moves.	RSI::Ch2_HowItWorks
What does RSI 30 actually mean in terms of gains vs losses?	RS = 0.43, meaning the average loss is about 2.3 times bigger than the average gain. Sellers have been dominating by more than 2-to-1. That's why it's considered "oversold" — the down-moves have been way bigger than the up-moves.	RSI::Ch2_HowItWorks
Why does RSI use 14 days? Is there anything magic about it?	Wilder picked 14 because it's half a lunar cycle (28 days / 2). He thought commodity markets had natural rhythms tied to lunar cycles. Whether that's real or not doesn't matter anymore — 14 became the standard because millions of traders use it, making it a self-fulfilling prophecy. Every platform defaults to 14.	RSI::Ch2_HowItWorks
After the first RSI value, how does Wilder keep updating it? (plain English)	He doesn't recalculate the full average each time. Instead he takes yesterday's average, multiplies it by 13 (giving heavy weight to history), adds today's new value, and divides by 14. This "rolling" method means old data fades out slowly but never fully disappears. It makes RSI smoother and less jumpy than if you recalculated from scratch each day.	RSI::Ch2_HowItWorks
How does the Stochastic work, explained simply?	Look at the last 14 candles. Find the highest high and the lowest low — that's the range. Now look at where today's close sits within that range. Close at the very top = 100. Close at the very bottom = 0. Close right in the middle = 50. That's %K. Then smooth it with a 3-day average to get %D. That's it — it just tells you where the close is sitting within the recent price range.	RSI::Ch2_HowItWorks
RSI vs Stochastic: what's the difference in what they're actually calculating?	RSI adds up how big the up-moves are vs how big the down-moves are — it cares about the SIZE of moves. Stochastic just looks at where the close landed within the high-low range — it cares about POSITION. A stock could close near the top of its range (high stochastic) even if the actual gains were small, as long as it's near the ceiling. RSI would read lower in that case because the gains were small.	RSI::Ch2_HowItWorks
Who created the RSI, and when was it published?	J. Welles Wilder Jr. published the RSI in June 1978, in both Commodities magazine and his book "New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems."	RSI::Ch1_Origins
What was Wilder's educational and professional background before trading?	Mechanical engineering degree from NC State (1962), served in U.S. Navy during Korean War, then worked in real estate development before pivoting to commodity futures trading.	RSI::Ch1_Origins
What six indicators did Wilder publish in his 1978 book?	RSI, ATR (Average True Range), Parabolic SAR, DMI (Directional Movement Index), ADX (Average Directional Index), and CSI (Commodity Selection Index).	RSI::Ch1_Origins
Why are Wilder's indicators so elegant and simple?	He developed them before personal computers existed. Every formula had to be hand-calculated with pencil and paper, so complexity was not an option. This constraint forced elegant, efficient designs.	RSI::Ch1_Origins
Why is the name "Relative Strength Index" confusing?	In traditional securities analysis, "relative strength" means comparing one stock against another (external). Wilder's RSI measures a security's price strength relative to its own recent history (internal). Same name, different meaning.	RSI::Ch1_Origins
What is the RSI formula?	RSI = 100 - (100 / (1 + RS)), where RS = Average Gain / Average Loss over the lookback period.	RSI::Ch2_Math
How is the first RSI value (seed) calculated?	Using a simple arithmetic average: First Avg Gain = Sum of Gains over 14 bars / 14. First Avg Loss = Sum of Losses over 14 bars / 14. This SMA seed is calculated only once.	RSI::Ch2_Math
What smoothing method does Wilder use after the RSI seed value?	Wilder's Smoothed Moving Average (SMMA/RMA): Avg = ((Prior Avg x (n-1)) + Current Value) / n. This is NOT an SMA or standard EMA.	RSI::Ch2_Math
How does Wilder's SMMA relate to a standard EMA?	Wilder's SMMA is equivalent to an EMA with alpha = 1/n. A standard EMA uses alpha = 2/(n+1). For n=14, Wilder's alpha is 0.0714 vs standard EMA's 0.1333, making Wilder's version smoother and slower.	RSI::Ch2_Math
A 14-period Wilder RSI behaves like what period standard EMA in terms of responsiveness?	A 27-period standard EMA. Because Wilder's smoothing decays more slowly (alpha = 1/14 = 0.0714), it gives more weight to older data.	RSI::Ch2_Math
Why did Wilder choose 14 periods as the default?	Based on the half lunar cycle: a 28-day lunar month divided by 2 = 14 trading days. He believed commodity markets exhibited natural rhythms tied to lunar cycles.	RSI::Ch2_Math
What RSI value do you get when gains equal losses?	RSI = 50. When RS = 1 (gains equal losses), the formula gives 100 - (100 / (1 + 1)) = 50.	RSI::Ch2_Math
What RSI values correspond to RS of 2.33 and 0.43?	RS = 2.33 (gains 2.3x losses) gives RSI = 70. RS = 0.43 (losses 2.3x gains) gives RSI = 30.	RSI::Ch2_Math
What is the "data length dependency" problem in Wilder's RSI?	Because Wilder's SMMA never truly forgets old data (exponential decay never reaches zero), the starting point in a dataset affects every subsequent RSI value. Two platforms starting from different historical dates will produce slightly different RSI readings.	RSI::Ch2_Math
Who developed a fix for the data length dependency problem, and how?	Peter Cutler replaced Wilder's SMMA with a simple moving average (SMA), which has a fixed window where old data drops off cleanly. This produces identical values regardless of where you start calculating.	RSI::Ch2_Math
What are the standard overbought/oversold RSI levels?	Above 70 = overbought, below 30 = oversold. These have been the default on every charting platform since 1978.	RSI::Ch3_Signals
What is the #1 RSI mistake traders make?	Treating overbought as "sell" and oversold as "buy" without trend context. In a strong uptrend, RSI above 70 often means "strongly trending up," not "about to reverse."	RSI::Ch3_Signals
What is bullish RSI divergence?	Price makes a lower low, but RSI makes a higher low. The new price low is not confirmed by new momentum low, suggesting selling pressure is weakening.	RSI::Ch3_Signals
What is bearish RSI divergence?	Price makes a higher high, but RSI makes a lower high. The new price high is not confirmed by new momentum high, suggesting buying pressure is weakening.	RSI::Ch3_Signals
What is hidden divergence, and what does it signal?	Hidden divergence signals trend continuation (not reversal). Bullish hidden: price makes higher low, RSI makes lower low = expect continuation up. Bearish hidden: price makes lower high, RSI makes higher high = expect continuation down.	RSI::Ch3_Signals
Memory rule: Regular divergence vs Hidden divergence?	Regular divergence = Reversal signal (price and RSI disagree, expect price to follow RSI). Hidden divergence = Continuation signal (price and RSI disagree, expect existing trend to continue).	RSI::Ch3_Signals
What was Wilder's own favorite RSI signal?	The failure swing. He considered it the most reliable RSI signal, more reliable than divergence.	RSI::Ch3_Signals
Describe a bullish failure swing pattern.	1) RSI drops below 30 (oversold). 2) RSI bounces back above 30. 3) RSI pulls back but holds above 30 (cannot re-enter oversold). 4) RSI breaks above the prior bounce high = buy signal. The failure to re-enter oversold shows sellers are exhausted.	RSI::Ch3_Signals
Describe a bearish failure swing pattern.	1) RSI rises above 70 (overbought). 2) RSI pulls back below 70. 3) RSI bounces but stays below 70 (cannot re-enter overbought). 4) RSI breaks below the prior pullback low = sell signal. Buyers are exhausted.	RSI::Ch3_Signals
Why are RSI trendlines considered the most underused RSI technique?	RSI trendline breaks frequently precede price trendline breaks by several bars. Momentum shifts before price does. Drawing trendlines on the RSI panel gives advance warning that a price trend is about to break.	RSI::Ch3_Signals
On what timeframes do RSI trendlines work best?	Higher timeframes (daily and weekly charts). On intraday charts, noise produces too many false trendline breaks.	RSI::Ch3_Signals
Who is Andrew Cardwell, and why is he important for RSI?	Known as "Dr. RSI," Cardwell trained directly under Wilder then spent decades extending RSI theory. His two major contributions (range shifts and positive/negative reversals) fixed the biggest problem with Wilder's original framework.	RSI::Ch4_Cardwell
What is Cardwell's range shift discovery?	RSI does not oscillate symmetrically between 0-100. In bull markets, RSI oscillates between 40-80. In bear markets, RSI oscillates between 20-60. The thresholds shift depending on the prevailing trend.	RSI::Ch4_Cardwell
In a bull market (Cardwell ranges), what RSI level acts as "oversold"?	RSI touching 40-50 in a bull market is the equivalent of "oversold" (not 30). Pullbacks to 40 are buying opportunities. RSI rarely drops below 40 unless the trend is reversing.	RSI::Ch4_Cardwell
In a bear market (Cardwell ranges), what RSI level acts as "overbought"?	RSI touching 50-60 in a bear market is the equivalent of "overbought" (not 70). Rallies to 60 are shorting opportunities.	RSI::Ch4_Cardwell
How do Cardwell range shifts signal trend changes?	When RSI breaks out of one range and establishes in another. A market in the 20-60 bear range that pushes above 60 and holds = shifted to bull range, trend has changed. A market in the 40-80 bull range that breaks below 40 = shifted to bear range.	RSI::Ch4_Cardwell
What is a Cardwell "positive reversal"?	Price makes a lower low but RSI makes a higher low (looks like classic bullish divergence). But if this occurs within an uptrend (RSI in 40-80 bull range), it's a continuation signal, NOT a reversal. The dip is a buying opportunity.	RSI::Ch4_Cardwell
What is a Cardwell "negative reversal"?	Price makes a higher high but RSI makes a lower high (looks like bearish divergence). But if this occurs in a downtrend (RSI in 20-60 bear range), it's a continuation signal predicting price will go lower. The rally is a shorting opportunity.	RSI::Ch4_Cardwell
The Cardwell Rule: When is a divergence pattern reversal vs continuation?	It depends entirely on the prevailing trend context. Same pattern, opposite interpretation depending on which RSI range is active. Most "bearish divergence" in uptrends are actually positive reversals (continuation).	RSI::Ch4_Cardwell
Who created the Stochastic Oscillator, and when?	George C. Lane, developed in the mid-1950s at Investment Educators in Chicago with a group of floor traders from the CBOT, CME, and MidAmerica Commodity Exchange. First taught in seminars starting in 1957.	RSI::Ch5_Stochastic
What fundamental question does the Stochastic Oscillator ask vs RSI?	RSI: "Are recent gains bigger than recent losses?" (momentum magnitude). Stochastic: "Where is price closing within its recent range?" (position within range). Both are momentum oscillators measuring different aspects.	RSI::Ch5_Stochastic
Why are the stochastic lines called %K and %D?	They were sequence labels from the development process. The group tested oscillator candidates named %A, %B, %C, %D, %K, %R, etc. %K was the raw oscillator that worked, %D was the smoothed signal line. Not abbreviations for anything.	RSI::Ch5_Stochastic
What is the Stochastic %K formula?	%K = ((Close - Lowest Low over n periods) / (Highest High over n - Lowest Low over n)) x 100. Default n=14. %K=100 means close is at the top of the range, %K=0 means at the bottom.	RSI::Ch5_Stochastic
What is Stochastic %D?	%D = 3-period SMA of %K. It's a smoothed version of %K that acts as a trigger/signal line. Crossovers of %K through %D generate buy/sell signals.	RSI::Ch5_Stochastic
What's the difference between Fast, Slow, and Full Stochastic?	Fast: raw %K + SMA(3) of %K as %D (very noisy). Slow: Fast %D becomes Slow %K, then SMA(3) of that = Slow %D (one extra smoothing, most commonly used). Full: fully parameterized, slowing=1 equals Fast, slowing=3 equals Slow.	RSI::Ch5_Stochastic
What stochastic OB/OS levels are standard?	Above 80 = overbought, below 20 = oversold. Same caveats as RSI: in strong trends, stochastics can camp in OB or OS for extended periods.	RSI::Ch5_Stochastic
What did George Lane consider the highest-quality stochastic signal?	Divergence, not crossovers alone. He also emphasized watching %D (not %K), saying the signal was in the turns of %D.	RSI::Ch5_Stochastic
Lane's observation about closing prices in trends?	In uptrends, closes tend to accumulate near the top of the range. In downtrends, near the bottom. As a trend fades, closes drift away from the range extreme. The stochastic captures this drift.	RSI::Ch5_Stochastic
How does Cutler's RSI differ from Wilder's RSI?	Cutler replaces Wilder's SMMA with a simple moving average (SMA). This fixes the data length dependency problem. Tradeoff: slightly more reactive, crosses OB/OS more frequently.	RSI::Ch6_Variants
What is RSI(2) and who popularized it?	Wilder's exact RSI formula with period set to 2 instead of 14. Popularized by Larry Connors and Cesar Alvarez in their 2008 book "Short-Term Trading Strategies That Work" for mean-reversion in equities.	RSI::Ch6_Variants
Describe Connors' RSI(2) mean-reversion strategy.	1) Stock above 200-day SMA (long-term uptrend). 2) Buy when RSI(2) drops below 10. 3) Exit when price crosses above the 5-day SMA. The more extreme the RSI(2) reading, the stronger the bounce (below 5 > below 10 > below 15).	RSI::Ch6_Variants
What are the three components of Connors RSI (CRSI)?	1) RSI(3): fast standard RSI. 2) Streak RSI: RSI(2) applied to consecutive up/down close counts. 3) ROC Percent Rank: today's price change ranked as percentile vs last 100 periods. CRSI = average of all three. Thresholds: 10/90.	RSI::Ch6_Variants
What is Stochastic RSI (StochRSI) and who created it?	Created by Tushar S. Chande and Stanley Kroll (1994). Applies the stochastic formula to RSI values instead of price: StochRSI = (RSI - Lowest RSI over m periods) / (Highest RSI - Lowest RSI). Result: much more sensitive than RSI, reaches extremes more often.	RSI::Ch6_Variants
Why was StochRSI developed?	In strong trends, standard RSI can spend weeks between 40-70 without touching OB/OS zones, making those zones useless. StochRSI normalizes RSI relative to its own recent range, generating signals even when RSI is in a narrow band.	RSI::Ch6_Variants
Who created the Laguerre RSI, and what makes it special?	John F. Ehlers (former aerospace engineer), published in 2004. Uses a 4-stage Laguerre filter cascade from digital signal processing. Key property: U-shaped distribution (spends more time at extremes near 0 and 1), and unusually low lag relative to its smoothness.	RSI::Ch6_Variants
What does the gamma parameter control in Laguerre RSI?	Gamma (0.2 to 0.8) is the damping factor. Lower gamma (0.2) = more reactive, faster, noisier. Higher gamma (0.8) = smoother, slower, less noise.	RSI::Ch6_Variants
What is RSI3M3 and who created it?	Walter Bressert's oscillator: a 3-period RSI smoothed by a 3-period SMA. RSI3M3 = SMA(3) of RSI(3). Despite simplicity, he reported 85% accuracy for identifying 10-day cycle bottoms on daily charts.	RSI::Ch6_Variants
Describe Bressert's RSI3M3 setup/trigger entry system.	RSI3M3 drops below 30 (setup), turns up (setup bar), then buy stop placed one tick above the setup bar's high (trigger). Protective stop below cycle low. Combined with timing bands where 70% of cycle tops/bottoms historically occur.	RSI::Ch6_Variants
What is Ehlers Adaptive RSI?	Uses Ehlers' Autocorrelation Periodogram to dynamically measure the dominant cycle length in price data, then feeds that as the RSI period. The RSI auto-adjusts to current market rhythm. Tradeoff: computational complexity and unstable estimates in choppy markets.	RSI::Ch6_Variants
What is RSX (Jurik RSI)?	Created by Mark Jurik. Replaces Wilder's SMMA with proprietary JMA (Jurik Moving Average) smoothing: multi-stage adaptive filter with near-zero lag. Results look smoother with less lag, but the algorithm is closed-source/proprietary.	RSI::Ch6_Variants
What is the problem with standard stochastic smoothing that DSS Bressert solves?	Standard stochastics use SMA smoothing, which gives equal weight to all bars. When the oldest bar drops off the window, it creates abrupt step-changes. If that old bar was a large move, the SMA jumps even if recent price was smooth, creating false signals.	RSI::Ch7_DSS
Who developed the double smoothing concept behind DSS?	William Blau published it in S&C magazine in January 1991, expanded in his 1995 book "Momentum, Direction, and Divergence." He was a mathematician and electrical engineer.	RSI::Ch7_DSS
How does DSS Bressert work (two-pass smoothing)?	First pass: EMA smoothing of the raw stochastic ratio = MIT (Moving Intermediate Term). Second pass: apply stochastic formula again to the smoothed MIT values, then EMA smooth that result. Double smoothed, double normalized.	RSI::Ch7_DSS
What are DSS Bressert's typical settings?	Period = 8 or 13, EMA smoothing period = 5. Thresholds: overbought above 80, oversold below 20. Tuned for short trading cycles of 5-13 bars.	RSI::Ch7_DSS
How does DSS compare to StochRSI and Standard Stochastic?	DSS: double EMA smoothing, low false signals, low lag, best for cycle timing. StochRSI: very high sensitivity, high false signals, low lag, best for fast/crypto markets. Standard Stoch: SMA smoothing, moderate sensitivity, moderate lag.	RSI::Ch7_DSS
What does ATR (Average True Range) measure?	Market volatility. True Range = max of (High-Low, |High-Prior Close|, |Low-Prior Close|). ATR = Wilder's SMMA of True Range over n periods (default 14). Doesn't tell direction, only how much a security is moving.	RSI::Ch8_Wilder
What is Parabolic SAR and how does it work?	A trailing stop system. SAR dot appears below price in uptrends, above in downtrends. Uses an Acceleration Factor (AF) starting at 0.02, increasing by 0.02 at each new extreme, capping at 0.20. When price hits SAR, the system reverses.	RSI::Ch8_Wilder
What does ADX measure, and what levels are significant?	ADX measures trend STRENGTH regardless of direction (0-100 scale). Above 25 = trending market. Below 20 = ranging/trendless market. It doesn't tell you if the trend is up or down.	RSI::Ch8_Wilder
How should you combine ADX with RSI?	ADX > 25 (trending): use RSI for trend confirmation (Cardwell ranges, failure swings). ADX < 20 (ranging): use RSI for mean reversion (classic 30/70 OB/OS). The same RSI reading means different things in different market regimes.	RSI::Ch8_Wilder
What is DMI and how do +DI and -DI work?	DMI consists of +DI (measures upward price movement) and -DI (measures downward). When +DI > -DI, trend is up. When -DI > +DI, trend is down. Crossovers generate directional signals. ADX is derived from the difference between them.	RSI::Ch8_Wilder
How is Williams %R mathematically related to Fast Stochastic %K?	They are arithmetically identical, just mirrored. %K = (Close - LL) / (HH - LL). %R = (HH - Close) / (HH - LL) x -100. When %K = 85, %R = -15. When %K = 10, %R = -90. Same information, different orientation.	RSI::Ch9_Cousins
Who is Larry Williams and what was his famous trading achievement?	1987 Robbins World Cup Championship: turned $10,000 into $1,137,600 (11,376% return in 12 months). His daughter Michelle Williams won the same competition in 1997.	RSI::Ch9_Cousins
What is the Money Flow Index (MFI) and how does it differ from RSI?	MFI is "volume-weighted RSI." Same RSI formula, but replaces price changes with dollar-weighted price changes (Typical Price x Volume). OB/OS levels: above 80 / below 20. MFI divergences are considered more reliable because they require both price AND volume confirmation.	RSI::Ch9_Cousins
How does the Chande Momentum Oscillator (CMO) differ from RSI?	CMO = ((Sum Up - Sum Down) / (Sum Up + Sum Down)) x 100. Ranges from -100 to +100 (vs RSI's 0-100), so the zero line is meaningful. Uses raw sums (no smoothing), making it more reactive but noisier. OB/OS: +50/-50.	RSI::Ch9_Cousins
What is the True Strength Index (TSI)?	Created by William Blau. Applies double EMA smoothing (EMA(25) then EMA(13)) to raw price momentum. TSI = double-smoothed momentum / double-smoothed |momentum| x 100. Range: -100 to +100. Zero line crossings are significant. OB/OS: +25/-25.	RSI::Ch9_Cousins
What is Constance Brown's CMB Composite Index?	Takes RSI(14) and adds a 9-period momentum of RSI to it. Result is unbounded (can go above 100 or below 0). Solves the RSI ceiling/floor problem: momentum can still accelerate when RSI is pinned at 85, and the Composite captures it.	RSI::Ch9_Cousins
How can Bollinger Bands be applied to RSI?	Instead of fixed 30/70, apply Bollinger Bands to the RSI line. RSI above upper band = overbought relative to its own recent behavior. Bands expand when RSI swings widely, compress when range-bound. Creates dynamic, adaptive OB/OS levels.	RSI::Ch9_Cousins
How does RSI behave at ultra-short periods (2-5)?	Becomes a mean-reversion detector. Swings violently, frequently reaching near 0 and 100. Useless for trend identification, powerful for spotting overextension. Use 5/95 or 10/90 thresholds instead of 30/70. Always filter with a longer-term trend indicator.	RSI::Ch10_Periods
What RSI periods work best for short-term swing and day trading?	RSI(7) to RSI(9). More responsive than 14, less chaotic than 2-5. Popular in forex markets. Adjust OB/OS levels to 20/80.	RSI::Ch10_Periods
What is the period tradeoff in RSI?	Shorter period = more signals, more noise, better for mean reversion. Longer period = fewer signals, less noise, better for trend confirmation. No "best" period exists, only the right period for your timeframe, asset, and strategy.	RSI::Ch10_Periods
Why is RSI(21-25) useful, and when should you use it?	Fewer signals, less noise, stays closer to 50. Best for weekly charts and position trading. When RSI(21) reaches an extreme, it's more significant than RSI(14) reaching the same level because it required a more sustained move.	RSI::Ch10_Periods
RSI period by chart timeframe: what to use for scalping vs daily vs weekly?	1m-5m scalping: RSI(7-9). 15m-1h day trading: RSI(9-14). 4h swing: RSI(14). Daily swing: RSI(14) for trend, RSI(2-5) for reversion. Weekly position: RSI(14-21). Monthly macro: RSI(14).	RSI::Ch10_Periods
How does RSI behavior differ in stocks vs crypto?	Stocks: RSI works best, especially mean-reversion. Gap dynamics create clean extremes. Use RSI(14) for trend, RSI(2-5) for reversion, standard 30/70 or Cardwell. Crypto: much higher volatility, 24/7, no gaps. Widen to 20/80 or 15/85. StochRSI popular. Bull range shifts to 40-90.	RSI::Ch11_Assets
What RSI settings are recommended for forex?	RSI(7-9) on 15m-4h charts, levels at 20/80. Always filter with higher-timeframe trend bias. RSI trendline breaks work well on daily forex charts. RSI has weaker track record in forex vs stocks.	RSI::Ch11_Assets
The Universal Rule for RSI across all asset classes?	Identify the trend regime FIRST, then choose your RSI technique. Trending (ADX > 25): use RSI for confirmation (Cardwell ranges, hidden divergence, failure swings). Ranging (ADX < 20): use RSI for mean reversion (classic 30/70, regular divergence).	RSI::Ch11_Assets
Describe the Three-Screen Multi-Timeframe RSI framework.	1) Weekly RSI(14): establishes trend direction (bull 40-80 or bear 20-60 range). 2) Daily RSI(14): identifies pullbacks within the trend. 3) 4h/intraday RSI or StochRSI: times the entry (failure swing, divergence). Never take lower-timeframe entries that contradict the higher timeframe.	RSI::Ch12_Trading
When do RSI and Stochastic agreement/disagreement matter?	Both oversold + stoch %K crossing above %D = strong buy. Both overbought + stoch %K crossing below %D = strong sell. If they conflict, the signal is weak. Proper response to conflicting signals: no trade.	RSI::Ch12_Trading
5 rules for higher-quality divergence trading?	1) Use Cardwell range shifts for trend context first. 2) Require at least one leg to touch 30 or 70. 3) Fewer bars = better (5-15 bars, not 30-50). 4) Wait for confirmation (failure swing, trendline break, candle pattern). 5) In clear trends, hidden divergence is often better than regular.	RSI::Ch12_Trading
Describe the RSI Trendline Breakout Strategy.	1) Daily chart, identify RSI trendline with 2-3 touches. 2) Wait for clean RSI trendline break. 3) Confirm with failure swing or Cardwell range shift. 4) Enter next candle after break, stop below recent swing low. 5) Use ATR for stop calibration (at least 1x ATR from entry).	RSI::Ch12_Trading
Key insight from Bressert's cycle timing framework?	Probability of successful trade is much greater LATER in the timing band than earlier. If timing band is days 15-25, a signal on day 22 is far more reliable than day 16. Be patient and let the cycle mature before acting on the oscillator signal.	RSI::Ch12_Trading
Which RSI variant for swing trading stocks?	RSI(14) with Cardwell ranges. Standard, well-tested, range shifts identify trend context.	RSI::Ch12_Trading
Which RSI variant for crypto day trading?	StochRSI or Laguerre RSI. Fast enough for volatile crypto, generates frequent signals.	RSI::Ch12_Trading
Which RSI variant for cycle-based timing?	RSI3M3 + DSS Bressert. Specifically designed for cycle top/bottom identification.	RSI::Ch12_Trading
Which RSI variant for reducing false signals?	Ehlers Smoothed RSI. SuperSmoother filter cuts noise while preserving the signal.	RSI::Ch12_Trading
Which RSI variant for short-term mean reversion in equities?	RSI(2) or Connors RSI. Hyper-fast, designed for 1-3 day bounce trades.	RSI::Ch12_Trading
Wilder's SMMA formula (general form)?	Avg = ((Prior Avg x (n-1)) + Current Value) / n. Equivalent to EMA with alpha = 1/n.	RSI::Ch13_CheatSheet
Stochastic %K formula?	%K = ((Close - Lowest Low) / (Highest High - Lowest Low)) x 100	RSI::Ch13_CheatSheet
StochRSI formula?	StochRSI = (RSI - Min RSI over m periods) / (Max RSI - Min RSI over m periods)	RSI::Ch13_CheatSheet
Connors RSI formula?	CRSI(3,2,100) = (RSI(3) + RSI(Streak, 2) + ROC Percentile Rank(100)) / 3	RSI::Ch13_CheatSheet
CMO formula?	CMO = ((Sum of Up Closes - Sum of Down Closes) / (Sum of Up Closes + Sum of Down Closes)) x 100	RSI::Ch13_CheatSheet
MFI formula?	MFI = 100 - (100 / (1 + Money Flow Ratio)). Money Flow Ratio = Sum(Positive Money Flow, 14) / Sum(Negative Money Flow, 14). Money Flow = Typical Price x Volume.	RSI::Ch13_CheatSheet
TSI formula?	TSI = (EMA(13) of EMA(25) of momentum) / (EMA(13) of EMA(25) of |momentum|) x 100. Momentum = Close - Prior Close.	RSI::Ch13_CheatSheet
ATR formula?	ATR = Wilder's SMMA of True Range. True Range = max of (High - Low, |High - Prior Close|, |Low - Prior Close|).	RSI::Ch13_CheatSheet
Signal quality ranking: which RSI signals are most reliable?	High: Failure swing, Cardwell range shift, RSI trendline break. Medium-High: Hidden divergence. Medium: Regular divergence, %K/%D crossover at extremes. Low: Overbought/oversold alone.	RSI::Ch13_CheatSheet
What is the DSS formula shorthand?	DSS = EMA(stoch(EMA(stoch(price)))) — a double smoothed stochastic using EMA at each stage.	RSI::Ch13_CheatSheet
Williams %R formula?	%R = ((Highest High - Close) / (Highest High - Lowest Low)) x -100. Overbought: 0 to -20. Oversold: -80 to -100. Mathematically identical to inverted Fast %K.	RSI::Ch13_CheatSheet
