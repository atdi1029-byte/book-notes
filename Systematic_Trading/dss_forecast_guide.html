DSS Forecast

Carver's Systematic Trading framework, rebuilt with DSS Bressert oscillators

This indicator takes Robert Carver's EWMAC forecast system from Systematic Trading and replaces the EMA crossover engine with DSS Bressert (Double Smoothed Stochastic) oscillators. The result is a multi-speed trend/cycle detector that combines into a single forecast number between -20 and +20.

The Two Parent Systems

EWMAC (what Carver built)

EWMAC stands for Exponentially Weighted Moving Average Crossover. Carver runs multiple fast/slow EMA pairs at a fixed 4:1 ratio (2/8, 4/16, 8/32, etc.). Each pair produces a raw crossover signal (fast EMA minus slow EMA), which gets divided by the 25-day standard deviation of price changes to normalize for volatility. A forecast scalar then rescales each signal so its average absolute value equals 10. The individual forecasts are combined with weights and a diversification multiplier, then hard-capped at +/-20.

The system is elegant but has a weakness: EMAs are pure trend followers. They're always lagging. In choppy or ranging markets, the fast/slow crossover whipsaws constantly, generating noise.

DSS Bressert (what we swapped in)

The Double Smoothed Stochastic, invented by Walter Bressert, is a stochastic oscillator that gets smoothed twice. This double application creates a natural sine-wave pattern that tracks cycles in price, oscillating between 0 and 100.

DSS Bressert: 4-Step Process
  1. First stochastic -- where is the current price relative to its high/low range over stochLen bars? Outputs 0-100.
  2. First EMA smooth -- smooth the raw stochastic with an EMA of length emaLen. Kills the noise.
  3. Second stochastic -- take a stochastic of the smoothed value over the same period. This is the "double" in double-smoothed.
  4. Second EMA smooth -- smooth again. The result is the DSS line: a clean sine wave between 0 and 100.

The signal line is simply an EMA of the DSS line. Crossovers between DSS and signal mark cycle turns.

The DSS Bressert pipeline: stochastic, smooth, stochastic again, smooth again

Why DSS Instead of EMA?

Property EWMAC (EMA crossover) DSS Forecast
Signal type Trend-following (lag by design) Cycle-detecting (finds turns)
Range behavior Whipsaws around zero Clean oscillation, fewer false crosses
Normalization Needs price volatility (std dev) Self-normalizing (0-100 by construction)
Forecast scalars Needs Appendix B lookup per pair Not needed -- linear rescale from 0-100
Trend detection Strong -- that's what EMAs do Good, but peaks/troughs at extremes
Mean reversion Weak -- EMAs chase price Strong -- oscillator returns to center

The key insight: EWMAC is purely a trend system. DSS Bressert is a cycle system. By running DSS at multiple speeds through Carver's combining framework, you get a multi-timeframe cycle consensus instead of a multi-timeframe trend consensus. This is better for instruments that oscillate (crypto, commodities, rangebound stocks) and potentially worse for pure trending markets (strong bull runs where you want to ride the trend).

When DSS > EWMAC

Ranging/choppy markets, crypto, mean-reverting instruments, detecting bottoms and tops, shorter-term trading.

When EWMAC > DSS

Strong trends, long-term position sizing, markets with persistent momentum (e.g. bond futures in a rate cycle).

The Six DSS Speeds

Like EWMAC's 6 EMA pairs, we run 6 DSS oscillators at increasing periods. The periods use Fibonacci-adjacent numbers for natural cycle spacing:

DSS 5/3
Fastest. Stoch period=5, EMA smooth=3. Catches short-term swings (2-5 bars). Noisy but responsive. Analogous to EWMAC 2/8.
DSS 8/5
Fast. Catches 5-10 bar swings. Good for intraday or short swing setups. Analogous to EWMAC 4/16.
DSS 13/8
Medium-fast. The "Fibonacci sweet spot." 10-20 bar cycles. Clean signals on 4h/daily. Analogous to EWMAC 8/32.
DSS 21/8
Medium. 15-30 bar swings. Often the strongest individual predictor. Analogous to EWMAC 16/64. Default weight: 21% (highest).
DSS 34/13
Slow. Catches multi-week moves. Smooth and reliable but late. Analogous to EWMAC 32/128.
DSS 55/13
Slowest. Monthly-scale cycles. Rarely flips, but when it does, it means something. Analogous to EWMAC 64/256. Default weight: 21%.

How the Forecast is Built

Step 1: Compute each DSS

Each of the 6 DSS pairs runs independently on close price. The DSS function outputs a value between 0 and 100.

Step 2: Rescale to forecast range

The DSS value (0-100) maps linearly to -20/+20: 

forecast = DSS * 0.4 - 20

So DSS = 0 becomes forecast -20 (max bearish), DSS = 50 becomes 0 (neutral), DSS = 100 becomes +20 (max bullish). No volatility normalization needed -- DSS is already bounded.

Step 3: Weighted combination

Each forecast is multiplied by its weight, summed, and divided by total weight: 

rawCombined = sum(forecast_i * weight_i)
            / sum(weight_i)

Step 4: Diversification multiplier

Carver's insight: because the individual forecasts aren't perfectly correlated, their combination underestimates true conviction. The diversification multiplier (default 1.31) corrects for this: 

scaledCombined = rawCombined * 1.31

Step 5: Cap at +/-20

No forecast ever exceeds +/-20. Carver shows this only clips about 5% of values and prevents over-leveraging during extreme moves.

The forecast scale: -20 (max short) through +20 (max long)

Reading the Indicator

The combined line (thick)

This is your primary signal. It changes color based on strength: deep green above +10, light green +5 to +10, pale green 0 to +5, pink 0 to -5, red -5 to -10, deep red below -10. Background shading appears when the forecast exceeds +/-10 to highlight strong conviction zones.

The info table

Top-right corner shows each active DSS pair's current forecast and the combined value. The Agreement row at the bottom shows how many pairs agree on direction -- e.g. "5/6 BULL" means 5 of 6 DSS speeds are positive. Full agreement (6/6) is rare and significant.

Individual forecasts (toggle on)

Enable "Show Individual Forecasts" to see all 6 DSS lines overlaid. This reveals which timeframes are leading: if the fast DSS pairs have flipped bullish but the slow ones are still bearish, you're seeing early rotation. If all 6 align, you have a strong multi-timeframe consensus.

How to Trade With It

Bullish Setup
  • Combined forecast crosses above +10 (strong buy zone)
  • Agreement shows 5/6 or 6/6 BULL
  • Forecast was recently below 0 (recovering from oversold -- the cycle is turning up)
  • Use as confirmation for your entry signal (RSI trendline break, support bounce, etc.)
Bearish Setup
  • Combined forecast crosses below -10 (strong sell zone)
  • Agreement shows 5/6 or 6/6 BEAR
  • Forecast was recently above 0 (rolling over from overbought)
  • Confirm with your short signal or use to avoid longs
Position Sizing (Carver method)

The forecast number is your position size. +20 = full long, +10 = half long, 0 = flat, -10 = half short, -20 = full short. Scale linearly. This is how Carver uses it in his systematic framework -- the forecast directly maps to how much exposure you want.

Default weights are based on Carver's original allocations adapted for DSS. The middle speeds (13/8 and 21/8) and the slowest (55/13) get the most weight because they tend to be the most reliable. Faster speeds are noisier and get less. Weights must sum to 100.

DSS vs EWMAC: Side by Side

Run both indicators simultaneously. You'll notice:

  • DSS turns earlier. Because it's a cycle detector, it'll start flipping before EWMAC catches the crossover. This gives earlier entries but more potential for premature signals in strong trends.
  • DSS is bounded, EWMAC isn't (before capping). EWMAC's raw signal can theoretically go to infinity before the cap clips it. DSS is always 0-100, so it naturally saturates at extremes. This makes DSS self-limiting -- it can't keep climbing during parabolic moves.
  • EWMAC trends, DSS oscillates. In a strong trending market, EWMAC will sit at +15 to +20 for weeks. DSS will cycle through peaks and troughs even during trends, briefly dipping negative before resuming. This is feature-or-bug depending on your style.
  • Agreement is more meaningful on DSS. When all 6 DSS speeds agree, it means cycles across all timeframes are aligned. That's rarer and more significant than all EMAs pointing the same way (which happens in any decent trend).

Combining with Other Indicators

This works well as a filter or confirmation layer, not a standalone entry system:

  • RSI Trendline Breakout -- use DSS Forecast > 0 as a go/no-go filter. Only take RSI breakout longs when the multi-speed cycle consensus is bullish.
  • Breathing Score / Rhythm Filter -- DSS Forecast tells you the instrument's cycle state. Breathing Score tells you the market's cycle state. Both bullish = high conviction.
  • EWMAC Forecast -- run both. When EWMAC and DSS agree, you have both trend AND cycle alignment. When they disagree, the market is probably choppy -- reduce size.
  • DSS Composite MTF -- the composite looks at one DSS across multiple chart timeframes. This indicator looks at multiple DSS speeds on one chart timeframe. They complement each other.
Key Principle

The forecast number is a conviction meter, not a binary signal. Don't think "buy when positive, sell when negative." Think "size proportionally to conviction." A +5 forecast means a small position. A +18 means go heavy. Zero means sit it out. This continuous scaling is the core of Carver's system and it applies equally to the DSS version.
