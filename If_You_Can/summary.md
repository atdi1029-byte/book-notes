# If You Can: How Millennials Can Get Rich Slowly
## William J. Bernstein (2014)

### One-Sentence Summary
A 26-page investment pamphlet that gives millennials a dead-simple three-fund index strategy for retirement — and then honestly warns them about the five psychological, educational, and institutional hurdles that will try to stop them from following it.

### Core Thesis
Getting rich slowly requires only one recipe: save 15% of your salary starting at age 25, split equally among three index funds (U.S. stocks, international stocks, U.S. bonds), and rebalance once a year. The strategy is simple enough for a seven-year-old, but executing it for 40 years requires overcoming five formidable hurdles: the inability to save, financial illiteracy, ignorance of market history, your own psychological biases, and the predatory financial services industry.

### Key Themes

**1. Simplicity vs. Discipline**
The investment strategy itself is trivially simple. The hard part is behavioral — actually saving 15%, not panicking in crashes, not chasing hot stocks, and not getting fleeced by brokers. Bernstein compares this to dieting: "eat less, exercise more" is simple advice, but most people can't follow it.

**2. The Death of Pensions**
The real retirement crisis isn't Social Security (which will survive in some form) — it's the disappearance of defined benefit pension plans. These acted as "investing fat farms" that enforced discipline. Without them, individuals must provide their own discipline, and most will fail.

**3. Risk and Return Are Inseparable**
If you want high returns, you must endure ferocious losses. If you want safety, you must accept low returns. There is no third option. Stocks lost 90% in the Depression and 60% in 2008 — and the actual experience is "unimaginably upsetting."

**4. The Economy-Returns Paradox**
Good economies produce low future returns (because stocks are expensive), and bad economies produce high future returns (because stocks are cheap). Buy when everyone is terrified; hold steady when everyone is euphoric.

**5. The Financial Industry as Adversary**
Most of the financial services industry exists to transfer your wealth to itself. Brokers lack fiduciary duty, fund companies serve their own shareholders, and fees are the silent killer. The only exception is Vanguard, which is owned by its fund shareholders.

### What Makes This Book Unique
- It's free and only 26 pages — deliberately accessible
- Brutally honest about what will go wrong
- Each hurdle comes with a specific book recommendation as "homework"
- Written by an insider (money manager) who has nothing to sell to the reader
- The dieting analogy runs throughout: simple ≠ easy
