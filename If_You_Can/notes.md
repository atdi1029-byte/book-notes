## William J. Bernstein (2014), 26 pages

## Introduction (Pages 3-7)

### The Three-Fund Strategy [MUST KNOW] N001
- Save 15% of salary starting at age 25 into 401(k), IRA, or taxable account
- Split equally into three index funds:
  1. U.S. total stock market index fund
  2. International total stock market index fund
  3. U.S. total bond market index fund
- Rebalance once per year to equal weights (15 minutes of work per year)
- Will outperform 90% of finance professionals in the long run
- Will likely accumulate enough to retire comfortably

### Social Security Is Not the Problem [SHOULD KNOW] N002
- Most young people wrongly believe Social Security won't be there for them
- Its imbalances are "relatively minor and fixable"
- Even if nothing is done, you'll still get ~75% of promised benefit
- The REAL problem: defined benefit pension plans have disappeared
- "There's only one person who can repair the gap... and you know who that is"

### The Big "If" — Five Hurdles [MUST KNOW] N003
- The strategy is "deceptively simple" — like saying "eat less, exercise more"
- "Dieting and investing are both simple, but neither is easy"
- Bernstein's analogy: the traditional pension plan was "an investing fat farm that involuntarily limited calorie intake and made participants run five miles per day"
- Five hurdles that must be overcome:
  1. People spend too much money
  2. Need adequate understanding of finance
  3. Need to learn financial and market history
  4. Must overcome your own psychology (the face in the mirror)
  5. Must recognize the monsters in the financial industry

### The Booklet as a Map [SHOULD KNOW] N004
- "This inexpensive, small booklet is not a taxi cab or an airliner; it's a map"
- Designed to be read twice: first pass in an hour, second pass with reading assignments over months
- Each section ends with a book recommendation as homework
- Full disclosure: Bernstein is an author (earns royalties) and co-principal in a money management firm for millionaires
- "I am writing this book for my children, my grandchildren, and for the millions of young people who don't have a prayer of retiring successfully unless they take control of their saving and investing"

## Hurdle 1: Even if you can invest like Warren Buffett, if you can't save, you'll die poor (Pages 8-10)

### The 15% Savings Rate [MUST KNOW] N005
- Starting at age 25, save at least 15% of salary to retire at 65
- On $50,000/year salary, that's $625/month — "a painfully thin margin"
- Can be wiped out by stringing together seemingly innocent expenditures:
  - A latte per day (~$100/month)
  - Too-rich cable package
  - Apartment that's a little too tony
  - Unnecessary brand-name purchases
  - A few restaurant meals
  - Excessive smartphone plan
- "Life without these may seem spartan, but it doesn't compare to being old and poor"
- Living with a roommate could save the entire $625 in one fell swoop
- "As bad as having a roomie may be, it's not nearly as awful as living on cat food at age 70"

### Debt Elimination Priority Order [MUST KNOW] N006
- ALWAYS max out employer match on 401(k) first — the "return" is 50-100%, higher than worst credit card rates
- Then eliminate credit card debt
- Then pay off car loan
- Then pay off educational loans (since expected investment return ~5% is likely lower than loan interest)
- "When, and only when, you've gotten rid of all your debt are you truly saving for retirement"

### Nominal vs. Real Returns [SHOULD KNOW] N007
- Expected nominal return ~5% (not adjusted for inflation)
- Long-term inflation running ~2%
- Real return therefore ~3%
- "What matters is the spending power of your portfolio, that is, its real value"
- All dollar figures in the booklet are in 2014 spending power terms
- Saving $625/month must increase with inflation over time (salary should too)

### Reading Assignment: The Millionaire Next Door [SHOULD KNOW] N008
- By Thomas Stanley and William Danko
- "The most important book you'll ever read"
- Emphasizes inverse correlation between spending and saving
- Methodological flaws acknowledged, but the message is lucid
- Key findings: most common millionaire car was F-150 truck (most pounds per dollar); plumber making $100K far more likely to be millionaire than attorney with same income (peer group pressure)
- Bernstein's father anecdote: modestly successful attorney, Depression-era compulsive saver. Neighbors "owned a lot, but didn't have a lot" — more than a few "owed a lot." When young Bernstein asked if they were rich: "Your mother and I are comfortably well to do. You don't have a dime."

## Hurdle 2: Finance isn't rocket science, but you'd better understand it clearly (Pages 10-15)

### Stocks vs. Bonds Explained [MUST KNOW] N009
- Starting a business: need money up front
- Two ways to get it: borrow (bond) or sell ownership (stock)
- Lenders get paid first (interest and principal) — legally and morally
- Stock owners are "residual owners" — only get paid after all expenses and debts
- Stocks are riskier than bonds → stocks deserve higher expected return
- "Expected return" = average result; risk = chance it falls short
- Coin toss analogy: $1 for heads, $0 for tails → expected return $0.50, but 50/50 chance of nothing

### Risk Aversion Explained [SHOULD KNOW] N010
- Most people would prefer a certain $0.40 over a 50/50 coin flip for $1
- Only at $0.20-0.30 certain payoff would average person prefer the flip
- Zero risk aversion = won't take less than $0.50 certain to avoid the flip
- Highly risk averse = would take $0.10 certain to avoid the flip

### Why Corporate Bonds Are Bad [MUST KNOW] N011
- Stock owners vote, bond owners don't
- Stock owners are willing to take risks for higher returns
- Bond owners care only about safety
- Corporate management serves stockholders, not bondholders
- Therefore corporate bonds are "in general a bad deal"
- Better to confine bond holdings to government offerings

### How Risky Are Stocks? [MUST KNOW] N012
- Great Depression: stocks lost ~90% of value
- 2008 financial crisis: lost almost 60%
- "It's one thing to think about temporarily losing 60 percent... but the actual experience, in the moment, is unimaginably upsetting"
- Fred Schwed quote: "There are certain things that cannot be adequately explained to a virgin either by words or pictures. Nor can any description I might offer here even approximate what it feels like to lose a real chunk of money that you used to own."

### Market Timing and Stock Picking Don't Work [MUST KNOW] N013
- "In the past 80 years, no one, and I mean no one, has ever [timed the market] reliably"
- "Like a broken clock that is right twice a day" — lucky guesses, not skill
- Past performance: "managers with superb prior performance usually fall flat on their faces going forward"
- Even Warren Buffett failed to beat market by significant margin over past decade (as of 2014)
- Stadium analogy: 10,000 people flipping coins, after 10 flips ~10 still standing — not skill, probability
- William Miller's Legg Mason Value Trust beat S&P 500 for 15 straight years, then gave back nearly all advantage in 3 years
- "If you could time the market... you'd borrow as much money as you could, bet on your predictions, and go to the beach"
- Three kinds of investors: "those who don't know where the market is headed, those who don't know that they don't know, and those who know they don't know but whose livelihoods depend on appearing to know"

### The Invisible Tennis Opponent [SHOULD KNOW] N014
- When you buy/sell stocks, the counterparty is Goldman Sachs, Fidelity, or a company insider
- "Trading stocks and bonds is like volleying with an invisible tennis opponent. More often than not, that person turns out to be one of the Williams sisters."

### The One-Sentence Summary of Finance [MUST KNOW] N015
- "If you want high returns, you're going to occasionally have to endure ferocious losses with equanimity, and if you want safety, you're going to have to endure low returns"
- Only two kinds of assets: risky (stocks — high return, high risk) and "riskless" (T-bills, CDs, money market — low return, low risk)
- Job one: figure out the appropriate mix

### Estimating Long-Term Returns [MUST KNOW] N016
- Government bonds: 30-year TIPS yield = 1.4% real return (as of 2014)
- Domestic stocks: 2% dividend yield + 1.5% real dividend growth = 3.5% real
- Foreign stocks: 3% dividend yield + 1.5% growth = 4.5% real
- Portfolio 2/3 stocks, 1/3 bonds → ~3% expected real return
- This is where the 15% savings rate comes from
- Goal: accumulate ~12 years of living expenses (combined with Social Security for ~25 years total)

### Jack Bogle and Vanguard [MUST KNOW] N017
- Reading assignment: Common Sense on Mutual Funds by Jack Bogle
- "Perhaps the best introduction to basic finance that's ever been written"
- Bogle founded Vanguard, gave ownership to fund shareholders — unique in industry
- Because shareholders own the company, no incentive to gouge with excessive fees
- "This is the only mutual fund company for which this is true"
- Every other fund company: you are NOT the owner, real owners want fees high
- Bogle would "almost certainly be a billionaire many times over" had he kept ownership
- "He is the only person in the history of the financial services industry to have done so"
- Bernstein: "If wanting to be the owner of my fund company and so pay rock-bottom fees makes me a shill, then I plead guilty"

## Hurdle 3: Those who ignore financial history are condemned to repeat it (Pages 15-18)

### The Economy-Returns Paradox [MUST KNOW] N018
- Natural but WRONG assumption: good economy → high stock returns
- "The exact opposite is in fact true"
- Good economy → stocks seem safe → people buy → prices high → low future returns
- Bad economy → stocks seem risky → must offer high returns to entice buyers → high future returns
- "The best fishing is done in the most stormy waters"
- Late 1990s: Internet would "change everything" → two bone-crushing bear markets → decade of negative real returns
- Great Depression/2008 crisis bottom buyers "made out like bandits"

### Sentiment as a Signal [MUST KNOW] N019
- Joseph Kennedy Sr.: sold stocks before 1929 crash because "shoeshine boys started offering him stock tips"
- Bernstein's own "shoeshine boy moments" in 1990s:
  1. TV ad for online brokerage featuring day trader who bought his own island
  2. A relative who knew nothing about investing joined her first stock club
- Early 2000s: "half your friends were flipping houses and brokering real estate"
- When everyone is invested, "there's no one left to join the party; the entry of naïve, inexperienced investors usually signals the end"
- Market bottoms: "when everyone is afraid of stocks, then there's no one left to sell"

### Finance Reverses Normal Recommendation Logic [SHOULD KNOW] N020
- For restaurants, movies, doctors — friend recommendations are useful
- Finance is "the exact opposite" — when all friends are enthusiastic about stocks, perhaps you shouldn't be
- When friends respond negatively to your investment strategy, "that's likely a good sign"

### Rebalancing as Passive Market Timing [MUST KNOW] N021
- Maintaining fixed allocation (33/33/33) is "an easy and effective form of market timing"
- By necessity: buying more stocks after falls (when pessimism reigns), selling after rises (when everyone seems rich)
- "The real purpose of learning financial history is to give you the courage" to rebalance

### Reading Assignments [NICE TO KNOW] N022
- Devil Take the Hindmost by Edward Chancellor — compendium of stock market manias
- The Great Depression: A Diary by Benjamin Roth — how things look at the bottom
- Together they provide "a beacon that will guide you through both the best of times and the worst of times"

## Hurdle 4: We have met the enemy and he is us (Pages 18-20)

### The Villa-White Cautionary Tale [MUST KNOW] N023
- Physician couple Lucie White and Mark Villa
- "Sucker punched" by 2008 crisis → sold everything, put money in bank
- 2013: as Dow and S&P hit record highs, hired advisor and "took the plunge back into the market"
- Classic buy-high sell-low behavior — sold at bottom, bought at top
- "This story speaks volumes about just how human nature can derail even the best designed portfolio"

### Evolutionary Mismatch [MUST KNOW] N024
- In nature, biggest risks = predators and other humans → quick, instinctive reactions have survival value
- As societies advanced, survival shifted to "long-term decision making — up to a time horizon of several decades"
- "Long-term planning... is a predisposition that our maker most definitely did not endow us with"
- "The nearly instantaneous emotional responses that served us so well on the prehistoric African plains turn out to be fatal in finance"

### Catalog of Behavioral Biases [MUST KNOW] N025
- Overconfidence: ~80% believe they're above-average drivers (logical impossibility); men worse → worse investors than women
- Recency bias/extrapolation: 1970s investors thought inflation would never end; current investors think it will never occur again — both wrong
- Pattern-seeking: humans are "pattern seeking primates who perceive relationships where in fact none exist"
- "95% of what happens in finance is random noise"

### The Coin Toss Classroom Demonstration [SHOULD KNOW] N026
- Professor leaves room; all but one student record 30 real coin tosses; one student simulates with pen
- Professor returns and identifies the simulator: their sequence almost never has 4+ straight heads or tails
- In reality, runs of 4+ are normal in random sequences
- Stock market participants make same mistake — "technical analysis is devoted to finding patterns in random financial data"

### Reading Assignment [NICE TO KNOW] N027
- Your Money and Your Brain by Jason Zweig
- "A real piece of chocolate cake" — enjoyable read
- "If Jason can't save you from yourself, then no one can"

## Hurdle 5: The financial services industry wants to make you poor and stupid (Pages 20-23)

### You Already Know More Than Your Broker [MUST KNOW] N028
- After completing the four reading assignments, "you'll know more about finance than the average stock broker or financial advisor"
- "Their main goal is to transfer your wealth to them"
- Applies to most mutual fund companies too — they exist for their owners' profits, not yours

### The Urban Combat Zone [MUST KNOW] N029
- "The prudent investor treats almost the entirety of the financial industry landscape as an urban-combat zone"
- Avoid at all costs: any stock broker, "full-service" brokerage firm, any newsletter, any advisor who buys individual securities, any hedge fund
- "Most mutual fund companies spew more toxic waste into the investment environment than a third-world refinery"
- "Most financial advisors can't invest their way out of a paper bag"

### Brokers vs. Real Professionals [MUST KNOW] N030
- Doctors, lawyers, accountants: post-graduate degrees, years of study, grueling exams
- "Your broker was not required to graduate high school"
- People don't enter financial services for the same reasons as social work, government service, or education
- Medicine, law, accounting: highly regulated, deviation from standard = peril to livelihood
- Physician who fails to treat bacterial pneumonia → license yanked
- No equivalent accountability for brokers

### Fiduciary Duty [MUST KNOW] N031
- Stock brokers do NOT owe "fiduciary duty" — obligation to put clients' interests first
- Without fiduciary duty, "you'll have little legal protection from a broker's incompetence and mendacity absent outright fraud"
- Investment advisors technically required to meet fiduciary standard, but brokers now call themselves "advisors" without the obligation

### Deflecting Financial Industry Friends/Family [SHOULD KNOW] N032
- Brokers likely to be "your old college roommate, brother-in-law, or church member"
- Solution: change the subject or claim no interest in money management
- "A ready routine for deflecting approaches from friends and relations in the finance industry is an essential survival skill"

### Mutual Fund Industry — Slightly Less Hostile [MUST KNOW] N033
- Greater transparency + 1940 Investment Company Act protections
- BUT fund company revenues come from AUM → incentive to grow fund size, not your returns
- "You are not the owner, and it is in the interest of the company's real owners... to keep fees high"
- Hidden fee inflation: raising fund fees from 1.0% to 1.5% = 50% revenue increase for company, but investor won't notice effect for years
- Transactional costs often exceed the stated expense ratio
- Exception: Vanguard (owned by fund shareholders)

### The Bottom Line [MUST KNOW] N034
- "You are engaged in a life-and-death struggle with the financial services industry"
- "Every dollar in fees and expenses you pay them comes directly out of your pocket"
- "Act as if every broker, insurance salesman, mutual fund salesperson, and financial advisor you encounter is a hardened criminal, and stick to low-cost index funds"

## Implementation: Nuts and Bolts (Pages 23-26)

### Savings Priority Order [MUST KNOW] N035
1. Get out of debt (only invest up to 401(k) match while in debt)
2. Build emergency fund: 6 months living expenses in T-bills/CDs/money market, in TAXABLE account (NOT in 401(k)/IRA — tax penalty to withdraw)
3. Then save seriously for retirement

### Optimal Savings Strategy for Young People [MUST KNOW] N036
1. Max out 401(k) employer match
2. Contribute maximum to Roth IRA (income limits: ~$200K married, ~$120K single, as of 2014)
3. Save in taxable account on top of that
- Goal: save at least 15% of salary
- "The best strategy is to save as much as you can, and don't stop doing so until the day you die"

### Traditional vs. Roth IRA [SHOULD KNOW] N037
- Traditional: tax deduction on contributions, pay taxes on withdrawal (generally after 59½)
- Roth: contribute with after-tax money, no taxes on withdrawals in retirement
- Roth generally better: $5,500 after-tax > $5,500 pre-tax in value; hopefully in higher tax bracket at retirement
- 401(k) can be rolled into traditional IRA after leaving employer

### 401(k) Plan Quality [SHOULD KNOW] N038
- Check fund expense ratios
- Above 1.0% = lousy plan → contribute only up to match
- Below 0.5% especially with Vanguard or Fidelity Spartan funds → consider maxing voluntary contributions
- "Fully maxing out voluntary 401(k) contributions (assuming good plan) and annual Roth limit will get well over 15% target"

### Specific Fund Recommendations [MUST KNOW] N039
- Vanguard: Total Stock Market Index, Total International Stock Index, Short-Term Bond Index or Total Bond Market Index
- Fidelity Spartan: Total Market Index, International Index, U.S. Bond Index or Short-Term Treasury Bond Index
- Target retirement funds: Vanguard Target Retirement funds (0.16-0.18% fees), e.g. 2060 fund has 90% stocks, declining ~1% per year

### Rebalancing and Tax Efficiency [SHOULD KNOW] N040
- Rebalance once per year to equal weights
- Avoid selling stocks in taxable accounts (capital gains taxes)
- Keep fair amount of stock in tax-sheltered 401(k) or IRA
- Not a problem for young investors with small taxable accounts

### Retirement Spending (Briefly) [NICE TO KNOW] N041
- Best approach: inflation-adjusted fixed annuity (mimics pension) or TIPS ladder
- Estate planning also deferred
- Both topics "will not become important for many decades" and investment options/tax law will be very different by then

### Final Reading Assignments [NICE TO KNOW] N042
- Allan Roth's How a Second Grader Beats Wall Street
- Rick Ferri's All About Asset Allocation
- Complete reading list in order:
  1. The Millionaire Next Door (Stanley & Danko) — saving
  2. Common Sense on Mutual Funds (Bogle) — finance basics
  3. Devil Take the Hindmost (Chancellor) — market manias
  4. The Great Depression: A Diary (Roth) — market bottoms
