## 1. Budgeting & Cash Flow

`savings-rate-target` — 5–10% of annual income minimum for long-term goals; Collins advocates 50%+ | Sources: PFD, SPW, MT
`emergency-fund` — 3–6 months of living expenses in liquid savings; up to 12 months for volatile income | Sources: PFD, BH, YMoYL, IWTYTR
`spending-tracking` — recording every expense; only necessary if not meeting savings goals | Sources: PFD, YMoYL, MT
`monthly-tabulation` — categorizing spending into personalized subcategories, converting totals to hours of life energy spent | Sources: YMoYL
`real-hourly-wage` — nominal wage minus all job-related costs divided by total hours devoted to work | Sources: YMoYL
`three-questions-framework` — (1) fulfillment proportional to life energy? (2) aligned with values? (3) how would this change if not working? | Sources: YMoYL
`net-worth-calculation` — total assets minus total liabilities; exclude personal residence unless planning to sell | Sources: PFD, BH
`cash-flow-management` — income minus expenditures; the fundamental equation of personal finance | Sources: PFD
`wall-chart` — large physical graph tracking income and expenses over months, revealing trends no single snapshot shows | Sources: YMoYL
`seasonal-spending-patterns` — holiday spikes, summer travel, annual insurance visible only on a multi-month chart | Sources: YMoYL
`extraordinary-expenses-are-ordinary` — every month is unusual; car repairs, medical bills, and holiday costs are a continuing part of life | Sources: YMoYL
`budget-vs-awareness-tool` — budgeting imposes external categories; YMoYL program builds awareness from the inside out | Sources: YMoYL
`overspending-causes` — easy credit access, credit card misuse, car loans framed as monthly payments, social pressure, retail therapy | Sources: PFD
`cash-flow-matching` — building a portfolio generating enough monthly income to cover expenses without ever selling shares | Sources: IA
`ten-ways-to-save` — don't shop, live within means, care for what you have, wear it out, DIY, anticipate needs, research durability, buy for less | Sources: YMoYL
`keep-exact-accounts` — Franklin's "Young Tradesman" advice: you will be shocked by how small trifling expenses accumulate | Sources: PRA
`small-expenses-compound` — a small leak will sink a great ship; track exact accounts to see how trivial purchases mount | Sources: PRA, BH
`purge-and-splurge-cycle` — month one reveals overspending → wallet fast → month two drops → month three rebounds | Sources: YMoYL
`financial-goals-priority` — Tyson's order: home → major purchases → retirement → education → small business | Sources: PFD
`personal-savings-goal-setting` — write down major financial goals with dates and dollar amounts; you can't reach a goal without a target | Sources: BH
`conscious-spending-plan` — not a budget: fixed costs 50-60%, investments 10%, savings 5-10%, guilt-free spending 20-35% | Sources: IWTYTR
`automatic-money-flow` — system of automated transfers from paycheck to 401(k), IRA, savings, and bills; runs without thinking | Sources: IWTYTR
`sub-savings-accounts` — separate savings buckets for specific goals: vacation, emergency, car, wedding, down payment | Sources: IWTYTR
`a-la-carte-method` — cancel all subscriptions, then buy only what you actually use on a per-occasion basis | Sources: IWTYTR
`annual-financial-checklist` — yearly review: renegotiate bills, check subscriptions, confirm max contributions, review debt payoff | Sources: IWTYTR
`big-wins-over-small-cuts` — focus on 5-10 high-impact decisions (salary, automation, accounts) rather than cutting lattes | Sources: IWTYTR
`eighty-five-percent-solution` — getting 85% right is infinitely better than 0%; don't wait for perfection to start | Sources: IWTYTR
`life-cycle-cost-vs-first-cost` — millionaires evaluate total cost over lifetime of use, not just purchase price | Sources: MM
`economically-productive-household` — systematic frugality: shopping lists, bulk buying, resoling shoes, reupholstering furniture | Sources: MM

## 2. Money Philosophy & Mindset

`money-as-life-energy` — reframe: money is the hours of your finite life you traded for it, not abstract paper | Sources: YMoYL
`fulfillment-curve` — spending climbs through survival/comforts/luxuries to a peak called "enough," then declines | Sources: YMoYL
`enough-concept` — defining a personal sufficiency point rather than treating more as always better | Sources: YMoYL
`gazingus-pin` — the item you can't pass without buying, revealing unconscious compulsive spending patterns | Sources: YMoYL
`money-taboo` — the cultural silence around personal finances that prevents growth and accountability | Sources: YMoYL
`happiness-income-plateau` — Kahneman research: beyond ~$75K/year, more income does not increase happiness | Sources: YMoYL, BH, MM
`conspicuous-consumption` — Veblen's 1899 concept: spending to signal status; social media has amplified it enormously | Sources: YMoYL, PRA, MT, MM
`lifestyle-inflation` — buying one fine thing forces ten more purchases to match it; status spending compounds | Sources: PRA, MT, MM, SPW
`artificial-wants-vs-natural-wants` — natural wants (food, shelter) are finite; artificial wants (fashion, status) are infinite | Sources: PRA
`joy-to-stuff-ratio` — frugality defined as maximum joy per unit of life energy, not suffering through scarcity | Sources: YMoYL, PRA
`frugality-as-joy` — true frugality from Latin roots: virtue, fruit, enjoyment — not deprivation | Sources: YMoYL, PRA
`consumerism-origins` — industrialists in the 1920s deliberately engineered "wants are insatiable" culture | Sources: YMoYL
`four-fis` — Financial Intelligence, Integrity, Independence, and Interdependence as philosophical scaffolding | Sources: YMoYL
`financial-integrity` — aligning every financial choice with your personal values and life purpose | Sources: YMoYL
`consciousness-vs-willpower` — lasting financial change comes from awareness and discernment, not deprivation | Sources: YMoYL
`no-shame-no-blame` — confronting spending truth is self-compassion; recrimination immobilizes, discrimination illuminates | Sources: YMoYL
`money-psychology` — doubling incomes hasn't made Americans happier; satisfaction is wanting what you have | Sources: PFD
`vice-as-hidden-tax` — idleness taxes you twice what government does; pride three times; folly four times | Sources: PRA
`time-is-money` — idle time has a real cost equal to wages not earned plus expenses incurred; coined by Franklin 1748 | Sources: PRA
`credit-is-money` — money left in your hands past its due date is effectively a free loan from your creditor | Sources: PRA
`money-is-prolific` — five shillings turned becomes six, becomes seven; compound growth theory from 1748 | Sources: PRA
`industry-and-frugality-formula` — "the way to wealth depends on two words: waste neither time nor money" | Sources: PRA
`motivated-reasoning` — Franklin: "so convenient it is to be a reasonable creature, since it enables one to find a reason for everything one has a mind to do" | Sources: PRA
`abcs-of-natural-wealth` — Abilities (skills), Belonging (relationships), Community (roots): three non-financial forms of wealth | Sources: YMoYL
`stop-trying-to-impress` — stopping status-driven spending saves thousands; impress people with how much you saved | Sources: YMoYL
`credit-card-pain-bypass` — MIT research: people pay up to twice as much with credit cards because plastic bypasses the loss signal | Sources: YMoYL
`living-below-your-means` — the foundational first principle; only 5% of Americans achieve financial independence by 65 | Sources: BH, MT, MM, SPW, IWTYTR
`three-financial-lifestyles` — Borrowers (live on credit), Consumers (paycheck mentality), Keepers/Savers (invest 10%+ off the top) | Sources: BH
`habit-of-saving` — acquiring the savings habit is the hardest part; reducing spending is more efficient than earning more | Sources: BH
`net-worth-mentality` — shift focus from income (how much you earn) to net worth (how much you keep) | Sources: BH, MM, MT
`invisible-money-scripts` — unconscious childhood beliefs about money that drive adult financial behavior; must be identified to overcome | Sources: IWTYTR
`f-you-money` — enough savings to walk away from a bad situation; working becomes optional; freedom is the product | Sources: SPW
`money-buys-freedom-not-stuff` — nothing money can buy is as valuable as autonomy; hedonic treadmill is the enemy | Sources: SPW, 4P
`balance-sheet-vs-income-statement-affluent` — BA has assets exceeding liabilities; IA earns big, spends big, owns nothing | Sources: MM
`hippocratic-rule-of-wealth` — above all, do no harm; build assets not debts; spend far less than you make | Sources: MT
`hedonic-treadmill` — new purchase demands matching purchases in an endless cycle of consumption upgrades | Sources: MT, 4P, SPW
`looking-rich-vs-being-rich` — most real millionaires drive Toyotas and live in modest homes; flashy spenders are often broke | Sources: MT, MM
`integrity-as-wealth-factor` — millionaires rank integrity and discipline as top success factors; IQ ranks 21st, grades rank last | Sources: MM
`discipline-over-luck` — only 12% of millionaires rated luck as very important; 67% cited discipline | Sources: MM
`mental-armor-plate` — resilience forged by deflecting negative evaluations from authority figures; C students develop it, A students often don't | Sources: MM
`courage-and-wealth` — taking positive moral actions that conjure up fear; millionaires systematically develop courage through preparation | Sources: MM
`self-employment-as-safety` — only 18% of households are self-employed but they dominate millionaire ranks; single income source is the real risk | Sources: MM
`opportunity-cost-of-spending` — every dollar spent takes with it all future compound returns; $20K car costs $36K+ over a decade | Sources: SPW
`monk-and-minister-parable` — financial independence runs in both directions: earn more or need less; the monk who needs little is freer than the minister who earns much | Sources: SPW
`complexity-benefits-industry` — complex investments exist to profit those who create and sell them; simplicity is the investor's friend | Sources: SPW, 4P
`perversity-of-risk` — risk is highest when it feels lowest and lowest when it feels highest; universal confidence is the danger signal | Sources: MIT

## 3. Debt Management

`good-debt-vs-bad-debt` — good debt: real estate, education, business (builds value); bad debt: consumer items (destroys value) | Sources: PFD, BH, SPW
`consumer-debt` — target: zero; paying off 12% credit card is equivalent to guaranteed tax-free 12% return | Sources: PFD, BH, MT, IWTYTR, SPW
`consumer-debt-elimination` — pay off credit cards before investing; highest guaranteed risk-free tax-free return available | Sources: BH
`high-interest-debt-math` — $8,000 at 18.9% with minimum payments = 8 years to pay off, $7,000+ in interest | Sources: BH
`debt-to-income-ratio` — consumer debt >25% of annual income is danger zone; consider bankruptcy | Sources: PFD
`credit-card-traps` — teaser rates jump to 15–29%+; no grace period when carrying a balance; minimum payments = decades of debt | Sources: PFD
`balance-transfer` — moving high-rate credit card debt to lower-rate card; saves interest during payoff | Sources: PFD
`mortgage-debt-vs-retirement-savings` — better to fund retirement accounts than pay down mortgage faster | Sources: PFD
`home-equity-as-debt-payoff` — converting consumer debt to mortgage debt for lower rate and potential tax deduction | Sources: PFD
`chapter-7-bankruptcy` — discharges eligible debts entirely; stays on credit report up to 10 years | Sources: PFD
`chapter-13-bankruptcy` — court-supervised repayment schedule instead of full discharge | Sources: PFD
`credit-counseling-warning` — most agencies funded by creditors; function essentially as collection agencies; avoid DMPs | Sources: PFD
`rent-to-own-trap` — effective interest rate exceeds 100–200%; worst way to purchase consumer goods | Sources: PFD
`debt-as-voluntary-slavery` — borrowing for luxuries gives the creditor legal power over your body | Sources: PRA
`debt-psychological-cascade` — debt → shame → avoidance → excuses → dishonesty; "lying rides upon debt's back" | Sources: PRA
`debt-as-negative-compounding` — credit card interest at 12–24% compounds against you just as relentlessly as index fund returns compound for you | Sources: YMoYL
`deferred-purchase-trap` — buying on credit at vendues: you pay the price of goods, plus cost of credit, plus pressure at maturity | Sources: PRA
`student-debt-trap` — advanced degree, $100K+ debt, net earnings couldn't pay down principal faster than interest accumulated | Sources: YMoYL, SPW
`debt-snowball-vs-avalanche` — snowball pays smallest balance first (psychological wins); avalanche pays highest rate first (math wins) | Sources: IWTYTR, SPW
`debt-interest-rate-framework` — below 3% pay slowly and invest; 3-5% either approach works; above 5% eliminate aggressively | Sources: SPW
`student-loan-hybrid-approach` — 50/50 split between extra loan payments and investing; captures compound growth while reducing debt | Sources: IWTYTR
`creditor-memory` — creditors are "great observers of set days and times"; the due date arrives with mathematical certainty | Sources: PRA

## 4. Credit & Identity Protection

`fico-credit-score` — 300–850 range; median ~720; best mortgage rates require mid-700s+; 760 vs 620 = $76K difference on mortgage | Sources: PFD, IWTYTR
`credit-report-rotation` — pull one bureau every 4 months for year-round free monitoring | Sources: PFD
`credit-freeze` — free by law; most effective identity theft protection; prevents new accounts from being opened | Sources: PFD
`fraud-alert` — 1-year initial or 7-year extended alert on credit files; lighter protection than freeze | Sources: PFD
`fdic-insurance` — $250,000 single, $500,000 joint account coverage | Sources: PFD
`phishing-awareness` — never respond to inbound calls requesting personal info; numbers can be spoofed | Sources: PFD
`credit-score-components` — 35% payment history, 30% amounts owed, 15% length of history, 10% new credit, 10% credit mix | Sources: IWTYTR
`credit-card-as-tool` — pay in full monthly, negotiate fees/APRs, use rewards; never close oldest card | Sources: IWTYTR
`negotiate-all-fees` — call providers to negotiate: lower APR, waive annual fees, waive late fees; banks often say yes to retain customers | Sources: IWTYTR

## 5. Compound Interest & Time Value

`compound-interest-power` — Einstein attributed: "the eighth wonder of the world"; earning returns on returns makes time the most valuable asset | Sources: BH, MT, IWTYTR, SPW
`rule-of-72` — divide 72 by annual return to find years to double; 8% return doubles every 9 years | Sources: BH, IWTYTR
`starting-early-advantage` — Eric Early vs. Larry Lately: Eric invested $40K (age 25–35), Larry $120K (age 35–65), same rate — Eric ended with 29% more | Sources: BH, MT, IWTYTR, SPW
`compound-interest-time-value` — $1 saved at 26 grows 4.8x by 66; at 40 only 2.8x; at 50 only 1.9x | Sources: PFD
`dollar-cost-averaging` — investing fixed amounts at regular intervals automatically buys more shares when prices are low | Sources: BH, MT, SPW, IWTYTR
`pay-yourself-first` — automatically deduct savings from every paycheck before you see it; minimum 10% | Sources: BH, MT, IWTYTR, SPW
`lump-sum-vs-dca` — Vanguard research: lump-sum investing beats DCA 2/3 of the time; if you have money, invest it now | Sources: IWTYTR, SPW
`compounding-daily-increments` — little strokes fell great oaks; constant dropping wears away stones | Sources: PRA

## 6. Index Funds & Passive Investing

`index-funds` — outperform ~75–80% of active funds over 10+ years primarily due to lower fees | Sources: PFD, PP, BH, MT, SPW, IWTYTR, 4P, IAA, SFLR
`bogleheads-philosophy` — pay off debt, live below means, invest in low-cost index funds, hold for many years | Sources: BH, YMoYL
`three-fund-portfolio` — total stock market + total international + total bond market; all most investors need | Sources: BH, PP
`bogles-folly` — Bogle's first retail index fund was initially mocked; now the largest mutual fund in the world | Sources: BH
`expense-ratio-as-predictor` — Financial Research Corporation found expense ratio is the only reliable predictor of future fund performance | Sources: BH, MT, SPW
`passive-vs-active-management` — active management costs 3.3% annually in total friction; average fund shareholder captures only 7.2% of a 10.5% market | Sources: BH, MT, SPW, 4P, IAA, SFLR
`survivorship-bias` — fund companies advertise recent winners while quietly closing recent losers | Sources: BH, MT, SPW, 4P, IAA
`efficient-market-theory` — Fama 1960s: stock prices are very efficient; index beats most pickers over time | Sources: BH, 4P, IAA, SFLR, MIT
`style-drift` — actively managed funds sometimes shift from stated mandate; index funds always match benchmark | Sources: BH
`dalbar-study` — 1993–2012: S&P 500 averaged 8.21%/yr; average equity fund investor earned 4.25% due to emotional timing | Sources: BH, MT, SPW
`total-stock-market-index-fund` — VTSAX or equivalent; owns essentially the entire US equity market | Sources: BH, YMoYL, SPW, MT
`expense-ratio-impact` — fund averaging 14% pre-tax can net less than 12% fund after fees and taxes; costs compound | Sources: PFD
`vanguard-ownership-structure` — unique mutual ownership structure: fund company owned by its fund shareholders; no external owners demanding profit | Sources: BH, SPW, 4P
`self-cleansing-index` — failing companies drop out automatically, replaced by stronger firms; capped downside but uncapped upside creates structural growth bias | Sources: SPW
`market-always-goes-up` — through every crash in history the long-term trend is relentlessly upward; crashes are normal as winter blizzards | Sources: SPW, SFLR
`vtsax-as-core-holding` — Collins' one-fund solution: Vanguard Total Stock Market Index Fund for wealth accumulation | Sources: SPW
`couch-potato-portfolio` — Scott Burns: 50/50 stock index/bond index, rebalance once a year; beat most active funds | Sources: MT, 4P

## 7. Investing Basics & Market Fundamentals

`stocks-as-business-ownership` — stocks are ownership shares in businesses, not lottery tickets or price charts | Sources: BH, HTROD
`stock-market-historical-returns` — US large cap ~10% annually since 1926 vs. bonds ~6%, savings ~4%, inflation ~3% | Sources: PFD, BH, MT, SPW, SFLR, IAA, 4P
`bear-market-history` — Dow fell >20% during 16 different periods in the 20th century; average decline lasted <2 years | Sources: PFD
`price-to-earnings-ratio-pe` — valuation metric; S&P 500 historical average ~15x; used to assess market expensiveness | Sources: PFD, AA, SFLR
`financial-media-skepticism` — media panic at 2009 lows caused people to sell and miss recovery | Sources: PFD, AA
`celebrity-financial-endorsers` — FTX spent hundreds of millions on celebrity endorsers; they have no special financial knowledge | Sources: PFD
`cryptocurrency-critique` — 23,000+ cryptocurrencies; no recourse, no inherent value, not truly scarce | Sources: PFD, AA, 4P
`investment-noise` — financial media, market forecasters, and newsletter writers exist to attract audiences, not make you money | Sources: BH, MT, SPW, 4P, IWTYTR
`stocks-as-ownership` — stocks represent fractional ownership in living, dynamic companies competing to grow; not lottery tickets | Sources: MT, SPW
`220-year-equity-record` — $1 in stocks in 1802 grew to $54.2M by 2021; 6.9% real return remarkably stable across all eras | Sources: SFLR
`equity-risk-premium` — the gap between stock and bond returns; averaged 3.3-4.3% over 220 years; the "equity premium puzzle" | Sources: SFLR, 4P, IAA
`gordon-equation` — expected stock return = dividend yield + dividend growth rate; remarkably accurate over 30+ year periods | Sources: 4P, SFLR
`risk-premium-concept` — compensation investors demand for bearing uncertainty; the most important term in investing | Sources: 4P, IAA, MIT
`intrinsic-value` — what an asset is actually worth based on fundamentals; the anchor for all intelligent investing | Sources: MIT
`price-vs-value` — investment success comes from buying things well (below value), not from buying good things at any price | Sources: MIT
`nifty-fifty-collapse` — buying America's best companies at P/E 80-90 led to 90% losses; quality at any price is still dangerous | Sources: MIT, 4P
`positive-skewness-of-stocks` — majority of individual stocks underperform T-bills; just 4% of companies provide entire equity risk premium | Sources: 4P, SFLR

## 8. Behavioral Finance & Investor Psychology

`loss-aversion` — we feel the pain of a $100 loss twice as intensely as the benefit of a $100 gain | Sources: PP, BH, MIT, SFLR
`performance-chasing` — buying funds that recently outperformed; reliably underperforms buy-and-hold | Sources: PP, BH, MT, 4P, MIT
`panic-selling` — selling during crashes and locking in losses; the single most wealth-destroying investor behavior | Sources: PP, PFD, MT, SPW, 4P, MIT
`recency-bias` — overweighting recent performance when making investment decisions; Business Week declared "Death of Equities" in 1979 | Sources: PP, BH
`overconfidence-bias` — Mensa Investment Club averaged 2.5% vs. S&P's 15.3%; intelligence creates dangerous overconfidence | Sources: BH
`herd-mentality` — following the crowd in investing means following it to the slaughterhouse | Sources: BH, MIT, SFLR
`greed-and-fear` — two primary investor emotions; greed drives performance-chasing (buying high); fear drives panic selling (selling low) | Sources: BH
`market-timing-failure` — Graham & Harvey tracked 15,000+ timing predictions by 237 newsletter writers; no evidence newsletters can time the market | Sources: BH, MT, SPW, 4P, SFLR
`buy-and-hold-superiority` — most active trader: 258% turnover → 11.4% returns; buy and hold: 2% turnover → 18.5% returns | Sources: BH, MT, SPW, SFLR
`past-performance-not-predictive` — 44 Wall Street Fund was top performer of 1970s, worst performer of 1980s | Sources: BH, MT, 4P
`mental-accounting` — treating a tax refund as "found money" when it's actually an interest-free loan to the government | Sources: BH
`paralysis-by-analysis` — with thousands of funds, some people never invest; every day without investing is a lost day of compounding | Sources: BH
`women-better-investors` — women trade less, take fewer risks, don't let ego drive decisions; earn better risk-adjusted returns than men | Sources: BH
`casino-account` — if you need excitement, keep max 5% as a speculative account; the rest stays in index funds | Sources: BH
`pre-commitment` — making investment rules in advance to prevent emotional decisions during crises | Sources: PP
`tracking-error-regret` — psychological pain of underperforming a visible benchmark even when long-term strategy is sound | Sources: PP
`recency-bias-portfolio` — overweighting recent asset class returns; leads to buying high after a run-up | Sources: PP
`endowment-effect` — overvaluing what you already own; do not hold more than 10% in your employer's stock | Sources: BH
`anchoring` — holding a losing stock until it "returns to your price" regardless of whether the thesis still holds | Sources: BH
`reversion-to-mean` — asset classes that outperform for a period are likely to underperform next | Sources: BH, SFLR, 4P, MIT
`second-level-thinking` — Howard Marks: not just what is true, but what everyone believes to be true; being right is necessary but not sufficient | Sources: HTROD, MIT
`dog-on-leash-metaphor` — stock price is the dog, business earnings are the owner; short-term the dog darts wildly, long-term it follows the owner | Sources: MT
`contrarianism-in-investing` — buying when others despondently sell and selling when others euphorically buy; requires analytical rigor, not mere opposition | Sources: MIT
`pendulum-of-investor-psychology` — swings between euphoria and depression, greed and fear; spends almost no time at the midpoint | Sources: MIT
`three-stages-bull-bear` — bull: few believe → most realize → everyone certain forever; bear mirrors in reverse; greatest opportunities at stage 3 of bear | Sources: MIT
`credit-cycle` — small economic fluctuations create large credit swings; worst loans made at best of times; inevitable and exploitable | Sources: MIT
`patient-opportunism` — waiting for bargains rather than chasing them; Ted Williams' strike zone analogy: no called strikes in investing | Sources: MIT
`finding-bargains` — look where others refuse: controversial, unseemly, trailing poor returns, recently abandoned; perception must be worse than reality | Sources: MIT
`risk-as-permanent-loss` — Marks: risk is not volatility but probability of permanent capital loss or falling short of goals | Sources: MIT
`risk-control-vs-risk-avoidance` — avoidance means refusing risk (low returns); control means bearing risk intelligently like an insurance company | Sources: MIT
`envy-as-destructive-force` — watching others earn returns you're missing triggers irresistible urge to abandon discipline | Sources: MIT, SFLR
`this-time-its-different` — the four most dangerous words in investing; someone declares cyclicality dead before every crisis | Sources: MIT, 4P, SFLR
`foam-vs-beer` — stock market served in dark mug: beer is business value, foam is price fluctuation; focus on the beer | Sources: SPW
`missing-best-days-risk` — missing just 10 best trading days (1982-2005) drops return from 10.6% to 8.1%; missing 50 drops to 1.8% | Sources: MT, SFLR

## 9. Asset Classes

`stocks-equities` — ownership stakes in companies; highest long-term return (~10% historical) but significant volatility | Sources: all
`bonds-fixed-income` — loans to governments or corporations paying fixed coupons; primary portfolio shock absorber | Sources: all
`us-large-cap-stocks` — S&P 500 companies; ~10.1% historical annual return, 19.7% standard deviation | Sources: PP
`us-mid-cap-stocks` — S&P 400 companies; historically best Sharpe ratio of all three size segments | Sources: PP, AA
`us-small-cap-stocks` — Russell 2000; 11.8% historical return but 31% standard deviation | Sources: PP, AA
`small-cap-value-stocks` — the historical sweet spot; 13.6% return in Fama-French data; best-performing equity segment long-term | Sources: PP
`small-cap-growth-stocks` — worst risk-adjusted equity segment; high volatility with low returns | Sources: PP
`international-developed-stocks` — MSCI EAFE markets; high correlation with US (0.80–0.90) since globalization | Sources: PP
`emerging-market-stocks` — lower correlation (~0.78) than developed international; -65% max drawdown historically | Sources: PP
`gold` — near-zero correlation with stocks (0.004); single best portfolio diversifier; crisis hedge; $1 in 1801 worth only $73 by 2011 vs stocks' $10.15M | Sources: PP, AA, MT, 4P, SFLR
`commodities-broad` — energy, agriculture, metals; near-zero stock correlation; inflation fighter | Sources: PP, AA
`real-estate-investment-trusts-reits` — required to distribute 90%+ of taxable income; high yields but crash with stocks | Sources: all
`certificates-of-deposit-cds` — FDIC-insured time deposits; typically yield 1–2% less than comparable bonds | Sources: PFD
`money-market-funds` — generally pay higher rates than bank savings; not FDIC-insured; good for cash management | Sources: PFD
`savings-accounts` — FDIC-insured; long-term "safe" investments are actually risky due to inflation erosion | Sources: PFD
`annuities` — only consider after fully funding all tax-deductible retirement accounts; often oversold | Sources: PFD, BH
`limited-partnerships` — avoid; 10%+ commission; not suitable for retirement accounts | Sources: PFD

## 10. Bonds & Fixed Income

`us-treasury-bonds` — safest bonds; government-backed, state-tax-exempt interest, zero credit risk | Sources: all
`short-term-treasuries` — 1–3 year maturities; near-cash substitute; minimal duration risk | Sources: AA, PP
`intermediate-treasuries` — 7–10 year maturities; classic 60/40 bond allocation; ~7% price move per 1% rate change | Sources: AA, PP
`long-term-treasuries` — 20+ year maturities; extreme rate sensitivity; can fall 50%+ in rising rate environments | Sources: AA, PP
`t-bills` — 1–3 month government bills; essentially risk-free rate; best cash management tool | Sources: AA, PP
`tips-treasury-inflation-protected-securities` — bonds whose principal adjusts with CPI; protect against inflation surprises | Sources: AA, PP, IA
`investment-grade-corporate-bonds` — BBB and above; yield 80–200 bps above Treasuries; moderate credit risk | Sources: AA, IA
`high-yield-junk-bonds` — below BBB; high correlation with stocks (0.50–0.70); not a true portfolio diversifier | Sources: PP, IA

`size-factor` — small-cap premium; exists almost entirely in small-cap value, not small-cap broadly | Sources: PP
`low-volatility-factor` — low-vol anomaly: historically similar or better returns than high-vol stocks with much less risk | Sources: PP, AA
`growth-vs-value-rotation` — cyclical shift in market leadership between growth stocks and value stocks; regime-dependent | Sources: PP, AA
`growth-stocks` — high price-to-book, high forecast growth; sensitive to interest rates; long-duration assets | Sources: PP, AA
