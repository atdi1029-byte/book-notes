### Protecting a Portfolio — Delta Approach [MUST KNOW]

- Call delta range: 0 to +100; Put delta range: 0 to –100
- 1 share of stock = +1 delta; 100 shares = +100 delta
- Perfect hedge: position delta approaches 0

**XYZ perfect hedge example (stock at $37.50, 100 shares):**

> "Options are a *variable delta* security — delta changes by gamma for each $1 in the underlying. Stock is a *fixed delta* security: always +1."

### Adjusted Options [MUST KNOW]

- **Adjusted options**: options that existed when corporate actions took place; contract terms required to adjust
- Triggering actions: stock splits, large cash dividends, mergers/acquisitions, spinoffs
- Regular dividends do NOT adjust; **special dividends** cause strike price adjustment

### Corporate Action 1 — Stock Splits [MUST KNOW]

- **2:1 split**: # of contracts doubled, strike price halved, new symbol created
- **3:2 split**: multiplier changes to 150, strike price to two-thirds of original

> "WARNING: Exercising a put without holding the underlying creates a short position."

### Corporate Action 2 — Mergers, Spinoffs, Dividends [MUST KNOW]

- Merger: 100 shares original + shares of acquired stock
- Spinoff: 100 shares original + shares of new stock
- Large cash dividend: 100 shares original + cash amount
- Acquisition (company acquired): no original stock + shares of acquiring company

> "TIP: If you found a deal that seems too good to be true, you may have stumbled upon an adjusted option. No free money on Wall Street."

- **Critical rule:** Never create a new position using an adjusted option you don't fully understand

### Valuing 3:2 Split-Adjusted Options [SHOULD KNOW]

Four-step process:
1. Adjusted strike × 150 = exercise value (e.g., 60 × 150 = $9,000)
2. Deliverable value: 150 shares × market price (e.g., 150 × $62 = $9,300)
3. Intrinsic value: $9,300 – $9,000 = **$300**
4. Extrinsic value: (option price × 150) – intrinsic value = e.g., ($3 × 150 = $450) – $300 = **$150**

### Detecting Adjusted Options [SHOULD KNOW]

- CBOE reports adjusted options: www.cboe.com/tradtool/contracts.aspx
- Warning signs: changed symbol, changed shares per contract, changed style (American vs. European), changed strike prices
- When something seems off in a quote or volume, dig deeper

## Chapter 11: Increasing Profit Potential and Decreasing Risk

### Leveraging Assets to Reduce Risk [MUST KNOW]

- Options leverage assets while **decreasing** the amount at risk
- Premium paid locks in a price without putting up 100% of stock value
- Max loss on a long option = premium paid (vs. stock which can fall near zero)
- Caveat: time decay works against you — can't wait indefinitely for the move

### Three Factors That Change Option Value [MUST KNOW]

1. **Stock price movement** (up/down with underlying)
2. **Implied volatility (IV)** — higher IV = higher option value
3. **Time passage** — always decreases option value (theta)

> "Establish long option positions when IV is relatively low to increase probability for profits."

### Market Timing for Options — Three Requirements [MUST KNOW]

1. Correctly predict the **direction** of the move
2. Correctly predict the **magnitude** of the move
3. Correctly predict the **maximum time** for the move to occur

With stocks you can hold sideways positions (dead money). With options, time decay makes sideways movement actively costly.

**To improve probability:**
- Trade with the trend (technical tools); or trade against weakening momentum (contrarian)
- Trade undervalued stocks gaining positive attention (fundamental tools)
- Limit losses in every trade with unbiased exit strategies
- Minimize magnitude shortfalls: use price projections; take partial profits; focus higher delta (higher probability) trades

### Vertical Spreads — Debit [MUST KNOW]

A **vertical spread** = long + short option, same type, same expiration, different strike prices. **"Vertical"** = how strikes line up in option chain. Each position = a **leg**.

**Bull call spread** (bullish debit):
- Buy lower strike call + sell higher strike call, same month
- Net debit = max risk; max reward = (strike spread × 100) – debit

**Bear put spread** (bearish debit):
- Buy higher strike put + sell lower strike put, same month
- Net debit = max risk; max reward = (strike spread × 100) – debit

**Example — Bull Call Spread (ABC at $37.65):**
- Buy 1 Jan 35 Call @ $4.20; Sell 1 Jan 40 Call @ $1.50
- Debit = Max Risk = ($4.20 – $1.50) × 100 = **$270**
- Max Reward = [(40 – 35) × 100] – $270 = **$230**
- Breakeven = $35 + $2.70 = **$37.70**

[PAGE IMAGE: page_270.png] — risk graph shows diagonal profit line from $35 to $40 with flat floors at –$270 and +$230

**Example — Bear Put Spread (XYZ at $50.85):**
- Buy 1 Jan 50 Put @ $2.75; Sell 1 Jan 45 Put @ $1.30
- Debit = Max Risk = $145; Max Reward = $355; Breakeven = **$48.55**

[PAGE IMAGE: page_272.png]

**Debit Spread Summary Table:**

|  | Bull Call | Bear Put |
|---|---|---|
| Risk | Initial debit | Initial debit |
| Reward | [(Higher – lower) × 100] – debit | [(Higher – lower) × 100] – debit |
| Breakeven | Long strike + (long price – short price) | Long strike – (long price – short price) |

**Key rules:**
- Enter debit spreads with at least **60 days to expiration**
- Exit **both legs together** — never exit only the long leg
- Time decay accelerates within 30 days — plan exit accordingly

### Vertical Spreads — Credit [MUST KNOW]

**Bear call spread** (bearish credit):
- Buy higher strike call + sell lower strike call, same month; receive credit
- Max reward = credit; max risk = (strike spread × 100) – credit

**Bull put spread** (bullish credit):
- Buy lower strike put + sell higher strike put, same month; receive credit
- Max reward = credit; max risk = (strike spread × 100) – credit

**Example — Bear Call Spread (XYZ at $50.85):**
- Buy 1 Jan 55 Call @ $0.95; Sell 1 Jan 50 Call @ $3.20
- Credit = Max Reward = **$225**; Max Risk = **$275**; Breakeven = **$52.25**

[PAGE IMAGE: page_276.png]

**Example — Bull Put Spread (ABC at $37.65):**
- Buy 1 Jan 35 Put @ $1.70; Sell 1 Jan 40 Put @ $4.10
- Credit = Max Reward = **$240**; Max Risk = **$260**; Breakeven = **$37.60**

[PAGE IMAGE: page_278.png]

**Credit Spread Summary Table:**

|  | Bear Call | Bull Put |
|---|---|---|
| Risk | [(Higher – lower) × 100] – credit | [(Higher – lower) × 100] – credit |
| Reward | Initial credit | Initial credit |
| Breakeven | Short strike + (short price – long price) | Short strike – (short price – put price) |

> "WARNING: Close a vertical credit spread for a debit on the last trading day before expiration if the underlying is near the short strike price."

**Universal spread rules:**
- Debit: bull call (bullish) / bear put (bearish)
- Credit: bear call (bearish) / bull put (bullish)
- Always exit both legs together
- Use limit orders; spread orders appeal to floor traders — often filled below quoted price

## Chapter 12: Combination Strategies — Spreads and More

### Combining Options with Stocks [MUST KNOW]

- **Covered call**: own stock + sell call → income + moderate risk reduction; caps upside
- **Covered put**: short stock + sell put → income + modest risk reduction; caps downside gains
- Neither offers strong downside protection for stock → need a **collar** for that

### Covered Call Strategy [MUST KNOW]

- Best established with **30–45 days or less** until expiration (time decay works in your favor)
- Best scenario: stock moves sideways; call expires worthless → sell another
- Strike price should be above stock purchase price

**Formulas:**
- Max Risk = (Stock Purchase Price × # shares) – Call Premium
- Max Reward = [(Strike – Stock Price) × 100] + Initial Credit
- Breakeven = Stock Purchase Price – Call Price

**Example (ABC at $37.72, 40 strike call @ $0.50):**
- Max Risk = $3,722; Max Reward = $278; Breakeven = $37.22

[PAGE IMAGE: page_287.png] — risk graph shows flat profit plateau above $40, steep loss below $37.22

> "WARNING: Never allow a short call to become uncovered by selling the underlying stock. Buy the call back first."

### Collar Strategy [MUST KNOW]

- **Collar** = long stock + long put (protection) + short call (offsets put cost)
- Result: **limited-risk, limited-reward** — significantly reduces long stock risk
- Primary use: protect gains late in calendar year without creating a taxable sale event

**Collar formulas:**
- Net Debit = (Put Price – Call Price) × 100
- Max Risk = [(Stock Price – Put Strike) × 100] + Net Debit
- Max Reward = [(Call Strike – Stock Price) × 100] – Net Debit
- Breakeven = Stock Purchase Price + Net Debit

**Example (ABC at $37.86, buy 37.50 put @ $1.20, sell 40 call @ $0.70):**
- Net Debit = $50; Max Risk = $86; Max Reward = $164; Breakeven = $38.36

[PAGE IMAGE: page_292.png] — flat bottom (put protection), rising middle, flat top (call cap)

### Calendar Spreads [MUST KNOW]

- **Calendar spread** = same type, same strike, **different expiration months**; long option in later month
- Longer-term option costs more → created for a **net debit**
- Short-term neutral/sideways; long-term directional bias

**When to use:**
- Call calendar: short-term neutral to bearish; long-term bullish
- Put calendar: short-term neutral to bullish; long-term bearish

**Three scenarios at short-option expiration:**
1. Stock significantly above strike: short assigned → exercise long, buy shares, or create bull call spread
2. Stock significantly below strike: sell long if still has value; consider outlook before holding
3. Stock near strike: sell long, roll into new calendar, or create new spread

**Risk graph** [PAGE IMAGE: page_298.png] — bell-curve/tent shape; max profit when stock pins at strike through short-option expiration

### Diagonal Spreads [MUST KNOW]

- **Diagonal spread** = same type, **different expirations AND different strike prices**
- Hybrid of vertical + calendar spread; can be debit or credit

**When to use vs. calendar:**
- Slightly more bullish short-term + bullish long-term → call diagonal debit
- Slightly more bearish short-term + bullish long-term → call diagonal credit
- Slightly more bearish short-term + bearish long-term → put diagonal debit
- Slightly more bullish short-term + bearish long-term → put diagonal credit

**Example:**
- Next month $50 call @ $1.80; 3-month $47.50 call @ $3.10
- Initial Debit (Max Risk) = $1.30 × 100 = **$130**
- Reward and breakeven points are variable estimates

[PAGE IMAGE: page_300.png] — asymmetric tent shape; peak near the $47.50 long call strike; unlimited upside after short expires

> "WARNING: If the long option covers a short option that expires, you'll hold a naked position. Risk ranges from limited-but-high to unlimited."

### Volatility Skews [SHOULD KNOW]

- **Price skew**: same-month options with significantly different IVs across strikes
- **Time skew**: different-month options with significantly different IVs
- **Capitalize on skew**: sell the high-IV option, buy the normal/low-IV option

## Chapter 13: ETFs, Options, and Other Useful Tricks

### ETF Basics [MUST KNOW]

- **ETF** = mutual fund that trades like a stock; tracks an index or segment
- ETF volatility tends to be **less** than its component stocks (diversification effect)
- Key advantage: you can **own** an ETF (unlike an index) → enables combination strategies
- Index options: cash-settled; European or American style
- ETF options: settled in underlying; **American style only**

**ETF vs. Index options — 3 key differences:**
1. ETF options enable combination strategies (you own the underlying)
2. Index options are cash-settled; ETF options deliver the underlying
3. Index options may be European style; ETF options are always American style

### ETF and Volatility [MUST KNOW]

- **HV (historical volatility)**: measures past price movement; comparison baseline
- **IV (implied volatility)**: expected 12-month future volatility implied by current option prices
- **Vega**: change in option value for every 1% change in IV
- **Bollinger Bands** on ETF price chart = quick view of relative volatility

[PAGE IMAGE: page_313.png] — 2-year SPY IV chart; 7-30 day = 17.23%, 30-60 day = 18.20%; IV spikes visible

[PAGE IMAGE: page_314.png] — 2-year SPY HV chart; 6-day = 21.19%, 100-day = 16.69%; shorter periods more jagged

**Trading rule**: when IV > HV, options expensive (favor selling); when IV < HV, options may be cheap (favor buying)

**Five key principles from SPY price + IV charts [MUST KNOW]:**
1. Technical analysis is a major component of options trading
2. Price of the underlying sets the tone for the option price
3. Let the underlying guide your strategy
4. Buy options when IV is low (risk is low)
5. Sell options when IV is high (risk is high)

### ETF Option Strategies [MUST KNOW]

- **Protective put**: long ETF + long put → unlimited upside, defined downside floor
- **Covered call**: long ETF + short call → income, moderate risk reduction, capped upside
- **Collared position**: long ETF + long put + short call → defined range, limited risk and reward

> "WARNING: Avoid low-liquidity ETFs even if they offer options. Wide spreads make it hard to exit."

## Chapter 14: Making Money Without Worrying About Direction

### Straddles and Strangles [MUST KNOW]

- **Core insight**: it's generally easier to anticipate a *change in volatility* than a change in price
- Two direction-neutral strategies: **straddle** and **strangle**
- Both combine a long call + long put; profit from a large move in either direction

### Long Straddle [MUST KNOW]

- Buy call AND put, **same strike, same expiration**
- Max risk = net debit paid; two breakevens:
  - **Downside breakeven** = Strike – (Call Price + Put Price)
  - **Upside breakeven** = Strike + (Call Price + Put Price)

**Three scenarios for straddle profit:**
1. Large move → ITM leg gains enough to cover cost of both options
2. Smaller move → ITM leg gains faster than OTM leg decays
3. Move in one direction, then reverses → sell each leg at different times

- Buy straddles when IV is **relatively low**; exit with **30+ days remaining** (two long options = 2× time decay impact)

[PAGE IMAGE: page_342.png] — V-shaped payoff; flat bottom at max loss; profits in both directions outside breakevens

**GS straddle example (GS at $178, 2 events: Fed meeting + earnings ~30 days to Oct expiry):**
- Oct 180 Call @ $9.40; Oct 180 Put @ $10.70 → Buy 2 of each
- Net Debit: $20.10 × 100 × 2 = **$4,020**
- Downside breakeven: $180 – $20.10 = **$159.90**
- Upside breakeven: $180 + $20.10 = **$200.10**

[PAGE IMAGE: page_343.png] — GS daily chart with Bollinger Bands; resistance ~$203, support ~$157
[PAGE IMAGE: page_344.png] — GS ATM IV chart; IV was low (~20-25%) then spiked to 40-50%

**Trade outcome:**
- On Fed meeting day, GS closed at $200.50 → exit 1 call at $22.80
- Two days later (earnings day) → exit remaining call at $30.10; exit both puts at $0.70
- Net gain: ($22.80 + $30.10 + $0.70 + $0.70) × 100 – $4,020 = **$1,410**

> "TIP: If you purchase an even number of contracts, you have an opportunity to do profit-taking while allowing greater gains for the portion left in place."

### Long Strangle [MUST KNOW]

- Buy call AND put, **different strike prices (both OTM)**, same expiration
- Lower cost than straddle → lower risk, but requires **larger move** to profit
- Breakevens are wider apart (call strike + total debit on upside; put strike – total debit on downside)

**GS strangle example (GS at $178):**
- Oct 185 Call @ $7.20; Oct 170 Put @ $6.70 → Buy 2 of each
- Net Debit = $13.90 × 100 × 2 = **$2,780**
- Downside breakeven: $170 – $13.90 = **$156.10**
- Upside breakeven: $185 + $13.90 = **$198.90**
- Outcome: ($18.80 + $25.50 + $0.30 + $0.30) × 100 – $2,780 = **$1,710**

[PAGE IMAGE: page_350.png] — **flat loss region** between $170–$185 (vs. V-bottom of straddle); profits in both directions beyond breakevens

> "REMEMBER: A strangle requires a bigger move than a straddle because there is a spread between the strike prices."
> "WARNING: The short strangle is extremely risky — combines limited-but-high risk (short put) and unlimited risk (short call)."

### Delta Neutral Trading [MUST KNOW]

- **Neutral view** = analyst opinion (neither buy nor sell)
- **Neutral strategy** = position benefiting from move up OR down, no directional bias
- Hedging = investing in securities that go up when original security goes down
- **Delta neutral** = position delta near zero; monitor delta + gamma throughout trade

> "WARNING: Delta neutral trading is NOT the holy grail. You still must evaluate potential risks and rewards and identify reasonable position sizes and exits."

**Calculating position delta:**
- Stock: +1 delta per share; options: variable
- Example: 100 shares + 2 puts (each delta –45) = 100 + (2 × –45) = **+10 combined delta**

**Real-life example — McKesson (MCK) [MUST KNOW]:**
- Bought MCK at $180 in May 2016 (100 shares = $18,000)
- Sept 19 (stock at $166): bought 3 Nov 160 puts @ $4.70 (delta ~0.32 → 3 puts ≈ delta neutral)
- Earnings Oct 27: MCK crashed, opened at $129, fell to $114
- Put trading range: $29.70–$45.24 → **550%–865% gain**; sold at $35.53 = **657% gain**
- Net P&L: stock sold at $129.80 + puts at $35.53 → **$4,229 gain (22.1%)**
- Without hedge: stock alone = **$5,020 loss (–27.9%)**

### Delta and Gamma Behavior [MUST KNOW]

- **Gamma is greatest for ATM options** and decreases as option moves ITM or OTM
- Gamma increases as expiration approaches
- Gamma always positive: when stock rises $1, call delta increases by gamma; put delta becomes less negative
- IV increases → all deltas move toward 0.50; time decreases → OTM deltas → 0, ITM deltas → 1

### Trade Adjustment Rules [MUST KNOW]

**How to adjust straddles:**
- Add more calls (+delta) or puts (–delta)
- Sell calls (–delta) or puts (+delta)
- Buy/sell stock

**When to adjust:**
- Once position becomes overly reliant on one direction
- If bought in high IV environment and IV declines: cut losses, don't adjust

### Volatility Skews [MUST KNOW]

- **Black-Scholes** assumes IV is constant across strike prices — reality: IV varies (sometimes significantly)
- **Price skew**: same-month options with different IVs across strikes
- **Time skew**: different-month options with different IVs
- **Forward price skew**: higher strike options have higher IV
- **Reverse price skew**: lower strike options have higher IV

**Trading edge**: sell high-IV options, buy normal/low-IV options as part of the spread

### Ratio Spreads [MUST KNOW]

**Ratio spread** = uneven number of long and short contracts; more short than long; generally for credit

> "WARNING: Ratio spreads have unlimited risk (call ratio) or limited-but-high risk (put ratio). Know and execute your exit BEFORE establishing."

**Call ratio spread** (neutral outlook, forward skew):
- Buy 1 lower strike call; sell 2+ higher strike calls; same month
- Usually 1:2 or 2:3 ratio

**Example (XYZ at $115.70):**
- Buy 1 XYZ 110 Call @ $7.70; Sell 2 XYZ 115 Calls @ $4.20
- Net credit: (2 × $4.20 – $7.70) × 100 = **$70**
- Max reward: $570 (at expiration with stock at $115)
- Breakeven (upside): **$120.70** → above this, unlimited losses

[PAGE IMAGE: page_382.png] — tent shape with unlimited downside risk arrow above breakeven

> "This is clearly an advanced strategy — I don't recommend it as your first trade, or at all, in fact."

**Put ratio spread** (neutral outlook, reverse skew):
- Buy 1 higher strike put; sell 2+ lower strike puts; same month
- Limited-but-high risk if stock falls sharply

[PAGE IMAGE: page_384.png] — tent shape with losses accelerating below breakeven

**Optimal conditions:**
- Call ratio spread: forward price skew; neutral outlook
- Put ratio spread: reverse price skew; neutral outlook
- Both: recent high volatility expected to subside

### Ratio Backspreads [MUST KNOW]

**Ratio backspread** = more long than short; **limited risk, potentially unlimited reward**

> "REMEMBER: Ratio backspreads have more long option contracts than short option contracts — all short options are covered."

**Call ratio backspread** (strongly bullish, reverse skew):
- Sell 1 lower strike call; buy 2+ higher strike calls; same month
- For credit if possible: profit if stock drops (keep credit) OR rises explosively
- Worst case: stock closes exactly at long call strike at expiration (max loss)
- Best case: explosive upward move

**Formulas:**
- Initial credit = [(# short × short price) – (# long × long price)] × 100
- Max risk = [(# short × strike diff) × 100] – initial credit
- Upside breakeven = Higher strike + [(strike diff × # short) ÷ (# long – # short)] – net option prices
- Downside breakeven = Lower strike + (net credit ÷ # short)

**Example (ABC at $69.90, 67 days, reverse skew):**
- Buy 3 ABC 75 Calls @ $0.60; Sell 2 ABC 70 Calls @ $2.45
- Net credit = [(2 × 2.45) – (3 × 0.60)] × 100 = **$310**
- Max risk (stock at 75): [(2 × 5) × 100] – $310 = **$690**
- Upside breakeven: **$81.90**; Downside breakeven: **$73.10**
- Outcome: stock barely moved, closed at $70.65; short calls bought back at $0.65 → **$180 profit**

[PAGE IMAGE: page_394.png] — reverse skew chart: lower calls have much higher IV; buying higher-strike, lower-IV calls
[PAGE IMAGE: page_395.png] — risk graph; below $70 = small credit profit; between $73.10–$81.90 = loss zone; above $81.90 = unlimited profit

**Put ratio backspread** (strongly bearish, forward skew):
- Sell 1 higher strike put; buy 2+ lower strike puts; same month
- Best case: explosive downward move

**Example (XYZ brokerage at $73.85):**
- Buy 2 XYZ 65 Puts @ $2.75; Sell 1 XYZ 75 Put @ $7.20
- Net credit = [(1 × 7.20) – (2 × 2.75)] × 100 = **$170**
- Max risk (stock at 65): [(1 × 10) × 100] – $170 = **$830**
- Max reward (stock → 0): $5,670
- Upside breakeven: **$73.30**; Downside breakeven: **$56.70**
- Outcome: stock fell to $55.08; short put @ $20.20; long puts @ $10.70 → **$290 total gain**

[PAGE IMAGE: page_399.png] — TWO breakevens visible; sweet spot above $73.30 (keep credit) or below $56.70 (profit from big decline)

**Implementation rules (both types):**
- Keep ratios to **1:2 or 2:3 at most**; calculate net delta
- Seek initial credit; if debit, keep as low as possible
- Use **90 days to expiration** when possible
- Take 50% profits on equal number of long + short legs at breakeven
- **Exit with 30 days to expiration** when time decay hurts long options
- Never hold ITM/ATM/slightly OTM short option into expiration weekend

> "Price skews provide ratio backspread opportunities; time skews provide calendar spread opportunities."

## Chapter 16: Trading Profitably When Markets Move Sideways

### Core Premise [MUST KNOW]

- Straight stock/ETF traders struggle during trendless periods
- Options allow profits when markets move sideways
- Two core sideways strategies: **long butterflies** and **condors**
- Sideways market is "an optionable trend"

### Covered Call in Sideways Market — XYZ/Dell Example [MUST KNOW]

**XYZ tech stock sideways case study:**
- Bought at $24.00 (late 2016); stop-loss at $22.00; stalls around $26.50 in March 2017
- HV and IV relatively low; earnings due mid-May
- Sell Apr 27.50 call @ $0.70 (delta 39.1 → 39% chance of assignment)
- Expiry: stock closed at $27.34 → call expires worthless; cost basis now reduced
- Sell May 27.50 call @ $0.55 (IV now slightly higher pre-earnings)
- Earnings: XYZ to $27.85; roll May call to Jun 30.00 call; net $0.30 loss on May call
- Final: Jun 30 call expires; stock at $23.68; **sell stock Monday**
- Result: **+$0.78/share gain** (covered calls) vs. **–$0.52/share** (buy and hold) = **0.8% vs. –2.2%**

[PAGE IMAGE: page_412.png] — Dell daily chart with Bollinger Bands + regression channel showing 18-month sideways grind; vertical lines mark each covered call sale

**Key lessons:**
- HV and IV decrease during trading ranges; Bollinger bands narrow
- Earnings/news can spike IV even during sideways price action
- When short calls close ITM: roll out a month and up a strike for modest gain
- Never place a standing stop-loss on covered call stock position → naked short call risk

> "WARNING: Do NOT place a standing stop-loss order for the underlying stock used in a covered call strategy — if triggered, you'll hold a short naked call."

### Butterfly Spreads [MUST KNOW]

**Butterfly** = two vertical spreads combined; limited risk, limited reward; profits from sideways trading

**Structure:**
- **Body**: 2 short options at middle (ATM) strike
- **Wing 1**: 1 long option at lower strike
- **Wing 2**: 1 long option at higher strike

**Call butterfly** = bull call spread + bear call spread (same short strike)
**Put butterfly** = bull put spread + bear put spread (same short strike)
**Iron butterfly** = bear call credit spread + bull put credit spread → creates a **credit**

### Call Butterfly — DIA Example (103-106-109) [MUST KNOW]

Setup: DIA at $106.48; ADX declining below 20 (trend losing strength); SMAs flat

[PAGE IMAGE: page_419.png] — DIA daily chart; 20-day + 30-day SMAs flat; ADX declining toward 20

**Trade:**
- Long 1 Aug 103 Call @ $4.00; Short 2 Aug 106 Calls @ $1.45; Long 1 Aug 109 Call @ $0.30
- Net debit = [(4.00 + 0.30) – (2 × 1.45)] × 100 = **$140** (= max risk)
- Max reward (two methods):
  - Bull spread: [(106 – 103) – (4.00 – 1.45)] × 100 = $45
  - Bear spread credit: (1.45 – 0.30) × 100 = $115
  - **Total: $160**
- Upper breakeven: 109 – 1.40 = **$107.60**
- Lower breakeven: 103 + 1.40 = **$104.40**

[CHART: chart_p421_02.png] — narrow profit tent; four time curves; at expiry = sharpest tent; max profit at $106 body strike

**Four expiration zones:**
1. Below $103: all options worthless → max loss $140
2. $104.40–$106: close bull call spread for profits; bear spread expires worthless
3. $106–$107.60: close bull call spread + additional short; still profitable
4. Above $109: close both spreads → max loss $140

**Outcome:** DIA closed at $105.73 → sold 103 call at $2.60, bought back 106 call at $0.05 → **$115 profit**

### Put Butterfly — DIA Example (103-106-109) [MUST KNOW]

- Long 1 Aug 109 Put @ $2.85; Short 2 Aug 106 Puts @ $0.85; Long 1 Aug 103 Put @ $0.30
- Net debit = [(2.85 + 0.30) – (2 × 0.85)] × 100 = **$145**
- Max reward: [(106 – 103) × 100] – $145 = **$155**
- Upper breakeven: 109 – 1.45 = **$107.55**; Lower breakeven: 103 + 1.45 = **$104.45**

[CHART: chart_p426_02.png] — nearly identical tent to call butterfly but slightly wider

**Outcome:** DIA at $105.73 → **$150 profit** (higher than call butterfly due to slight bearish move)

### Butterfly Wing Width Tradeoffs [MUST KNOW]

**Narrow wings → lower risk, higher reward-risk, tighter breakeven, more neutral bias:**

| Strikes | Risk | Max Reward | Reward-Risk | Breakeven % | Delta |
|---------|------|-----------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| 105-106-107 | $30 | $70 | 2.33 | 1.3% | –3.7 |
| 104-106-108 | $75 | $125 | 1.67 | 2.4% | –10.1 |
| 103-106-109 | $140 | $160 | 1.14 | 3.0% | –16.6 |

**Wide wings → higher risk, lower reward-risk, wider breakeven, more directional bias:**

| Strikes | Risk | Max Reward | Reward-Risk | Breakeven % |
|---------|------|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| 102-106-110 | $215 | $185 | 0.86 | 3.5% |
| 101-106-111 | $305 | $195 | 0.64 | 3.7% |

- **OTM body**: adds directional bias (slightly bullish/bearish)
- **Quick rule**: (breakeven spread) ÷ stock price = breakeven range %

### Iron Butterfly [MUST KNOW]

Created using bear call spread + bull put spread (both credit); position entered for a **credit**

**Strike structure (lowest to highest):**
1. Long put (wing)
2. Short put (body)
3. Short call (same strike as short put — body)
4. Long call (wing)

**Example (MO at $69.52, Jul 60-70-80 iron butterfly):**
- Long 1 Jul 60 Put @ $0.05; Short 1 Jul 70 Put @ $1.20
- Short 1 Jul 70 Call @ $1.30; Long 1 Jul 80 Call @ $0.05
- Net credit = [(1.30 – 0.05) + (1.20 – 0.05)] × 100 = **$240** (= max reward)
- Max risk = [(80 – 70) × 100] – $240 = **$760**
- Upper breakeven: 70 + 2.40 = **$72.40**
- Lower breakeven: 70 – 2.40 = **$67.60**

[PAGE IMAGE: page_435.png] — tent centered at $70; max profit at body; P&L axis –800 to +200

**Outcome:** MO at $69.80; short put bought back for $20; **net gain = $220**

### Condor Spreads [MUST KNOW]

- **Condor** = butterfly but with the center strike **split** into two short strikes
- Uses **four different strike prices**; one debit spread + one credit spread
- Increases range of profitability vs. butterfly
- Decreases maximum reward vs. butterfly

**Three condor types:**
1. Long call condor
2. Long put condor
3. **Long iron condor** (bear call spread + bull put spread, short legs ≥ 1 strike apart; created for credit)

**Butterfly vs. Condor comparison (stock at $134.45, 5-pt spreads):**

| Strategy | Risk | Reward | Reward-Risk | Breakeven Range |
|---------|------|--------|-------------|----------------|
| Butterfly 130-135-140 | $1.25 | $3.75 | 3.0 | 5.6% |
| Condor 130-135-140-145 | $2.45 | $2.55 | ~1.04 | 7.5% |

- Condor has higher probability of profit but worse reward-risk

**Five condor expiration zones:**
1. Below lowest strike: max loss on lower spread
2. Between lower breakeven and lower short strike: partial profit zone
3. **Sweet spot between two short strikes**: maximum profit
4. Between upper short strike and upper breakeven: partial profit zone
5. Above highest strike: max loss on upper spread

### Iron Condor — DIA Example [MUST KNOW]

- Long 1 Aug 103 Put @ $0.30; Short 1 Aug 106 Put @ $0.85
- Short 1 Aug 107 Call @ $0.85; Long 1 Aug 110 Put @ $0.15
- Max reward = [(0.85 – 0.15) + (0.85 – 0.30)] × 100 = **$125**
- Max risk = (3 × 100) – $125 = **$175**
- Lower breakeven: 106 – 1.25 = **$104.75**; Upper breakeven: 107 + 1.25 = **$108.25**

[PAGE IMAGE: page_442.png] — iron condor shape; sweet spot between $106–$107; time decay sharpens shape

**Outcome:** DIA at $105.73; short put bought back for $0.35 → **$90 profit** (vs. $115 call butterfly, $150 put butterfly)

> "Don't be in a hurry. Work it out before you risk real money. Paper trading really makes a difference."

**Key butterfly/condor rules:**
- Never let long options expire worthless while short options carry assignment risk
- Avoid zombie ETFs (low volume) for multi-leg strategies — slippage adds up
- Paper trade before live deployment

## Chapter 18: Ten Do's and Don'ts in Options Trading

### Do Focus on Managing Risk [MUST KNOW]

- Risk management is the #1 priority. "Tell them you're a risk manager."
- Options study rewires how you look at markets: options are inherently about managing risk, attention to detail, and planning the exit before entry
- **Portfolio-level risk**: diversify across sectors AND strategies, not just trade sizing
- Five or more trending trades in the same sector with the same strategy = violated portfolio-level discipline

### Don't Avoid Losses [MUST KNOW]

> "Keep your losses small and let your winners roll."

- Taking small losses is a **skill developed by experienced traders**
- **Reframe success**: a successful trade is one that follows your rules — not necessarily one that profits
- Duarte's biotech loss story: 30% loss on a failed clinical trial taught him to spot bad management and assess binary risk → became "immeasurable advantage"

### Do Trade with Discipline [MUST KNOW]

- Follow your rules on **every trade** — not sometimes, every time
- Early luck masking poor discipline is dangerous
- Per-trade discipline: allocate reasonable amount, identify max risk, identify entry/exit signals, execute when plan requires
- Deeper discipline: do homework, review trades after, assess plan regularly

### Don't Expect to Remove Your Emotions [MUST KNOW]

- Managing emotions is the goal; eliminating them is not possible (nor desirable)
- **What elevates emotion**: discretionary trading, trading during market hours, trading stocks that "owe you," trading during personal problems
- Practical: add emotional state column to trade log; if you can't sleep at night, your emotions are managing you

### Do Have a Plan [MUST KNOW]

> "When you fail to plan, you plan to fail."

- Writing by hand helps retention; revisit at 3 months, 6 months, then regularly
- Plan includes: risk management elements, general rules (buy low IV / sell high IV), steps to implement, separate analysis schedules for investments vs. trades

### Do Be Patient [SHOULD KNOW]

- Plan adjustments happen **outside of market hours**
- Sometimes the best action is no action — waiting for the right trade is a skill
- Reference: *Reminiscences of a Stock Operator* (Edwin Lefevre) — Jesse Livermore; "one of the few lucky ones who sold before the 1929 crash"

### Don't Suffer from Analysis Paralysis [MUST KNOW]

- The market is "predictably unpredictable" — even sound data doesn't guarantee outcomes
- **Going live changes everything**: paper trading won't replicate live emotional response
- Balance: after sufficient testing and practice, go live with **limited-risk strategies and proper trade sizes** to build real experience

### Do Take Responsibility for Your Results [MUST KNOW]

> "Never, ever, ever shift responsibility for trading results onto anyone or anything but yourself."

- Reference: *Trader Vic: Methods of a Wall Street Master* (Vic Sperandeo) — losing a trade because he answered his wife's phone call; concluded it was entirely his own fault for not having a rule
- If time is insufficient: shift to strategies you have time to do right, or step away entirely — markets will still be there when you return

### Don't Stop Learning [SHOULD KNOW]

- No single strategy works every time; external events never repeat exactly
- Master one or two strategies before adding more
- Set year-start general goals + specific goals (e.g., learn two new strategies, deepen technical analysis knowledge)

### Do Love the Game [MUST KNOW]

- Successful traders **love what they do** — love reading about markets, learning strategies, hearing other traders' experiences
- Making money as the **singular driver** eventually leads to large losses for most traders
- "Be passionate about your chosen field and embrace its challenges"
