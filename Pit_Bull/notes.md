# Pit Bull: Lessons from Wall Street's Champion Trader
## Martin "Buzzy" Schwartz (with Dave Morine and Paul Flint)

---

## Chapter 1: Trade or Fade (+ Mashed Potatoes)

[MUST KNOW] N001 — First day on the Amex floor, Aug 13 1979. Schwartz
buys his first trade: 10 Mesa Petroleum Oct 65 call options at $3 each
($3,000 total). He's terrified — "trade or fade" is the specialist
Chickie Miceli's challenge. Mesa immediately drops, he doubles down
buying 20 more at 2-9/16 ($5,125), then 20 more at 2-3/16 ($4,375).
Total: 50 call options, hemorrhaging money on day one.

[MUST KNOW] N002 — Background: Schwartz spent 9 years as a securities
analyst, bouncing city to city, kissing ass with portfolio managers.
Degrees from Amherst College and Columbia Business School, plus Marines.
Smart, well-educated, but couldn't make money in markets. His wife
Audrey pushed him: "You're 34, you've always wanted to work for
yourself. The worst that can happen is you go bust and go back to
being an analyst."

[SHOULD KNOW] N003 — Family history as motivation: father was "chosen
son" of immigrant family, went to Syracuse, graduated into 1929 crash.
Bounced jobs until buying a grocery store 4 doors from a First National
supermarket — doomed from the start. Mother said: "He was so desperate.
I had to give him the chance to fail. Even failure was better than doing
nothing." Schwartz terrified of repeating this pattern.

[MUST KNOW] N004 — Bob Zoellner: Schwartz's mentor, "the best trader
I'd ever met." Met him at Edwards and Hanly in 1973. Zoellner kept the
firm afloat in 1974 bear market by shorting stocks. Zoellner gave him
the Mesa tip and kept reassuring him to hold. Schwartz called Zoellner
repeatedly from the floor for emotional support.

[SHOULD KNOW] N005 — The Amex floor culture: B team of finance,
descendants of the Curb Exchange (bootleg traders on streets outside NYSE
from 1890s-1921). Guys named Chickie, Frannie, Donnie. Irish, Italians,
Jews — not WASPs. Crepe-soled shoes mandatory (no seat, just standing).
Members' lounge: Naugahyde not leather, cigarettes not pipes.

[SHOULD KNOW] N006 — Alexander Hamilton parallel: Schwartz sits in
Trinity Church Burial Ground after bad first day, in front of Hamilton's
grave. Hamilton was first Treasury Secretary but resigned due to personal
financial problems. Schwartz wonders how someone so smart could get
so screwed up — "Now I was beginning to understand."

[MUST KNOW] N007 — First trade outcome: Mesa opens up on day 3,
explodes higher. Schwartz sells 20 at 4, 30 at 4¼. Total proceeds
$20,750 on $12,500 invested = ~$8,000 profit. "I'd found the promised
land." Immediately rolls into Digital Equipment options with specialist
Frannie Santangelo, who won't give him a s'teenth. "I know you want to
show me who's boss, you fuckin' pizza parlor operator."

[MUST KNOW] N008 — "Mashed Potatoes" lesson: After a couple months,
Schwartz is up $10K on paper at 3:59 PM, dancing the mashed potatoes
with Hayes Noel instead of closing positions. Market opens way down
next day, loses the whole $10K. Lesson: "When you feel like doing the
mashed potatoes, it's a visceral clue that you've lost your objectivity,
you've gotten too emotional, and you're about to go into the shitter."
Cash the profits before celebrating.

## Chapter 2: The Plan (+ The Grubstake)

[MUST KNOW] N009 — The Plan originated from visiting Rich and Susan
Bertelli in Danbury, CT (summer 1978). Susan said "You need a plan."
Schwartz and Audrey earning $100K+ combined but couldn't afford a house.
Driving back, missed the turn to NYC, ended up heading to Newburgh.
Schwartz did a U-turn at the Fishkill toll plaza. That night they sat at
the kitchen table and wrote THE PLAN:
1. Develop a methodology for trading that fits my style
2. Accumulate a grubstake of $100,000 (within one year)
3. Make Zoellner my mentor
4. Get a seat on some exchange
5. Take a sabbatical from Hutton

[MUST KNOW] N010 — Methodology: Terry Laundry's Magic T Theory
was the key. Laundry was an MIT grad and marine living on Nantucket.
Theory: market spends equal time going up as going down. Cash buildup
phase is a prelude/reenergizing before the next up move. The T shape
represents bilateral symmetry. Schwartz worked 14 hrs/day, 7 days/week:
drawing trend lines, formulating opinions, reviewing charts nightly,
recalculating averages, figuring inflection points, setting entry/exit prices.
"The Magic T and I became as one."

[MUST KNOW] N011 — Transformation from fundamentalist to technician:
Before the plan, Schwartz was a fundamentalist (inflation rates, P/E ratios,
yields, profit margins). Now becoming a technical analyst/market timer.
Key difference: "A trader looks at the market as one living, breathing
organism instead of a collection of individual stocks." He was a
synthesizer, picking and choosing from many sources: Richard Russell's
Dow Theory Letter, Barron's, S&P Trendline Charts, Mansfield Charting,
CMI Charting, R.E. MacMaster's "The Reaper."

[SHOULD KNOW] N012 — Zoellner mentoring style: Schwartz would visit
him in Hackensack. Zoellner sat watching the tape machine (glass-bubbled
stand-up model) with a huge Atlantic salmon on the wall — his two loves.
"Marty, you have to feel the tape. It'll tell you everything you need to
know. It can rise on bad news and sink on good news. If you can read
the tape, you know when it's healthy, and when it's sick."

[MUST KNOW] N013 — Trading style emerging: Primarily trading options
(mostly calls, bullish on market) on major companies he knew from analyst
days (Syntex, IBM, Honeywell, Teledyne, Polaroid, Xerox). Scalping —
jumping in/out in hours or minutes. Positions $5K-$15K, looking for
$1K-$3K per play. "Singles hitter, not going for the home run."
After breaking even in '76-'77, now consistently making money.

[MUST KNOW] N014 — The process creates confidence: "The process of
doing the work was giving me an inner resource, a place to reach back
in my mind. It was helping me make better decisions. When you're
trading real time, and things start popping, you're challenged to make
immediate decisions. Having a methodology gave me strength, because
in my mind's eye I could see that I'd been there before."

[SHOULD KNOW] N015 — Leaving Hutton: Quitting on a Monday morning
(early in day, early in week). Audrey counseled honesty with boss Dan
Murphy rather than just walking out. Left on such good terms that Murphy
let him keep an office at Hutton for 6 more months — unheard of on
Wall Street where they normally "seal your files, check your briefcase,
give you a rectal exam, and escort you to the door." Because he was
going out on his own (vertical leap), he left like a hero.

[MUST KNOW] N016 — Grubstake philosophy: "You have to prove your
abilities and test your methods by actually trading, and making real money,
before you depend on trading for your livelihood." Borrowed $50K from
in-laws as backup (Dumbo's feather — never used it). Grubstake must be
earned through trading, not borrowed. "Gamblers Anonymous is full of
people who borrowed their grubstakes." Making it AND saving it teaches
money management discipline. Size it so no single trade can take you out.

## Chapter 3: Paradise Island (+ Viva Las Vegas)

[SHOULD KNOW] N017 — Gambling history as training ground: Baseball
cards (flipping against walls, age 7), shoveling snow for poker money,
caddying for grandfather Pappy, horse racing at Aqueduct and Hinsdale NH.
"I wasn't there to eat, drink, or socialize. I was there to make money."
He'd study racing forms, research trainers, chart jockeys, calculate
projected times, scrutinize tote boards. "Preparation pays."

[SHOULD KNOW] N018 — European casino trip (1967): Between Amherst
graduation and Columbia Business School. Parents gave $1K. Won money
across Europe but blew $400 in London craps playing against himself
(betting don't pass while being the shooter, making 7 straight passes).
Wrote dramatic journal entry about never gambling for large stakes again.
"Of course, it was all bullshit."

[SHOULD KNOW] N019 — Ricky G. and Carmine the bookie: Russian
Interrogation Team reserve unit in Brooklyn. Ricky G. got him connected
to bookie Carmine at the Aqua Vitae Diner in Yonkers. Code name:
"Maple Sugar." Football betting was fine, basketball got out of control.
Won $4K on a "double if-then reversal" bet — Louisville came back
from 15 points down. Collected cash in front of a Godfather movie poster.

[SHOULD KNOW] N020 — Fixed horse race: My*Tune at Aqueduct, tipped
off by Ricky G.'s friend Billy H. who knew a trainer. Won $2,800 at
7-2 odds. Hired a limo back to Kuhn Loeb instead of taking the subway.
"Nothing can beat knowing what's going to happen before it happens,
except when it doesn't."

[SHOULD KNOW] N021 — Honeymoon on Paradise Island: Audrey appeared
in negligee with champagne and chocolate strawberries. Schwartz: "Why
aren't you dressed? The tables open at one." She locked herself in the
bathroom. "Keep your priorities straight."

[MUST KNOW] N022 — Three rules from craps for trading:
1. DIVORCE YOUR EGO FROM THE GAME — don't get emotional,
   don't double up to get even. Be like the house, never personal.
2. MANAGE YOUR MONEY — keep money in safe deposit box at casino,
   force yourself to physically leave table to get more. Same with
   trading: keep money in separate account broker can't access.
3. CHANGE TABLES AFTER A WINNING STREAK — the luckier you've
   been, the surer your luck will change. Deposit winnings, then find
   a new table. Only way to avoid getting ground down by house edge.

[SHOULD KNOW] N023 — "Show me a great trader and I'll show you
someone who understands gambling." Academic degrees aren't enough.
"Unless they've got that feeling in their stomach, they can't toe the line,
they can't pull the trigger, they can't be a winner."

## Chapter 4: The Great Pyramid (+ Inside Skinny)

[MUST KNOW] N024 — Career at Kuhn Loeb (1970-72): First real job,
$16K salary. Mentor Abe Bronchtein (MIT grad, drug analyst). Got into
health care stocks: drug chains, then hospital management. Early lesson
from Four Seasons Nursing Centers — Jack Clark with his alligator shoes
was a fraud. Inflated construction profits as operating earnings. Stock
halted, Clark went to prison. "No rational person would pay $1,500 to
walk down the street in a depreciating asset."

[MUST KNOW] N025 — The Great Pyramid debacle: Schwartz moved to
a prestigious boutique firm (unnamed, called "The Great Pyramid") at
$50K salary. Unlike Kuhn Loeb's team culture, the Pyramid was everyone
for themselves. The Sphinx (accounting analyst) warned him hospital
management earnings were "fulla shit" — receivables inflated, Medicare
audits years behind. They co-wrote a negative report that was essentially
a SELL recommendation (unprecedented — "HOLD" was as low as you went).

[MUST KNOW] N026 — The leak: Analyst Paul Standish took the
confidential draft, shared it with someone at Scudder Stevens & Clark.
Scudder's clients started selling. AMI stock dropped 5-7/8, HCA fell 22%.
Schwartz testified 6 hours at NYSE. Eventually exonerated — Standish
admitted he leaked it. But then Schwartz was sued personally by
California stockholders for $816K alleging conspiracy and market
manipulation.

[MUST KNOW] N027 — Fired from Pyramid: Despite being exonerated,
the Pyramid kept him on payroll just long enough for suits to be dismissed,
then fired him. "On Wall Street, you didn't wear a bulletproof vest in
the front, you wore it in the back." Nobody else would hire him either —
he was a "tar baby." Filed for unemployment on Broadway and 89th.

[SHOULD KNOW] N028 — Sphinx was wrong long-term: Hospital management
stocks got crushed in 1973-74 bear market, but companies grew over
next 20 years as health care went from 6% to 16% of GNP. Report was
"part right and part wrong." But the experience toughened Schwartz up
and led him to meet Zoellner.

[SHOULD KNOW] N029 — What the Prophet should have said: Hilarious
rewrite of the encouraging memo — "Schwartz, you idiot. Nobody wants
a negative report. Trust officers want reports that cover their asses.
Stay away from the Sphinx. Start looking out for Number One."

[SHOULD KNOW] N030 — Commodities disaster: After being fired, took
summer off in Hamptons. Got into commodities — $5K into a computer
geek's time-sharing system (only ran at 3 AM), $20K into a Russian
wheat futures deal through a chain of people who supposedly had a
connection to the Department of Agriculture. Lost $25K total.

## Chapter 5: Auric Schwartz (+ Going for the Gold I)

[SHOULD KNOW] N031 — Gold fascination is cultural/genetic. Jews have
loved gold because it's portable wealth for people on the run. Grampa
Sam Schwartz escaped pogroms, kept savings in gold. Cashed in $20
Double Eagles at face value when he went senile in 1957 (worth ~$100
each). Gramma Rose saved a few; gave Marty a 1925D Saint-Gaudens
Double Eagle for his bar mitzvah. He became a numismatist.

[SHOULD KNOW] N032 — Goldfinger scene at age 19: Watching James
Bond with date Ellen Fine, became more interested in Auric Goldfinger's
plan to nuke Fort Knox than in the girl. "Why try to steal the gold when
you could just irradiate it?" Goldfinger became his hero.

[MUST KNOW] N033 — ASA gold stock options: In Dec 1979 started
trading ASA (South African gold investment company) options on the Amex.
Discovered correlation: Canadian/American gold stocks led the price of
gold itself. ASA tracked the metal more closely. When Canadian/American
gold stocks went up, buy ASA call options. Made $600K in 1980 and
$1.2M in 1981. When Reagan got inflation under control and gold cooled,
advantage disappeared. "Any old junkyard dog in a blue smock could
understand it."

[SHOULD KNOW] N034 — Going for the Gold I (Aug 1982): Inside Skinny
calls — "Volcker's called back all bank presidents. Mexico's going under."
Schwartz sends Audrey and brother Gerry to bank to retrieve $100K+ in
Krugerrands from safe deposit box. "Under the bed, where else. I'm
sleeping on it." Volcker reliquefied Mexico over the weekend, crisis over.
Audrey: "My arm still hurts from lugging it over here. I got it, you
can bring it back."

## Chapter 6: Made to Trade (+ Switch Hitting)

[MUST KNOW] N035 — S&P 500 futures: The "ultimate contract." Launched
by the Merc on April 21, 1982. Leo Melamed called it the "ultimate
contract." Schwartz saw immediately it was his game — all his techniques
(Magic T, 10-day moving averages, oscillators, stochastics) fit perfectly.
First trade April 29: bought 20 June SPMs, lost $370. Second day: bought
40 at 117.20, sold at 117.70, made $10,000.

[MUST KNOW] N036 — Discovery of cash bond/S&P correlation (Sept 1982):
Using his Telerate machine (quote service for physical bond prices),
Schwartz discovered that after-hours movement in cash Treasury bonds
predicted next-day S&P openings. If physicals went up after 3 PM bond
close, S&Ps would open up. This gave him a predictive edge — he knew
what was going to happen before it happened. Used this indicator to make
$160K in September (market was flat overall), then $1.4M in October 1982.
"I was one of the first to realize there was a direct correlation between
cash bonds and S&P futures."

[MUST KNOW] N037 — Leverage mechanics: S&P contract = 500 × index
value. With $120K in T-bills as performance bond, could control $2.4M
in assets. No cost of capital (earned interest on T-bills while trading).
20:1 leverage meant 5% move doubled or wiped out margin.

[MUST KNOW] N038 — Chicago Merc culture vs. New York: "I went to a
fight the other day and ended up trading futures in Chicago." Dual trading
allowed brokers to trade for own account while executing client orders.
Front running was "an art form." Brother-in-law standing next to broker
as bagman. "Pit committee" could "whistle out" trades — go back in time
and make trades disappear. Schwartz accepted "slippage" as cost of
doing business. He was supporting many "families" in Chicago.

[SHOULD KNOW] N039 — Switch Hitting: Schwartz used Frannie
Santangelo's upstairs office at the Amex. Jerry Muldoon and Leon
D'Agostino there to piggyback his ideas. One day Schwartz went long
50 ASA options, then went to the floor, didn't like what he saw, switched
to short. Jerry came at him with a softball bat: "How the fuck could you
change your position?" Lesson: "When the market changes sides, you
can't hold your position and hope it turns around." Be a switch hitter
like Mickey Mantle.

## Chapter 7: Never Short a Republican (+ The Losing Streak)

[MUST KNOW] N040 — The $800K loss (Nov 3, 1982): Election Day 1982,
Audrey goes mink coat shopping with her mother Sally Polokoff. Without
Audrey there, Schwartz loses his balance. Market surges 43 points (then-
record). He keeps shorting into it — 250 S&P contracts short by close.
Market locks limit up at 143.85 but the NYFE ("knife") keeps trading,
showing the S&Ps really at 145+. He keeps selling at the locked limit.
"Sunspot theory: 2% of the time, you become uncontrollably irrational."
Next morning, Audrey screams "Get smaller, get smaller!" Covers at
$800K loss (expected $1M). Fought back — down only $57K for Nov,
made $928K in Dec, ended year up $3M+ in futures. The mink coat:
"Very nice. But then it ought to be nice. It cost us eight hundred
thousand dollars."

[MUST KNOW] N041 — Audrey as trading partner: She sat at a 90-degree
L-shaped desk next to him. "I understood the market, and Audrey understood
me." She'd read his body language, scribbles, phone calls — knew what
he really wanted to do before he did. Would murmur "Buzzy, you like it.
Go for it." Her absence during the mink coat episode proved she was
essential. "Having Audrey sitting there, reinforcing my feelings, gave me
just the edge I needed."

[MUST KNOW] N042 — Losing streak advice: "The best way to end a
losing streak is to STOP. Stop the losses, stop the bleeding." You can
never shift from reverse to first without going through neutral. When you
come back, trade small with very tight stops. "Make little profits, make
little profits." Rebuild confidence before size. 1996 worst losing streak:
even when he saw the trades, fear of losing slowed his reaction time,
making him take MORE risk, not less.

## Chapter 8: Champion Trader (+ Honor Thy Stop)

[MUST KNOW] N043 — U.S. Trading Championship: Norm Zadeh, the
"Don King of trading," organized it. Started 1983. First contest: 74
entrants. Schwartz placed 3rd but won "Most Money Made" — more than
all other contestants combined. Second contest: 133 entrants, placed 6th
in futures (winner was amateur with 388% return on $5K minimum).
Then won the championship with 254.9% return, parlaying $482K to $1.2M
in 4 months, beating rival Frankie Joe by 3.4%. Next contest: 443.7%
return, 262 entrants. Frankie Joe died of heart attack shortly after.

[MUST KNOW] N044 — Trading style as boxing: Divided the day into 15
rounds (9:00 AM to 4:15 PM). Boxer-counterpuncher: "I'd spot an opening,
hit it, jump back. In and out, bob and weave, a point here, a point there."
Win 200 out of 250 trading days by small amounts (lose $5K, make $6K,
30-40 times a day). Win the other 50 days by clear knockout — $75K-
$150K. Total: ~$5M/year.

[MUST KNOW] N045 — Honor Thy Stop: "One of the great tools of trading
is the stop, the point at which you divorce yourself from your emotions
and ego and admit that you're wrong." Double Bogey bought Bay Networks
at $43, doubled down at $35, watched it sink to teens. "The problem
with amateurs is they only have half a plan — they know how much
profit they're willing to take, but they don't have the foggiest idea
how much they're willing to lose." The market is like the bleached blonde
at the craps table — she doesn't care about you. "Be like Odysseus:
tie yourself to the mast with an automatic stop."

## Chapter 9: Little Brown Bags (+ To Thine Own Self Be True)

[SHOULD KNOW] N046 — Park Avenue co-op: Bought 12-room apartment
for $3M cash (1/3 of net worth). Co-op board president (old garment man)
questioned if he could pay monthly maintenance. "You're in the commodities
business. That's like gambling." Schwartz's net worth was $9M. Another
"dumb financial decision" taking capital out of play, but he wanted to
taste the fruits. "There came a time when you had to spend the money
you'd been making so you could understand why you'd been killing
yourself."

[MUST KNOW] N047 — Third LaSalle Services crisis (April 1985): Moved
to new apartment, then clearing firm Third LaSalle went under due to
exposure to Bevill, Bresler & Schulman repo fraud. Schwartz had $1.2M
in T-bills in a regulated account (supposedly inviolate). Couldn't trade
for days. "DON'T FUCK WITH ME! That's a regulated account. It's
inviolate." Eventually made whole — account transferred to Saul Stone.
"The boys at the Merc went down to the Merc Club, sat around the bar,
and worked things out."

[SHOULD KNOW] N048 — Birth timing rule: "Don't trade too heavily the
month before and two months after your wife gives birth." Hormones
changing, no sleep, no dinner, no anything. Lost $150K in May-June-July
1983 around daughter's birth.

[SHOULD KNOW] N049 — Mark Cook's "What Makes a Successful Trader":
1. Complete commitment, full-time profession. "The market will quickly
find out if you are lazy." 2. Fit trading habits to your personality —
recognize your dominant emotion (fear or greed). 3. Planning is the
objective part — start with worst-case scenario. "Get out, retreat, and
live to fight another day."

## Chapter 10: Lots 204 and 207 (+ Big Shots Make Big Targets)

[SHOULD KNOW] N050 — Art collecting: American Impressionists as an
emerging market. Bought paintings at Sotheby's auction with advice from
Al Fresco and Cliff Palette (former Columbia classmate). Bought
Prendergast's The Garden for $240K and Frieseke's On the River for

The auction was "just a cleaner, more genteel Amex, and John Marion
was just a better-dressed Frannie Santangelo."

[SHOULD KNOW] N051 — Big Shots Make Big Targets: Visited Merc floor

hour. "Hey Schwartz! Come back again! You've been taking our money
over the phone for years! We like to take yours in person!" Lesson:
"Don't get in a shootout if you've left your guns at home."

## Chapter 11: Going for the Gold II (+ Sitting Down by the Lake)

[MUST KNOW] N052 — October 19, 1987 Crash: "I was long and I was
wrong." Up $8M for the year. Bought 40 S&Ps on Friday before the crash
at 283.50. Warning signs: Marty Zweig on Wall Street Week predicted
another 500 points down. Treasury Secretary Baker lambasting Germans
over interest rates. Monday: Dow plunged 150 in first 15 minutes. "The
marines teach you never to freeze under fire... But they were talking
about conventional warfare. I was getting nuked." At 10:30 bounce, sold
40 S&Ps at 267.50 (loss of $315K) — "one of the greatest trades of my
life." Timed it 1.50 off the day's high. Then dumped everything else.
Lost ~$2M total but stopped the bleeding.

[MUST KNOW] N053 — Going for the Gold II: Walked to bank during the
crash to retrieve Krugerrands. Passed David Rockefeller's townhouse,
saw limos parked outside — convinced it was an emergency meeting.
"These bastards weren't going to screw me." Cleaned out safe deposit
box: $250K+ in Krugerrands, jewelry, 1925D Double Eagle, deed to
apartment. Walked home carrying 40 pounds of gold. Cashed $20K at
Morgan Guaranty, told the assistant VP "you might not be open tomorrow."
After the crash, Dow ended Tuesday up 102 (record), Wednesday up 175.

[MUST KNOW] N054 — Soros trade (Oct 22, 1987): Was short 12 S&P
contracts at 255. Next morning, Shearson drops 1,000-contract sell
order for Soros's Quantum Fund at the market. S&Ps crash from 258 to
195-200 in minutes. Schwartz covers his 12-lot: 5 at 200, 5 at 210,
2 at 215. Profit: $290,000 (boys at Merc took ~$50K in slippage).
"One of the most famous trades in the history of the Merc." Soros
reportedly lost $800M. "I just remember it as the day I out-traded the
great George Soros."

[SHOULD KNOW] N055 — Zoellner's puke rule: "When it gets so bad that
you want to puke, you probably should double your position." Schwartz
went for the gold twice, and both times the market roared back and he
made a bundle. "Maybe Zoellner was right."

[SHOULD KNOW] N056 — Bob Prechter: Brilliant market technician,
predicted 1982 bull market, became guru of the 80s. After 1987 crash,
went totally negative and stayed negative even as market climbed 2000+
points. Schwartz told him in 1989: "Even if the market's going to crash,
ride it until it does." Prechter wouldn't listen. "Classic example of
somebody who's sure he's right and the market's wrong."

## Chapter 12: Commodities Corp (+ How I Read the WSJ)

[MUST KNOW] N057 — Commodities Corp (Princeton): Founded by Helmut
Weymar (MIT computer jock from Nabisco, 1969). Recruited academic
traders: Michael Marcus ($30K to $80M over 18 years), Bruce Kovner
($300M profits in 1987 alone), Paul Tudor Jones. Semi-Annual Trader's
Dinner: top dogs in trading gathered to sniff each other out. Schwartz
went in Armani suit and Bally alligators. They offered $250K to trade.
He laughed. "I'm making $5-6M a year. I'll give YOU $250K and you can
trade it for me." They upped to $10M with 30% of profits.

[MUST KNOW] N058 — OPM changes everything: "Having that much money
forced me to change my style and my time horizon. If I was wrong for
myself, I'd get right out. With hundreds of contracts, there was a
tendency to wait and give the position more time." When he lost
Commodities Corp's money, felt like the whole world was watching.
Basically quit trading their account within 2 months.

[SHOULD KNOW] N059 — Helmut Weymar liked Schwartz's methodology:
hand-drawn charts, moving averages, massaging the numbers. "Computers
are a great tool, but you've still got to get your hands dirty with data."

[SHOULD KNOW] N060 — How I Read the WSJ: Scanner, not a reader.
Goes straight to "What's News" column (front page left side) for market-
moving items. Then checks credit markets (bond yields), then the Money
& Investing section for market data. Total time: 20 minutes max. Never
reads the feature articles during market hours.

## Chapter 13: Sabrina Partners

[MUST KNOW] N061 — Sabrina Partners L.P. (domestic hedge fund) launched
1989. Also Sabrina Offshore Fund Ltd. Started with Elders Futures offering
$20M with 6% management fee + 20% performance fee. Schwartz's
mentor approach: had lunch with Paul Tudor Jones to discuss fund
structure. Jones ran Tudor Investment Corp from Greenwich. "Four and
twenty!" Jones growled about Schwartz's fees. "That's more than I charge!
I only charge one and twenty, and I'm the best!" Jones was eating a
blintz when he said it.

[MUST KNOW] N062 — OPM psychology fully explored: Schwartz found
that with investors' money, he couldn't trade the same way. His big
advantage (quick in/out, tight stops) broke down because: (1) larger
positions meant more slippage getting in/out, (2) investors expected him
to always be in the market, (3) losing their money felt different from
losing his own, (4) quarterly reporting created artificial pressure to
show profits. "I learned how to lose, and I was able to accept the loss,
but I couldn't do it with other people's money."

## Chapter 14: How's My Money Doing? (+ Sorry, Dad, You're Fired)

[MUST KNOW] N063 — Fund management stress: Investors called constantly.
"How's my money doing?" became the dreaded phrase. Every losing period
triggered calls, complaints, redemptions. Investors wanted to withdraw
at worst possible time, forcing liquidation of positions at losses.

[SHOULD KNOW] N064 — Neil Weisman: One of Schwartz's best friends,
met at the Great Pyramid in 1972. Weisman became a broker who was
always collecting information. Later became an investor in Sabrina Partners
and a sounding board.

[SHOULD KNOW] N065 — "Sorry, Dad, You're Fired": Schwartz's father
invested in Sabrina Partners. When the fund had a bad period, Schwartz
had to have the agonizing conversation. The father-son dynamic made it
even worse than with other investors.

## Chapter 15: Down the Tubes (+ Two Lessons for Life)

[MUST KNOW] N066 — Fund near-collapse: Albert Backward, Bernard Le
Buffoon, Helmut Scheisskopf, and Pierre Tete du Merde (pseudonyms for
European investors) were some of his worst investor experiences. Heavy
redemptions during bad periods. Schwartz pushing hard in November 1990
trying to post a strong month to retain investors.

[MUST KNOW] N067 — Two Lessons for Life:
I. BREAK THE PRESSURE BEFORE IT BREAKS YOU. Ray Gura was a
stocky options trader in the bullpen at Bear Stearns who died of a
heart attack. Schwartz was diagnosed with viral pericarditis (heart
inflammation). His body was breaking down from trading stress.
He saw a therapist who helped him understand he needed to separate
trading from the rest of his life.

II. YOU ARE NOT THE MARKET. The market doesn't know you exist.
It doesn't care about your positions. Stop taking it personally.
Get exercise, get sleep, see a therapist if needed. The physical
and psychological health are non-negotiable inputs to trading
performance.

## The Pit Bull's Guide to Successful Trading

[MUST KNOW] N071 — Core rules (laminated checklist):
- Check charts and moving averages BEFORE any trade
- Are we above or below the moving averages? (positive/negative mode)
- Are we above or below a dominant trend line?
- Has recent price action taken out previous highs or lows?
- Is the Magic T Oscillator in positive or negative mode?
- Always ask: "Do I really want to have this position?"
- Always know the uncle point before entering
- After a very profitable run, REDUCE position size
- After a successful period, take a day off as a reward

[MUST KNOW] N072 — The 10-day Exponential Moving Average (EMA):
GREEN LIGHT: price above 10-day EMA → look for longs only
RED LIGHT: price below 10-day EMA → look for shorts only or stay flat
Formula: New EMA = (Current × 0.18) + (Previous EMA × 0.82)
Tracks on: S&P 500, NY Composite, OEX, XMI, bond futures, Eurodollar
futures, S&P futures. NEVER fight the EMA trend.

[MUST KNOW] N073 — Channel lines (1% bands): Lines drawn 1% above
and below the 10-period moving average. Price near LOWER band in
green light mode = buy signal. Price near UPPER band in red light mode
= sell signal. Each weekend, writes channel levels on 5×8 index card
taped to quote machine as buy/sell targets for the week.

[MUST KNOW] N074 — Key indicators monitored:
- TICK (NYSE Net Ticks): -1000 = countertrend buy signal; +1000 = buy
  programs ending, likely reversal. DJIA up but TICK at -200 = bearish
  divergence, go short.
- TRIN (Arms Index): (adv issues/dec issues) / (adv vol/dec vol).
  Below 0.80 = buying bias (bullish). Above 1.20 = selling pressure.
- TIKI (DJIA Net Ticks): rarer, more powerful than TICK.
- Put/Call ratio: above 1.00 = contrarian buy. Below 0.45 = contrarian sell.

[MUST KNOW] N075 — "The most important change in my trading career
occurred when I learned to DIVORCE MY EGO FROM THE TRADE."
You are not playing against the market — the market doesn't know you
exist. You are playing against yourself. The sole objective is to hear
the cash register ring, not to prove you are right. Quote from Pogo:
"We have met the enemy, and they are us."

[SHOULD KNOW] N076 — Tricks of the trade:
- Gaps: Breakaway (bullish), Continuation (confirms), Exhaustion (reversal).
  If gap not filled in 2-3 days, strong signal to trade in gap direction.
- Mutual fund cash infusions: market stronger last day of month + first
  4 days of new month (new money invested).
- Three-Day Rule: after 3-day move, day 3 is the sucker play.
- How market reacts to news (Zoellner): bad news + market up = bullish.
  Good news + market down = bearish.
- "Worst fears not realized" = position is correct, should add to it.
- Program trading: wait for program-driven extremes, then counterattack.
- New highs/new lows: momentum is real, objects in motion stay in motion.
- Option expiration: severe drop before expiration week, then rally into
  expiration. Expiration day itself = stay out.
