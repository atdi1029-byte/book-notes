## SPEECH 1: Economic Club of Dallas — "Economic Security First"
**Date:** February 20, 2026
**Venue:** Economic Club of Dallas
**Source:** treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0403 (via mondovisione.com full text)
**As prepared for delivery**

### Framework: Three Pillars of Economic Security
Bessent structures the entire speech around three pillars:
1. Industrial and Technological Dominance
2. Investment in America
3. Preparedness

### Opening — Dallas as Model [SHOULD KNOW]
- Thanks Ray Washburne, Michael Levy, Economic Club of Dallas leadership
- Dallas: "a city built by risk-takers, created by a hydrocarbon base now stacked with Fortune 500 headquarters, and sustained by the simple conviction that prosperity follows production"
- Texas labeled "Y'All Street" — one of fastest-growing financial hubs
- "Parallel Prosperity is alive and well right here in Dallas"
- Texas as national model: "economic security rooted in production, resilience, and long-term, durable growth"

### Core Thesis — Economic Security as Foundation [MUST KNOW]
- "Economic security is the foundation that allows a country to fulfill its most basic obligation of safeguarding its people"
- For economic security: nation must possess "productive capacity to withstand shocks, deter adversaries, and deliver rising prosperity to its citizens"
- Past administrations allowed "faux efficiency gains and short-sighted profit-obsession to eclipse security resilience and long-term value"
- Trump "saw the points of failure decades ago"
- Cannot "compromise on economic sovereignty, accept structural vulnerabilities, or allow strategic industries to erode"

### Pillar 1: Industrial and Technological Dominance [MUST KNOW]

**Historical context:**
- Founding Fathers understood independence required economic security
- Hamilton: ability to produce essential goods, sustain public credit, foster domestic industry = foundation of sovereignty
- Post-WWII: US made deliberate trade concessions to rebuild allies, defend against Communism
- Those decisions brought global stability but with tradeoff: "as markets became freer, they did not become fairer"

**The China Shock:**
- "With the 'China Shock' of the 21st century, strategic industries were hollowed out, supply chains consolidated overseas"
- "We became wholly dependent on single foreign suppliers within certain sectors"
- Between 1999 and 2011: US lost nearly 6 million manufacturing jobs
- Not just employment loss — "diminished productive capacity and resilience"
- "A nation that cannot produce the critical goods and resources to sustain itself is exposed and vulnerable to coercion"
- COVID-19 "fully exposed these vulnerabilities on the world stage"

**Current policy:**
- Encouraging companies to "reduce single points of failure by diversifying their production"
- Bring "critical outputs back to the United States"
- Policies have "compelled firms to reassess their sourcing strategies and pour trillions in new investment back into American manufacturing"
- "Historic trade agreements have begun resetting the global trade paradigm"
- Diversified supply chains also "reduce inflationary volatility by lowering the risk of sudden disruption"

**Technology as existential battle:**
- "Industrial might and technological dominance are mutually dependent and reinforcing"
- "The global economy is undergoing a period of rapid transformation with breakthrough advances in artificial intelligence, quantum computing, and advanced manufacturing"
- "We are in an existential battle to maintain and accelerate technological dominance"
- "Production and development of AI infrastructure will be crucial to both economic growth and national security"
- Through FSOC: working with regulators on "responsible AI use in the financial system"
- Regulatory shift: "moving from a posture focused solely on constraint toward one that recognizes that failure to adopt productivity-enhancing technology is itself a risk"

**Stablecoins:** [SHOULD KNOW]
- "A well-regulated, dollar-based stablecoin market can reinforce the global role of the U.S. dollar and extend its network effects into emerging digital payment systems"
- GENIUS Act gives Treasury oversight for "transparency and confidence"

### Pillar 2: Investment in America [MUST KNOW]

**Dollar as reserve currency:**
- Dollar as reserve currency is "a key factor in enabling economic security"
- Benefits: anchors borrowing costs, deepens capital markets, strengthens sanctions, reinforces global finance leadership
- Status "rests on confidence in our institutions, and critically, in the health of the U.S. Treasury market"

**Treasury market significance:**
- "The Treasury market is the foundation of the global financial system as the benchmark risk-free asset"
- Serves as "collateral across markets and the channel through which global capital flows into the United States"
- "Preserving the strength, liquidity, and credibility of that market is central to economic security, and it is not something that can be taken for granted"

**Capital mobilization — regulation reform:**
- Over next 6 months: Treasury will "engage industry, academia, and national security experts to evaluate how supervisory frameworks can better mobilize capital toward sectors critical to national strength"
- Sectors: advanced manufacturing, energy infrastructure, semiconductors, defense innovation

**Trump Accounts — wealth inequality fix:** [MUST KNOW]
- "Almost 40 percent of Americans today have no exposure to the U.S. equity market"
- They participate "as workers and producers, but not as owners"
- Trump Accounts: "a fundamental rewriting of that arrangement"
- Every eligible American child receives "$1,000 Treasury-funded seed investment at birth"
- Invested in "diversified index fund tied to the long-term growth of the U.S. economy"
- Additional contributions from "philanthropists, families, employers, and state governments"
- "Give every child a stake in the American Dream from Day One"
- Texas Senator Ted Cruz authored the precursor provision in One Big Beautiful Bill
- Michael and Susan Dell: "historic $6.25 billion donation to top up Trump Accounts for 25 million children"
- "Economic security is strongest when it is broadly shared"

### Pillar 3: Preparedness [MUST KNOW]

**1973 oil embargo lesson:**
- Arab oil embargo: "A geopolitical decision thousands of miles away triggered drastic energy shortages, inflation, economic chaos, and market turmoil"
- Oil jumped "nearly 300%" before embargo lifted
- "A foreign power could so fundamentally disrupt the U.S. economy with the stroke of a pen"
- "The very definition of a single point of failure"

**Two current risk frontiers:**
1. "A major cyber incident disrupting banks, payment systems, or other financial market infrastructures"
2. "More than 90 percent of advanced chips are still manufactured in Taiwan"

**Deterrence:**
- "Preparedness must also include deterrence"
- "No adversary believes it can hold the American economy hostage or disrupt the well-being of American citizens"
- "Geopolitical risk must be a key factor considered in policy making across agencies, and not treated as an afterthought"

### International Posture [SHOULD KNOW]
- "America First does not mean America Alone"
- Economic security strengthened when Western Hemisphere is "aligned around free-market principles and the rule of law"
- Deepening cooperation with aligned partners
- Working with "longstanding allies such as the U.K., Japan, and the EU as they modernize regulatory frameworks"
- US hosting G20: "reject the premise of weakened global growth"
- Advancing "growth and economic security agenda promoting deregulation, competitiveness, and reciprocal opportunity"

### Supreme Court Response (Extemporaneous) [MUST KNOW]
- Addresses same-day Supreme Court ruling on tariffs
- "Despite the misplaced gloating from Democrats, ill-informed media outlets, and the very people who gutted our industrial base, the Court did not rule against President Trump's tariffs"
- Six Justices ruled "IEEPA authorities cannot be used to raise even one dollar of revenue"
- Administration will "invoke alternative legal authorities to replace the IEEPA tariffs"
- Will leverage "Section 232 and Section 301 tariff authorities that have been validated through thousands of legal challenges"
- "Use of Section 122 authority, combined with potentially enhanced Section 232 and Section 301 tariffs will result in virtually unchanged tariff revenue in 2026"

### Key Quotes
- "Prosperity follows production"
- "A nation that cannot produce the critical goods and resources to sustain itself is exposed and vulnerable to coercion"
- "We are in an existential battle to maintain and accelerate technological dominance"
- "Failure to adopt productivity-enhancing technology is itself a risk"
- "The Treasury market is the foundation of the global financial system"
- "Give every child a stake in the American Dream from Day One"
- "Economic security is strongest when it is broadly shared"
- "The very definition of a single point of failure" (on oil embargo)

## SPEECH 2: Reagan National Economic Forum — "While America Slept"
**Date:** May 29-30, 2026
**Venue:** 2026 Reagan National Economic Forum, Ronald Reagan Presidential Foundation and Institute
**Source:** treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0514 (via freerepublic.com full text)
**As prepared for delivery**

### Core Thesis [MUST KNOW]
America confused comfort for strength, efficiency for resilience, consumption for production. Economic security IS national security. The country drifted into dangerous dependencies through politically expedient decisions over decades.

### Opening — America's 250th as Reckoning [SHOULD KNOW]
- Special significance: celebrating 250 years of the American story
- "Milestones of this magnitude demand more than ceremony. They ask something of us."
- "They compel us to confront—and correct—decisions that have diminished our sovereignty in recent decades"
- Reagan quote: "One thing that has made our Republic great is that we don't hide from our mistakes. We learn from them; then we go on and do things better than we did before."

### "America Has Been Asleep" — The Diagnosis [MUST KNOW]
- "For too long, America had been asleep. We mistook comfort for strength."
- "We treated efficiency as a substitute for resilience, and consumption as a measure of prosperity"
- "We told ourselves that so long as goods were cheaper overseas, it did not matter whether factories went dark in Michigan, Ohio, or Pennsylvania"
- Assumed supply chains would always function, adversaries would behave responsibly, invisible hand would correct vulnerabilities
- "While we reassured ourselves with those assumptions, risks accumulated all around us"

### Foundational Principle [MUST KNOW]
- "Economic security is national security, for a nation that cannot manufacture, mine, ship, or refine its needs gradually cedes its strength—and sovereignty—to others"
- "That is a dangerous dependency for any country. It is an unacceptable one for the United States."

### The Drift Into Dependence [MUST KNOW]
- "Nations rarely cede their security in a single moment. More often, they drift into dependence through a series of politically expedient decisions."
- Mistakes were "some bipartisan, others ideological, and many defended long after their costs became impossible to ignore"

**Mistake 1 — Trade vs. National Strategy Siloed:**
- "Treating trade policy as though it existed apart from national strategy"
- "Convinced ourselves that we could pursue commercial partnerships in one silo while managing security relationships in another"
- "A nation that relies on a rival for critical inputs, finances the rise of countries that do not share its interests, and allows its own productive base to erode while promising to defend the international order will find, sooner or later, that those two silos collapse into each other"

**Mistake 2 — Strategic Trust Extended to China:**
- China's WTO accession and Permanent Normal Trade Relations "were sold to the American people as steps that would level markets and moderate behavior"
- "Instead, we left our workers to compete against state-led subsidies, excess capacity, and practices that distort trade and undermine reciprocity"
- "We assumed the rules-based system would discipline these behaviors only to find that in too many sectors, it accommodated them"

**Mistake 3 — Just-In-Time Over Just-In-Case:**
- "In the name of efficiency, we began to celebrate 'just in time' while neglecting 'just in case'"

### Supply Chain Vulnerabilities [MUST KNOW]
- "The pandemic did not create our brittle supply chains so much as expose them"
- "Suddenly, the world's most advanced economy found itself partially dependent on foreign suppliers for semiconductors, large-capacity batteries, critical minerals, and medicines"
- Post-pandemic recovery under prior administration "did not bring reform"
- Today: "US still imports most of the rare earth minerals essential to the technologies that will shape military and economic power"
- "Only a small share of the active pharmaceutical ingredients that we use in the United States are made here"
- "American shipbuilding capacity—which once helped to secure victory in war and prosperity in peace—has shrunk to a fraction of global production"

### Ignored Warnings [SHOULD KNOW]
- "Workers warned us when their plants shuttered and communities decayed"
- "Military planners warned us when supply chains narrowed"
- "Manufacturers warned us about subsidized competitors and dumped products"
- "Public health officials warned us about pharmaceutical dependencies"
- "The warning lights were glaring all around us. But our political class preferred the comfort of old formulas"
- "Cheaper was always better. Offshoring was inevitable. Industrial policy was unfashionable. And strategic dependence was acceptable so long as the cost remained invisible."

### The Philosophical Failure [MUST KNOW]
- "In reducing economics to consumption, we forgot production"
- "We measured abundance at the checkout counter rather than the factory gate"
- "We talked about GDP, but not enough about its composition"
- "We prized low-cost inputs without first asking whether a nation can remain sovereign when it loses command over the things that matter most"

### Manufacturing as Ecosystem [MUST KNOW]
- "Manufacturing is more than output on a balance sheet. It is a reservoir of practical capability"
- "Engineers and welders, tool-and-die makers and logistics networks, plant managers and workers who know how to solve problems on the factory floor"
- "When that ecosystem is strong, a country can adapt quickly. When it is hollowed out, adaptation becomes slower, more costly, and less uncertain"
- COVID: scramble for ventilators, masks, medical equipment
- Ukraine war: "high-intensity conflict consumes weapons and munitions at a pace far beyond peacetime assumptions"

### Surge Capacity [MUST KNOW]
- "Surge capacity cannot just be conjured overnight. It must be built before a crisis arrives"
- "A country that allows its defense-industrial base to shrink in the quiet years should not be surprised when it struggles to replenish stocks in the dangerous ones"
- "In a crisis, the nation with a broad and skilled industrial base has options. The nation that has hollowed out that base has excuses."
- "America should never again put itself in a position where its security depends on supply chains it does not control, factories it no longer maintains, or skilled trades it failed to preserve"

### Trump Administration Actions [SHOULD KNOW]
1. **America First Trade Policy memorandum** (Day 1) — directive to Treasury, Commerce, DHS, USTR to stop treating trade as "technocratic afterthought" and use it as "instrument of national strategy"
2. **Reciprocal tariff actions** (April) — declared national emergency tied to foreign trade practices; framed around national and economic security
3. **Section 232 critical minerals executive action** — linking import dependence to risks for national security, defense readiness, price stability, economic resilience
4. **Maritime dominance executive order** — established Office of Maritime and Industrial Capacity at NSC; Maritime Action Plan to rebuild shipbuilding
5. **Strategic Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients Reserve** — executive order to fill the reserve; tariffs on patented pharma; Section 232 investigations on PPE, medical devices, robotics

### What the Doctrine Does NOT Mean [MUST KNOW]
- "Does not mean retreating from the world. On the contrary, it means engaging with it on stronger, fairer, and more sustainable terms"
- "Does not mean severing ties indiscriminately. It means distinguishing healthy interdependence from dangerous overdependence"
- "Does not mean rejecting efficiency. It means refusing to worship efficiency when efficiency leaves our nation exposed"
- "Does not mean treating allies and adversaries alike. It means bringing our trading relationships and our security relationships back into alignment"
- Building supply chains with "trusted partners where appropriate, rebuilding capacity at home where necessary"

### America's Enduring Advantages [SHOULD KNOW]
- World's deepest capital markets
- Extraordinary innovators
- Abundant energy potential
- Leading universities
- Unmatched entrepreneurial culture
- Workers who "can out-build and out-compete anyone so long as the playing field is fair"
- "All we needed was a government willing to declare that as decline is a choice, renewal can be one, too"

### The Awakening — Closing Vision [MUST KNOW]
- "While America slept, our vulnerabilities grew"
- "While America slept, too many leaders accepted frail supply chains as the price of modern life"
- "While America slept, we let industrial capacity erode and Main Street decay. We tolerated unfairness in the name of stability and conflated market access with strategic success"
- "But America has awakened"
- New awareness: "a resilient economy is the foundation of a strong republic"
- "We can measure the health of our economy not merely by what it produces, but by whom it lifts"
- "Secure supply chains matter as much as stock indices. Productive capacity is power."
- "Trade policy, industrial policy, and national security policy must all fit together, or they will each fail separately"

### Closing Vision — What Renewal Looks Like
- "A more robust Main Street and industrial base than ever before"
- "Safer supply chains. More secure medicines. More reliable access to critical minerals"
- "A revived maritime capacity"
- "A financial system aligned with national purpose"
- "Trading partnerships that reinforce, rather than undermine, our security relationships"
- "The strength of this Republic lies not in denying that the old consensus failed"
- Must "restore resilience where complacency took hold; rebuild capacity where dependence took root"
- "Recover the confidence to believe a nation that knows how to build, invent, produce, and lead is never destined for decline"
- "Under President Trump, we have awoken to morning again in America"
- Conversation to follow with "Larry" (likely Larry Kudlow)

## SPEECH 3: Economic Club of New York (First Visit) — Three Pillars of America First
**Date:** March 6, 2025
**Venue:** Economic Club of New York, 118th Year, 790th Meeting
**Source:** econclubny.org PDF transcript (sb0045 on treasury.gov)
**Moderator:** Larry Kudlow (Fox Business Network, former NEC Director)
**Introduced by:** Chair Robert K. Steel (Perella Weinberg Partners)
**Full transcript including Q&A**

### Bio Context [NICE TO KNOW]
- 79th Secretary of the Treasury, sworn in January 28, 2025
- 40+ years in global investment management
- Former CIO of Soros Fund Management
- Founded Key Square Capital Management (global macro hedge fund)
- Taught history at Yale (his alma mater)
- South Carolina native, B.A. from Yale
- Philanthropy: McLeod Rehabilitation Center, Harlem Children's Zone, Rockefeller University
- Member of Council on Foreign Relations
- Advocate for financial literacy and education

### Three Pillars Framework [MUST KNOW]
Bessent structures the speech around three pillars of America First (explicitly excluding tax policy, which is saved for the Q&A):
1. Responsibly deregulating the financial sector — "re-privatization of the economy"
2. Reorienting international economic relations — tariffs and trade from first principles
3. Economic security = national security — Treasury's financial tools as foreign policy

### Pillar 1: Financial Deregulation — "Re-Privatization of the Economy" [MUST KNOW]

**Core philosophy:**
- "At Treasury, we will lead a comprehensive and assertive effort across the Administration to empower our nation's banks to finance the economy's pursuit of job growth, wealth creation, and prosperity for all Americans"
- Began career as financial sector analyst: "a strong, yet efficient regulatory framework is needed to mitigate risk and protect taxpayers' hard-earned dollars"
- "The regulatory overreach of the past few years in pursuit of political agendas has missed material risk, stymied growth, and squashed innovation"
- Goal: make regulation "more efficient, effective, and appropriately tailored"

**Executive order on regulatory review:**
- Requires OCC, FDIC, Federal Reserve to submit regulatory actions for review at Office of Management and Budget
- Purpose: "improve analytical rigor and discipline, while increasing accountability"

**SVB / 2023 banking crisis — supervisory failure:** [MUST KNOW]
- "The supervisory failures at the heart of the 2023 banking crisis under President Joe Biden should have been a wakeup call"
- Fed's own review: "supervisors did not fully appreciate Silicon Valley Bank's vulnerabilities as it grew in size and complexity"
- "When risks were identified, they did not take sufficient steps to ensure that SVB fixed those problems quickly"
- "The result was the third largest bank failure in United States history. It was a supervisory failure."
- Financial regulatory agenda must start with "fundamental refocusing of supervisors' priorities"
- "Leadership must drive a culture that focuses on material risk taking, rather than box checking"

**FSOC and coordination:**
- Plans to use FSOC and President's Working Group on Financial Markets "to drive change"
- "We need our financial regulators singing in unison from the same song sheet"
- Not consolidation of agencies — coordination via Treasury

**Large bank regulation — backward-looking problem:** [SHOULD KNOW]
- Banks "weighed down by unduly burdensome regulatory requirements and a broken supervisory culture"
- "Backward looking policies in response to an undercapitalized system predating the global financial crisis almost two decades ago should not drive today's approach"
- "Bank regulation and supervision should reflect the current needs of the economy"

**SLR (Supplementary Leverage Ratio) critique:** [MUST KNOW]
- "The enhanced supplementary leverage restriction, the SLR, can risk becoming a binding constraint instead of a backstop"
- Problem: "the safest asset in the country, U.S. Treasuries, are not treated as such when leverage restriction is applied"
- Some suggest risk-free exposures (central bank reserves, short-duration Treasuries) "should not be capitalized even under a risk-insensitive leverage capital restriction"
- Not making specific policy announcement, but "rigorous analysis must be applied"

**Community banks:** [MUST KNOW]
- "Given my small-town roots, I'm extremely focused on the success of America's community banks"
- 4,000 community banks operating nationwide in every county
- Hold only 15% of industry assets and deposits BUT:
  - 40% of loans to small businesses
  - 70% of agricultural loans
  - 40% of commercial real estate loans
- "Increasingly hamstrung with burdensome supervision and regulation"
- "Overloaded with unproductive reporting requirements that have little to do with reducing material financial risk"
- "Regulatory rating systems have suffered from mission drift as regulators apply subjective standards"
- "Productive and synergistic mergers are often slowed due to immaterial supervisory issues"

**De-leverage government, re-leverage private sector:** [MUST KNOW]
- Regulations should "ensure the safe and soundness of U.S. banks, drive affordability across goods and services and facilitate economic growth"
- Goal: "improving the efficiency and effectiveness of our financial sector to underwrite and finance domestic activity"
- "De-leveraging the public sector and re-leveraging the private sector begin and end with smartly reinvigorating our regulated financial institutions"

### Pillar 2: International Economic Relations and Trade [MUST KNOW]

**International trading system as web:**
- "The international trading system consists of a web of relationships — military, economic, political"
- "One cannot take a single aspect in isolation"
- Trump sees the world "not as a zero-sum game, but as inter-linkages that can be reordered to advance the interest of the American people"

**US as subsidizer of world order:**
- Other countries advanced their own interests "while our policymakers largely forgot about the tradeoff of unconstrained trade misalignment"
- "The United States provided a source of massive demand, acted as arbiter of global peace, but did not receive adequate compensation"
- US subsidizes world's under-spending in defense — "not just a security issue"
- US also "provides reserve assets, serves as a consumer of first and last resort, and absorbs excess supply in the face of insufficient demand in other countries' domestic models"
- "This system is not sustainable"

**The American Dream redefined:** [MUST KNOW]
- "Access to cheap goods is not the essence of the American Dream"
- "The American Dream is rooted in the concept that any citizen can achieve prosperity, upward mobility, and economic security"
- "For too long, the designers of multilateral trade deals have lost sight of this"

**What tariffs address:**
- "Leveling the playing field such that the international trading system begins to reward ingenuity, security, rule of law, and stability"
- Not: "wage suppression, currency manipulation, intellectual property theft, non-tariff barriers and draconian regulations"
- America First Trade Policy: "identifying bad actors across a range of criteria"
- Not just tariffs on exports — also non-tariff barriers, unfair fines, subsidies, currency manipulation

**Non-tariff barrier examples:**
- India on U.S. motorcycles, EU on American cars (quantitative tariff differences)
- Apple can't sell iPhone-16 in Indonesia due to local content laws
- Currency manipulation / suppression
- Unfair subsidization of industries via bank lending or wage suppression
- EU's "gigantic fines on our U.S. tech companies" = non-tariff barrier

**Burden sharing:** [SHOULD KNOW]
- "No longer should American tax dollars, American military equipment, and in some cases, American lives, be the sole bearers of upholding friendly trade and mutual security"
- "Burden sharing is not a matter of offloading risk, but a matter of all benefitting parties having interest in the system"
- "The shared interest ultimately strengthens the international system as the cost of disruption outweigh any benefits of dissolution"

### Pillar 3: Sanctions as Statecraft [MUST KNOW]

**Sanctions overuse warning:**
- Trump expressed view (in ECNY speech Sept 2024) that "overuse of sanctions could affect the U.S. dollar's supremacy"
- Bessent: "I couldn't agree more and would add that, not unlike the overuse of antibiotics, the target becomes immune and mutates"
- "Lackadaisical sanctions simply create new markets, which must then be sanctioned and so on"

**Russia sanctions critique:**
- Biden administration had "egregiously weak sanctions on Russian energy"
- Motivated by "worries about upward pressure on U.S. energy prices during an election season"
- Jake Sullivan raised sanctions "on the way out the door in January" — "a craven political move"
- "What was the point of substantial U.S. military and financial support over the past three years, without a commensurate and fulsome sanction support"
- Trump administration "kept the enhanced sanctions in place and will not hesitate to go all in should it provide leverage in peace negotiations"

**Iran maximum pressure:** [MUST KNOW]
- "The Iranian economy is in disarray; 35% official inflation, a currency that has depreciated 60% in the last 12 months, and an ongoing energy crisis"
- "I know a few things about currency devaluations, and if I were an Iranian, I would get all of my money out of the Rial now"
- Iran exports 1.5-1.6 million barrels per day via "ghost fleet" and "shadow network of financial facilitators and black-market oil shippers"
- Campaign targets "all stages of Iran's oil supply chain"
- "We will close off Iran's access to the international financial system"
- "Making Iran Broke Again will mark the beginning of our updated sanctions policy"
- "If economic security is national security, the regime in Tehran will have neither"

  1. Pass Tax Cuts and Jobs Act — "the sooner, the better" — permanence + full expensing of capital equipment retroactive to Jan 20

## SPEECH 4: Institute of International Finance — Bretton Woods Reform
**Date:** April 23, 2025
**Venue:** Institute of International Finance (IIF)
**Source:** treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0094 (via mondovisione.com full text)
**As prepared for delivery**

### Core Framework [MUST KNOW]
A "blueprint to restore equilibrium to the global financial system and the institutions designed to uphold it." The IMF and World Bank have strayed from their founding missions through mission creep and must be refocused.

### Opening — Bretton Woods Context [SHOULD KNOW]
