How to Read This Guide

This document consolidates every known error, stale number, missing topic, and cross-guide contradiction from all four finance guides: ATLAS The Asset Atlas, PLAYBOOK The Portfolio Playbook, INCOME The Income Architect, and FORGE The Metal Forge. Each item is tagged by guide and severity.

Severity levels: WRONG factually incorrect, needs fixing before anyone reads it. STALE was correct once, now misleading because markets moved. GAP topic promised or implied but never covered. CONFLICT two guides say opposite things.

Fixes Needed

Portfolio Playbook

PLAYBOOK WRONG

Ch 4 — Sharpe ratio contradiction: The same paragraph claims "Golden Butterfly has the highest Sharpe" and "60/40 has the highest Sharpe at 0.73." One of them is wrong. The Golden Butterfly's Sharpe is around 0.58–0.65 depending on the period; 60/40 at 0.73 is the higher figure. Check the source data and pick one.

PLAYBOOK WRONG

Ch 3/4 — 60/40 max drawdown: Listed as −31% in Chapter 3 and −35% in Chapter 4. The actual figure depends on the period: the 2008–09 drawdown for a 60/40 US portfolio was roughly −33% to −35%. Pick one number and use it consistently.

PLAYBOOK WRONG

Ch 4/5/20 — All-Terrain fund count: Described as 6 funds but the table lists 7. Worse, the Chapter 5 version (VTI 35% / BND 15% / DBMF 10%) doesn't match the Chapter 20 "Portfolio B" labeled the same name (VTI 40% / BND 15% / DBMF 5%). The reader building this portfolio gets different allocations depending on which chapter they follow.

PLAYBOOK WRONG

Ch 2/4/13 — Value drought dates: "2008–2025" in Chapter 13 vs "2010–2020" in Chapters 2 and 4. The commonly cited growth/value divergence ran roughly 2007–2020 (Russell 1000 Growth vs Value). The 2025 endpoint implies value still hasn't recovered, which overstates the case given the 2021–22 reversion.

PLAYBOOK WRONG

Ch 10 — Gold tax treatment: "Gold in taxable gets the LTCG rate" is incorrect. GLD and IAU are taxed as collectibles at a maximum 28% rate, not the standard 15/20% LTCG rate. Gold belongs in the IRA/Roth column, not taxable. DBMF and PDBC throw off ordinary income via K-1s and also belong in tax-advantaged accounts.

PLAYBOOK WRONG

Ch 13 — Momentum premium: The "+7–8%" cited is the academic long-short momentum premium (going long winners and shorting losers simultaneously). MTUM is a long-only fund that captures only a fraction of that spread. The table should label which kind each row represents — the raw academic premium is not what an investor in MTUM actually earns.

PLAYBOOK WRONG

Ch 2 — PDBC/DJP Sharpe: "Sharpe 2.35 / 2.30 over a decade" is implausible for commodity funds with double-digit volatility. A Sharpe above 2.0 sustained over a decade would make these among the greatest funds ever launched. Recheck the source. Also: DJP is an ETN (unsecured Barclays credit risk), not an ETF. Say so.

PLAYBOOK WRONG

Ch 4 — Golden Butterfly ER: Weighted expense ratio computes to ~0.16% with GLD at its current 0.40% ER, not the stated 0.12%. Check whether the calculation assumed a cheaper gold vehicle (IAU at 0.25% or GLDM at 0.10%) and clarify which fund the portfolio actually uses.

PLAYBOOK WRONG

Ch 20 Portfolio E: Uses GLD while the ETF comparison table names IAU as the primary gold fund. Either update Portfolio E to use IAU or explain why this portfolio specifically uses GLD (perhaps for options liquidity).

The 8% is a nominal increase per year of PIA (primary insurance amount), with COLA applied on top. The effective real return is close but not literally "8% real." This matters because readers comparing SS delay against bond yields need accurate framing.

Metal Forge

FORGE WRONG

Ch 13 — DSS reference: "Use the DSS Index Health indicator from the Asset Atlas" — no such indicator exists in the Atlas. Should point to Atlas Layer 14 (PMI, CPI, copper/gold ratio) for regime identification.

FORGE WRONG

Ch 2 — Gold-to-S&P ratio: "Bottomed near 1:1 in 1980 and 2011" doesn't hold for the S&P 500. The classic ratio is Dow/gold ≈ 1 in January 1980 and ≈ 6 in September 2011. The S&P/gold ratio has a completely different scale and history.

FORGE WRONG

Ch 23 — DJP roll methodology: DJP does not use the "Invesco DB optimized-roll algorithm." DJP is an iPath/Barclays ETN tracking the Bloomberg Commodity Index with standard roll rules. The optimized-roll methodology belongs to the DB family: DBA, DBB, DBC, and PDBC.

FORGE WRONG

Ch 35 — GLD LEAPs tax: "LEAPs on GLD are taxed as collectibles" needs rechecking. An options contract itself is not a §408(m) collectible. The 28% collectible rate applies if you exercise and hold the underlying shares. Selling the LEAP before expiry is a standard capital gain/loss. The distinction matters for the options strategy the chapter recommends.

FORGE WRONG

Ch 13 Regime 4: Gold's 2008 liquidation selloff is dated to March. The actual forced-liquidation drawdown was October–November 2008 (−25% peak to trough). Gold rallied in the initial Sep–Oct panic, then sold off as margin calls hit everything.

FORGE WRONG

Barrick ticker: Barrick Gold rebranded to Barrick Mining and now trades as B, not GOLD. Referenced in Chapters 29 and 35 under the old ticker. Every mention needs updating.

FORGE WRONG

Ch 31 — PSLV: PSLV is recommended for physical silver exposure but never gets its own card in Chapter 12 (the ETF/equity chapter). Also, PSLV is a PFIC (passive foreign investment company) which requires a QEF election to avoid punitive tax treatment — this is mentioned nowhere. Chapter 29 (tax) should cover this.

Stale Data

All four guides carry a "Last updated August 2026" or similar stamp but are anchored to mid-2024 market data. In a world where gold went from $2,500 to $5,000+ and the Fed cut rates, the stale numbers change actual conclusions.

Portfolio Playbook

PLAYBOOK STALE

Gold allocation data: No data after 2022 despite gold's record run since then. The guide calls 15–18% gold "optimal" based on data that ends before the biggest move in gold's history. This is the single most consequential stale number across all four guides.

PLAYBOOK STALE

Managed futures: Only shows the good years (2022 CTA boom). The weak 2023–24 stretch — exactly the "will you hold this through a drawdown?" test the guide emphasizes for other assets — is completely absent. A reader who bought DBMF or KMLM in late 2022 on this guide's recommendation sat through 18+ months of underperformance with no context.

Income Architect

INCOME STALE

T-bill yields: SGOV/T-bills anchored at 5.0%, which is a 2024 number. Bills have been well below that since the 2024–25 Fed cuts. Portfolio A (55% SGOV/VGSH) has its income overstated by roughly a fifth, and the caveat about "reinvestment risk when rates eventually fall" has already materialized. The reader building Portfolio A today gets substantially less income than the guide promises.

INCOME STALE

SCHP yield: TIPS fund distributions float with CPI, but the guide presents "4.0%" as if it were a fixed coupon in the model portfolios. In a lower-inflation environment, the actual yield could be significantly lower. Presenting a floating number as fixed misleads the income planner.

INCOME STALE

Ch 16 — Social Security: Omits the Social Security Fairness Act (WEP/GPO repealed January 2025) and the trust-fund depletion date (~2033–34, with a ~20%+ cut absent new legislation). For a guide that frames SS as "the best bond you own," the biggest risk to that bond — a potential 20% haircut in 7–8 years — is a glaring omission.

FORGE GAP

London silver squeeze (Ch 30/31): October 2025 — 1-month lease rates spiked to a record ~35–39% before easing as metal shipped from NY and China. This is the third silver squeeze alongside the Hunt brothers (1980) and WSB (2021). Chapter 30's squeeze history section needs this entry.

FORGE GAP

Rare earths and critical minerals (Ch 9): China's 2025 heavy-REE export controls and magnet shortage. DoD–MP Materials price-floor/equity deal. DRC cobalt export suspension (2025). Antimony, tungsten, gallium, and germanium export controls. Aluminum and steel Section 232 tariffs at 50% (Chapters 8/36 still say 2018 rates).

FORGE GAP

Jurisdiction risk updates (Ch 11/25): Cobre Panamá shutdown (2023) is the modern social-license/nationalization case study. Mali's seizure of Loulo-Gounkoto. Niger/Orano uranium. Pebble EPA veto. Resolution/Oak Flat. Juukan Gorge. Mexico under Sheinbaum post-AMLO. The chapter discusses jurisdiction risk in theory — these are the examples.

FORGE GAP

FORGE WRONG

Ch 27 — Bitcoin reserve: "No sovereign holds Bitcoin as a reserve" — the U.S. Strategic Bitcoin Reserve Executive Order was signed in 2025. Whether you count it as a true "reserve" or a policy signal, the categorical claim is now false.

Cross-Guide Conflicts

These are cases where two or more guides give contradictory advice to the same reader. A person reading all four will notice and lose trust in whichever one they read second.

CONFLICT CONFLICT

Gold tax treatment: The Forge (Ch 29) correctly states gold ETFs are taxed as collectibles at 28% max and belong in a Roth. The Playbook (Ch 10) incorrectly says "gold in taxable gets the LTCG rate." These are opposite claims about the same asset. Resolution: The Forge is right. Align the Playbook to match.

CONFLICT CONFLICT

Withdrawal philosophy: The Playbook (Ch 9) says "don't chase dividends, use total return, 3.7% baseline SWR." The Income Architect (Ch 13/15) says "spend 4.6–5.7% natural yield, never touch principal." These are opposite strategies presented as best practice to the same reader. Resolution: Add a reconciling paragraph in both guides. The total return approach is academically stronger but psychologically harder; the natural yield approach provides behavioral guardrails at a tax cost. Name the trade-off.

CONFLICT CONFLICT

REITs: The Playbook (Ch 2/16) calls REITs an "overrated diversifier" and says homeowners should skip them. The Income Architect (Ch 14) puts 5–10% REITs in every model portfolio. Resolution: Both can be true if the framing is different (total return vs income), but neither guide acknowledges the other's position. Add a cross-reference.

CONFLICT CONFLICT

Tax drag: The Income Architect (Ch 1) says income investing is worth "any theoretical tax drag." The Playbook (Ch 10) quantifies that tax drag at 0.6–1.5% per year. One guide dismisses it; the other puts a number on it that adds up to serious money over decades. Resolution: One sentence in the Income Architect acknowledging the trade-off would square them.

CONFLICT CONFLICT

SGOV expense ratio: 0.09% in the Atlas and Playbook, 0.05% in the Income Architect. The correct figure is 0.09%. Small in absolute terms but the inconsistency undermines credibility on a fund all four guides recommend.

CONFLICT CONFLICT

Regime identification: The Playbook (Ch 8), the Forge (Ch 13), and the Income Architect (Ch 12) all describe economic regimes with no shared methodology for identifying the current one. All three should point to a common framework — Atlas Layer 14 exists for exactly this purpose but none of them link to it. Resolution: Each guide's regime chapter should cross-reference Atlas L14 and use the same indicator set.

CONFLICT CONFLICT

Crypto stance: The Atlas has no crypto layer at all. The Playbook dismisses crypto in one line. The Forge (Ch 27) gives a detailed 0–5% portfolio sizing framework. Three different stances for the same reader. Resolution: Pick one approach (the Forge's is the most thoughtful), reference it from the others, and give the Atlas a crypto layer.

CONFLICT CONFLICT

"Figures as of" dates: All four guides say "August 2026" but carry 2024 numbers. A single "figures as of [date]" convention per chapter across the entire shelf would fix most of the staleness perception and let readers calibrate their trust.

Content Gaps: Portfolio Playbook

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 2 — Missing asset classes: Cash and T-bills get no standalone section despite being a core building block. TIPS are used in most Day One portfolios but only appear as a table row with no explanation of how they work, when they shine, or their quirks (phantom income, deflation floor). Currency hedging via HEFA/DXJ and the home-bias debate deserve coverage. Crypto should be evaluated on the same correlation/drawdown framework as every other asset, with a sizing rule (0% or 1–3% capped).

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 3–4 — Period mismatch: The comparison tables mix data from 1926–2024, 1972–2020, 1978–2020, "30-year," and "10-year" windows. A reader can't compare portfolios when each one uses a different measurement period. Add a common-period table (e.g., 1978–2024) where every portfolio gets the same ruler. Also missing: leveraged implementations (NTSX, RPAR), the Larry Portfolio, and the Faber Ivy Portfolio.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 5 — 401(k) translation: The guide tells you exactly which ETFs to buy but most readers' largest accounts are 401(k)s with limited menus. A "closest index fund on your menu" section mapping ETFs to mutual-fund equivalents would be the most actionable page in the entire guide. Also missing: SGOV/USFR for cash management, VT as a single-fund solution, and AVDV.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 6 — Life-stage gaps: Social Security and pensions should be treated as bond-like assets that shift your effective allocation (a $30,000/yr pension is like holding $750,000 in bonds). Goals under 10 years (house down payment, tuition) need goal-based buckets, not a single age-based glide path. The rising glide path concept needs a caveat that the effect is small and assumes the reader is comfortable holding high equity exposure at age 80.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 7 — Cross-account rebalancing: The chapter discusses rebalancing within a single account but never addresses the reality that most people have 3–5 accounts (401k, Roth, taxable, HSA, spouse's 401k). The key insight: treat all accounts as one portfolio and do your rebalancing inside the tax-advantaged accounts where trades are tax-free.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 8 — Regime identification: The chapter describes four economic regimes but provides no way to identify which one you're currently in. Link to Atlas Layer 14 or provide a simple PMI/CPI/yield-curve framework. Also fix the "2021–2022 rising growth" example, which misidentifies the regime.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 9 — Retirement income: Missing several critical retirement planning tools: TIPS ladders for inflation-protected income floor, SPIAs and annuities as longevity insurance, withdrawal sequencing across account types (which account to draw from first), Roth conversions during the "gap years" between retirement and RMDs, and Social Security claiming age as a portfolio lever.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 10 — Tax tools: Treasuries are state-tax-exempt — a material benefit in high-tax states that changes the tax-equivalent yield calculation. Municipal bonds (VTEB) are completely absent despite being the most tax-efficient fixed income for high earners. The HSA (triple tax advantage) deserves its own card.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 11 — Return measurement: The chapter discusses tracking performance but never explains the difference between money-weighted returns (XIRR — what you actually earned given your cash flows) and time-weighted returns (what the fund earned irrespective of when you added money). Most investors use the wrong one and either overcount or undercount their performance.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 12 — Missing common mistakes: Employer-stock concentration (holding too much company stock in your 401k), using margin or leverage without understanding the mechanics, and "waiting for a dip" cash drag (the opportunity cost of sitting in cash while waiting for a pullback). These are among the most wealth-destroying retail mistakes.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 14 — Emergency scenario: "Lost my job and need cash now" is the most stressful financial scenario a reader will face and the one where they most need guidance. The chapter covers market crashes but not personal financial emergencies — which account to tap first, whether to sell investments or use a credit line, how to sequence spending cuts.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 15 — Bond depth: Missing: convexity (how bond prices react non-linearly to rate changes), starting yield as the best forward-return predictor for bonds, individual Treasuries and ladders vs bond ETFs (which have no maturity date and thus different risk characteristics), and TLT's full 2020–23 drawdown (~−45%) as the definitive case study for duration risk.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 16 — Home economics: A mortgage is a negative bond (you're short duration). The pay-down vs invest decision depends on the mortgage rate relative to expected after-tax returns — this is one of the most common financial decisions people make and the chapter doesn't address it.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 17 — Tax-loss harvesting depth: The wash-sale rule is mentioned but substitute pairs (VTI↔ITOT, VXUS↔IXUS) aren't provided. Also missing: tax-gain harvesting in 0% LTCG bracket years (a free Roth conversion equivalent), the Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT) 3.8% surcharge, donor-advised funds for appreciated shares, and direct indexing for portfolios with large embedded capital gains.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 18 — Fund costs: Tracking difference (the actual gap between an ETF's return and its index) is more important than the expense ratio (the stated fee), because tracking difference includes securities lending revenue, sampling error, and cash drag. Some funds with higher ERs have better tracking difference than cheaper competitors.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 19 — Automation: Auto-invest and auto-rebalance features at Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard, and M1 Finance remove the behavioral risk of "I'll do it next month." Also missing: the concept of a written investment plan simple enough that a spouse or executor could follow it without financial expertise.

PLAYBOOK GAP

Ch 20 — Day One prerequisites: The "Day One" chapter tells readers to start investing immediately but never mentions the emergency fund precondition (3–6 months of expenses in cash before investing a dollar). This is absent from the entire guide, not just this chapter. Also missing: account sequencing priority (401k up to employer match → HSA → Roth IRA → back to 401k → taxable).

Global Issues

These affect all four guides and should be fixed with a single convention applied everywhere.

CONFLICT GAP

Sources and citations: None of the four guides cite sources for their claims, data tables, or statistics. A reader who wants to verify a number (like the "60/40 returned 9.1% since 1926" claim) has no way to trace it. Each chapter should have a brief sources section or at minimum a footnote for key data points.

CONFLICT GAP

Glossary: The Portfolio Playbook, Income Architect, and Metal Forge have no glossary. The Atlas doesn't either. Terms like "Sharpe ratio," "convexity," "AISC," "EFP," and "SOFR" are used without definition. A shared glossary across all four guides — or at minimum one per guide — would make them accessible to less experienced readers.

CONFLICT GAP

Cross-references: The four guides cover overlapping territory with no links between them. When the Playbook discusses gold, it should point to the Forge. When the Income Architect discusses regimes, it should point to the Atlas. The guides were written as a system but read as isolated documents.

CONFLICT GAP

"Figures as of" convention: All four guides should have a per-chapter or per-layer "figures as of [date]" line so readers know exactly how stale the data is. Better than one "Last updated" stamp at the bottom that overstates currency.

CONFLICT GAP

Income Architect renumbering: Chapters 4B and 9B should get proper sequential numbers. The lettered suffixes make the table of contents inconsistent and suggest they were inserted after the fact.

CONFLICT GAP

Metal Forge changelog: Needs a changelog and per-chapter "figures as of" dates given the extreme price moves since mid-2024.
