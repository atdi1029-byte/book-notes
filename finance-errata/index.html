How to Read This Guide

This document lists every content gap across all four finance guides: ATLAS The Asset Atlas, PLAYBOOK The Portfolio Playbook, INCOME The Income Architect, and FORGE The Metal Forge. Each item is tagged by guide. Topics that were promised, implied, or logically belong but were never covered.

Content Gaps: Portfolio Playbook

PLAYBOOK

Ch 2 — Missing asset classes: Cash and T-bills get no standalone section despite being a core building block. TIPS are used in most Day One portfolios but only appear as a table row with no explanation of how they work, when they shine, or their quirks (phantom income, deflation floor). Currency hedging via HEFA/DXJ and the home-bias debate deserve coverage. Crypto should be evaluated on the same correlation/drawdown framework as every other asset, with a sizing rule (0% or 1–3% capped).

PLAYBOOK

Ch 3–4 — Period mismatch: The comparison tables mix data from 1926–2024, 1972–2020, 1978–2020, "30-year," and "10-year" windows. A reader can't compare portfolios when each one uses a different measurement period. Add a common-period table (e.g., 1978–2024) where every portfolio gets the same ruler. Also missing: leveraged implementations (NTSX, RPAR), the Larry Portfolio, and the Faber Ivy Portfolio.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 5 — 401(k) translation: The guide tells you exactly which ETFs to buy but most readers' largest accounts are 401(k)s with limited menus. A "closest index fund on your menu" section mapping ETFs to mutual-fund equivalents would be the most actionable page in the entire guide. Also missing: SGOV/USFR for cash management, VT as a single-fund solution, and AVDV.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 6 — Life-stage gaps: Social Security and pensions should be treated as bond-like assets that shift your effective allocation (a $30,000/yr pension is like holding $750,000 in bonds). Goals under 10 years (house down payment, tuition) need goal-based buckets, not a single age-based glide path. The rising glide path concept needs a caveat that the effect is small and assumes the reader is comfortable holding high equity exposure at age 80.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 7 — Cross-account rebalancing: The chapter discusses rebalancing within a single account but never addresses the reality that most people have 3–5 accounts (401k, Roth, taxable, HSA, spouse's 401k). The key insight: treat all accounts as one portfolio and do your rebalancing inside the tax-advantaged accounts where trades are tax-free.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 8 — Regime identification: The chapter describes four economic regimes but provides no way to identify which one you're currently in. Link to Atlas Layer 14 or provide a simple PMI/CPI/yield-curve framework. Also fix the "2021–2022 rising growth" example, which misidentifies the regime.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 9 — Retirement income: Missing several critical retirement planning tools: TIPS ladders for inflation-protected income floor, SPIAs and annuities as longevity insurance, withdrawal sequencing across account types (which account to draw from first), Roth conversions during the "gap years" between retirement and RMDs, and Social Security claiming age as a portfolio lever.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 10 — Tax tools: Treasuries are state-tax-exempt — a material benefit in high-tax states that changes the tax-equivalent yield calculation. Municipal bonds (VTEB) are completely absent despite being the most tax-efficient fixed income for high earners. The HSA (triple tax advantage) deserves its own card.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 11 — Return measurement: The chapter discusses tracking performance but never explains the difference between money-weighted returns (XIRR — what you actually earned given your cash flows) and time-weighted returns (what the fund earned irrespective of when you added money). Most investors use the wrong one and either overcount or undercount their performance.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 12 — Missing common mistakes: Employer-stock concentration (holding too much company stock in your 401k), using margin or leverage without understanding the mechanics, and "waiting for a dip" cash drag (the opportunity cost of sitting in cash while waiting for a pullback). These are among the most wealth-destroying retail mistakes.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 14 — Emergency scenario: "Lost my job and need cash now" is the most stressful financial scenario a reader will face and the one where they most need guidance. The chapter covers market crashes but not personal financial emergencies — which account to tap first, whether to sell investments or use a credit line, how to sequence spending cuts.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 15 — Bond depth: Missing: convexity (how bond prices react non-linearly to rate changes), starting yield as the best forward-return predictor for bonds, individual Treasuries and ladders vs bond ETFs (which have no maturity date and thus different risk characteristics), and TLT's full 2020–23 drawdown (~−45%) as the definitive case study for duration risk.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 16 — Home economics: A mortgage is a negative bond (you're short duration). The pay-down vs invest decision depends on the mortgage rate relative to expected after-tax returns — this is one of the most common financial decisions people make and the chapter doesn't address it.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 17 — Tax-loss harvesting depth: The wash-sale rule is mentioned but substitute pairs (VTI↔ITOT, VXUS↔IXUS) aren't provided. Also missing: tax-gain harvesting in 0% LTCG bracket years (a free Roth conversion equivalent), the Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT) 3.8% surcharge, donor-advised funds for appreciated shares, and direct indexing for portfolios with large embedded capital gains.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 18 — Fund costs: Tracking difference (the actual gap between an ETF's return and its index) is more important than the expense ratio (the stated fee), because tracking difference includes securities lending revenue, sampling error, and cash drag. Some funds with higher ERs have better tracking difference than cheaper competitors.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 19 — Automation: Auto-invest and auto-rebalance features at Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard, and M1 Finance remove the behavioral risk of "I'll do it next month." Also missing: the concept of a written investment plan simple enough that a spouse or executor could follow it without financial expertise.

PLAYBOOK

Ch 20 — Day One prerequisites: The "Day One" chapter tells readers to start investing immediately but never mentions the emergency fund precondition (3–6 months of expenses in cash before investing a dollar). This is absent from the entire guide, not just this chapter. Also missing: account sequencing priority (401k up to employer match → HSA → Roth IRA → back to 401k → taxable).

Content Gaps: Asset Atlas

ATLAS

L0 — Market plumbing: Treasury issuance and TGA mechanics (bill vs coupon mix, debt-ceiling drains and refills). Global central banks (BOJ, ECB, PBOC) and the yen carry trade (the August 2024 unwind was the biggest cross-asset shock of the year and gets no mention). Vol-control, CTA, and risk-parity flows. Leveraged-ETF end-of-day rebalancing mechanics. Vanna, charm, and VIX-expiration Wednesdays.

ATLAS

L1 — Small cap fix: IJR/VBR (profitability-screened small cap) solves the "40% of Russell 2000 companies are unprofitable" problem but has no card. VT (total world in one fund) is missing. AVUV should move here from Layer 15.

ATLAS

L2 — Fixed income depth: Agency MBS card (MBB/VMBS) and convexity explanation. A real yield-curve section (10y/30y yields, term premium, Fed funds/SOFR futures). USFR/TFLO for floating-rate. ZROZ/EDV for ultra-long duration. EMLC for EM local-currency bonds. Target-maturity ladders (iBonds, BulletShares).

ATLAS

L3 — Sectors: Independent power producers (VST, CEG, NRG) — the AI-power thesis is mentioned in 4 cards but none has a ticker. Autos, OIH, XME, ITB. Private credit and BDCs (BIZD).

ATLAS

L4 — Commodities: Cocoa/sugar (the 2024 cocoa spike is a perfect contango/backwardation case study). REMX and critical minerals. Physical uranium. KRBN (carbon credits). Baltic Dry and shipping as an indicator. Commodity supercycle and capex cycle framing.

ATLAS

L5 — Currencies: Beyond UUP: FXE, FXY, FXB, FXF, CYB, EM basket, and the carry-trade concept. Country ETFs: ARGT, EWQ.

ATLAS

L6 — Volatility depth: Short-vol side: SVIX/SVXY plus the February 2018 XIV "Volmageddon" as a warning. Single-stock leveraged ETFs (NVDL/TSLL). TMF. Inverse ETFs (SH/PSQ). VVIX and SKEW as vol-of-vol indicators.

ATLAS

L7 — Thematic (biggest hole): Crypto is the largest gap in the entire Atlas: IBIT, FBTC, ETHA, MSTR, WGMI, BLOK — none have cards. Cannabis and metaverse are named in the intro but have no cards. Missing: QTUM, GRID, SRVR/DTCR. YieldMax single-stock income funds deserve a warning card.

ATLAS

L8/8B/15 — Missing stocks: IBM, ANET, GEV, CEG/VST, SNPS/CDNS, DELL/SMCI/VRT, CVS, HCA, MCK, ADP, ORLY/AZO, CSX, DAL/UAL, MAR/HLT, RCL/CCL, COF, HOOD, MSTR, USB/PNC/TFC, EPD/ET/KMI, CF/NTR, ADM/BG. ADRs: BABA/PDD, TM, SAP, SHEL/BP, BHP/RIO/VALE, NVS/AZN, LVMH.

ATLAS

L9 — Deep sector ETFs: OIH, XME, XAR/PPA, PPH/IHF, IYZ, BJK, PEJ, IGF, SEA.

ATLAS

L10 — Advanced fixed income: MBS, BNDX, SRLN, JBBB, SHYG, private credit. Callable bonds and convexity explanation.

ATLAS

L11 — Missing countries: ARGT, EWQ, EIS, EWN, GREK, EPHE, Nordics.

ATLAS

L12 — Commodity gaps: Replace dead JO ticker. Add cocoa, sugar, lumber, UGA, BNO, SIL.

ATLAS

L13 — Strategy ETFs: PFIX, SVOL, BTAL, KMLM/CTA, SWAN, QAI.

ATLAS

L14 — Indicator dashboard: ISM Services PMI, weekly jobless claims, JOLTS, Sahm rule, retail sales, consumer sentiment and inflation expectations, housing starts, LEI. Valuation gauges: CAPE, forward P/E, equity risk premium. Real yields and breakevens. 3m/10y yield curve. Funding stress (SOFR–IORB, FRA-OIS). Breadth beyond A/D line (% above 200-DMA, new-highs minus new-lows). Sentiment surveys (AAII, NAAIM, COT). Net Liquidity is defined in L0 but not on the dashboard.

Content Gaps: Income Architect

INCOME

Ch 2 — Structural hole: No annuities at all (SPIA, DIA, QLAC) — this is the largest single gap in the income guide. Annuities are the only instrument that provides guaranteed lifetime income, which is literally the guide's topic. Also missing: CDs and brokered CDs, royalty trusts (a classic yield-trap category), interval funds, and non-traded BDCs.

INCOME

Ch 3 — Dividend ETFs: VYM (the largest dividend ETF by AUM) is missing. COWZ and the shareholder-yield concept (dividends + buybacks) aren't covered. Buybacks vs dividends as capital return mechanisms. The 61-day qualified-dividend holding period rule. Individual stock examples. The 2009 evidence (S&P dividends fell ~20%; ~10% of payers cut or suspended in 2020) to stress-test the "cash is real" argument.

INCOME

Ch 4 — Bond instruments: Target-maturity ETFs (iBonds, BulletShares), TIPS ladder, USFR, I-Bond detail. HY and EM bonds (HYG/JNK/EMB are named but never get their own card). Callable bonds. Agency MBS.

INCOME

Ch 4B — International dividends: SCHY and VIGI/IGRO missing. The foreign tax credit limitation and $300/$600 simplified election aren't explained.

INCOME

Ch 7 — Preferreds depth: Baby bonds ($25 exchange-traded debt). $1,000-par institutional preferreds. AT1/CoCo risk (the Credit Suisse 2023 wipeout is the case study). Bank preferreds are mostly non-cumulative — this caveat is absent and matters enormously during a bank stress event.

INCOME

Ch 8 — Covered calls: The table of contents promises "rolling your own" covered calls but there's no DIY section (strike selection, delta, cash-secured puts, the wheel strategy). Missing: SPYI/QQQI, 0DTE covered-call ETFs. YieldMax single-stock funds belong in Ch 10 as a "distribution rate ≠ yield" warning.

INCOME

Ch 9 — CEFs: PDI and NUV are named in prose but have no cards. Missing: discount z-score analysis and distribution coverage/UNII (undistributed net investment income) as health metrics.

INCOME

Ch 9B — Private credit: Non-traded BDCs (BCRED etc.). CLO ETFs (JAAA/JBBB). The 2025 private-credit scare (First Brands, Tricolor) as a stress test for the "private credit is safer than HY" claim.

INCOME

Ch 10 — Yield measurement: Never defines how yield is measured. SEC 30-day yield, trailing twelve-month (TTM) yield, and distribution rate are three different numbers that can vary by 100+ bps for the same fund. A reader comparing funds without knowing which metric to use will make bad decisions.

INCOME

Ch 11 — Tax depth: 0% qualified-dividend and LTCG bracket (a powerful tool for low-income years and early retirees). IRMAA (income-related monthly adjustment amount for Medicare premiums — a stealth tax that catches retirees). Taxation of Social Security benefits (provisional income formula). State income taxes on dividends and interest.

INCOME

Ch 12 — Regime context: Stagflation regime coverage. "Which regime are we in now" pointer to Atlas Layer 14.

INCOME

Ch 14 — Portfolio costs: Weighted expense ratio per model portfolio. Fee layering for CEFs and BDCs (management fee + incentive fee + fund-level borrowing costs).

INCOME

Ch 15 — RMDs and conversions: Required Minimum Distributions force withdrawals from the exact IRA sleeves the guide recommends, starting at age 73. This is a structural conflict with the "never touch principal" philosophy. Roth conversions and bracket management during the gap years aren't covered.

INCOME

Ch 16 — Social Security depth: Spousal and survivor benefits. Earnings test before full retirement age. The Roth-conversion window as part of the SS-delay bridge strategy.

Content Gaps: Metal Forge

FORGE

Ch 12 — ETF and equity cards: PSLV, PALL, GLDM, SILJ, URNM/physical uranium, REMX. Individual stocks: Newmont, Barrick, Agnico Eagle, Royal Gold (described as "one of three dominant streamers" but never actually profiled), BHP, Rio Tinto, Vale, SCCO, Ivanhoe, Cameco, Albemarle, MP Materials.

FORGE

Ch 8 — Industrial metal equities: Investment vehicles per metal: Alcoa/CENX (aluminum), Glencore (diversified), Alphamin/MLX (tin), Teck/Boliden (base metals). Also: Simandou iron ore coming online as a supply shock.

FORGE

Ch 10/18 — Mining business model: Hedge books (the Barrick late-1990s disaster is the case study). Co-product vs byproduct AISC accounting (changes which metals look profitable). Streaming obligations as debt-like claims on future production.

FORGE

Ch 14 — Valuation: Free-cash-flow yield at spot price. Reserve price decks vs spot (reserves are booked at $1,400–1,700 gold, far below current spot). EV/EBITDA for base-metal miners.

FORGE

Ch 21 — Signals: The real-yield model has broken since 2022 (real yields at 2%+, gold tripled — the inverse correlation is dead or dormant). Gold making ATHs in non-USD currencies as a leading signal. COMEX registered vs eligible inventory. EFP/basis trading.

FORGE

Ch 28 — Drill results: CuEq/AuEq (copper-equivalent/gold-equivalent) grade inflation as a promotional trick used by junior miners. S-K 1300 reporting standard alongside NI 43-101 and JORC.

FORGE

Ch 32 — Lassonde curve: The chapter describes the lifecycle of a mining project (exploration → development → production) and the stock-price pattern it creates — but never names this as the Lassonde Curve, which is what every mining investor calls it.

FORGE

Ch 29 — Tax: Canadian dividend withholding (recoverable in taxable accounts, lost in IRAs). NIIT (3.8% surcharge on collectibles gains). State sales tax on physical bullion (varies by state). PFIC/QEF election for Sprott trusts (PHYS, PSLV).

FORGE

Ch 22 — Physical gold: Costco gold bars as a demand channel (surprisingly significant retail flow). Self-directed gold IRA mechanics and custodian requirements.

FORGE

Ch 36 — Steel: U.S. Steel card needed. CLF–Stelco acquisition. Section 232 tariffs now at 50% (chapter still references the 2018 rate).

Global Issues

These affect all four guides and should be fixed with a single convention applied everywhere.

CONFLICT

Sources and citations: None of the four guides cite sources for their claims, data tables, or statistics. A reader who wants to verify a number (like the "60/40 returned 9.1% since 1926" claim) has no way to trace it. Each chapter should have a brief sources section or at minimum a footnote for key data points.

CONFLICT

Glossary: The Portfolio Playbook, Income Architect, and Metal Forge have no glossary. The Atlas doesn't either. Terms like "Sharpe ratio," "convexity," "AISC," "EFP," and "SOFR" are used without definition. A shared glossary across all four guides — or at minimum one per guide — would make them accessible to less experienced readers.

CONFLICT

Cross-references: The four guides cover overlapping territory with no links between them. When the Playbook discusses gold, it should point to the Forge. When the Income Architect discusses regimes, it should point to the Atlas. The guides were written as a system but read as isolated documents.

CONFLICT

"Figures as of" convention: All four guides should have a per-chapter or per-layer "figures as of [date]" line so readers know exactly how stale the data is. Better than one "Last updated" stamp at the bottom that overstates currency.

CONFLICT

Income Architect renumbering: Chapters 4B and 9B should get proper sequential numbers. The lettered suffixes make the table of contents inconsistent and suggest they were inserted after the fact.

CONFLICT

Metal Forge changelog: Needs a changelog and per-chapter "figures as of" dates given the extreme price moves since mid-2024.
