## Misadventures in Trading (Schwager's Personal Account)

### "Scared Money" and the British Pound

- [MUST KNOW][N013] **"Scared money" virtually guarantees failure** — cascade of errors: miss best opportunities, exit prematurely, take profits too quickly, yet paradoxically STAY in losers (frozen by indecision).
- [MUST KNOW][N014] **Waiting for the truly compelling trade is an accidental edge** — Schwager inadvertently practiced extreme selectivity. **James Rogers**: "I just wait until there is money lying in the corner."
- [SHOULD KNOW][N015] **Post-sharp-break sideways consolidation is bearish** — everyone waiting to sell the first rally means the rally never comes.

### The Harvey Trade: Cascade of Errors

- [MUST KNOW][N016] **Taking tips initiates a cascade of trading sins**: greed → violated beliefs → no exit criteria → ignored signals → surrendered risk control.
- [MUST KNOW][N017] **If you enter on someone else's analysis, you have no exit mechanism** — asymmetry of entering on someone else's logic while exiting on your own is structurally impossible to execute.
- [MUST KNOW][N018] **When a market moves opposite to expected news response, that is a major warning signal** — contrarian response to news = exit cue.

### Ed Seykota Insights

- [MUST KNOW][N019] **"Everybody gets what they want out of the market"** — traders must audit hidden psychological agendas. People who keep losing may be satisfying a need to lose.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N020] **Magazine covers as contrarian indicators** — by the time a trend warrants a cover, it's fully discounted. "Read the covers and forget the articles inside."

### Core Lessons

- [MUST KNOW][N021] **You don't always have to be in the market** — need BOTH confidence AND desire to trade. Trading with only one is a setup for loss.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N022] **Trading sins cascade** — first error is rarely the expensive one; it's the errors that follow FROM the first.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N023] **Markets confound the most traders** — when everyone waits for the same thing, it doesn't happen.

## PART II: Bill Lipschutz — The Sultan of Currencies

### FX Market Structure

- [MUST KNOW][N011b] **FX is the world's largest market** — ~$1 trillion/day; 24-hour decentralized OTC market following the sun. Primary function: hedge exchange risk.
- [MUST KNOW][N037] **FX is fundamentally a relationship business** — same $10M order gets radically different treatment based on relationship. Anonymous call: "dealer's in the bathroom." Known call: "for you I'll see what I can do."

### Lipschutz Background

- [SHOULD KNOW][N018b] Started in Cornell architecture, grandmother's $12K stock portfolio became trading capital. Tape-reading before formal theory — watched cable TV stock tape for hours, "developed a feel for price action."
- [SHOULD KNOW][N023b] **Sidney Gold hired based on market memory depth** — "Do you know this specific chart, this specific level, this specific history?" Not resume, not credentials.

### Salomon Culture

- [MUST KNOW][N027] **Salomon Brothers' culture** defined the firm — "street fighters betting their own money who really understood what it meant to take risk." Training indoctrinated values person-to-person.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N028] Culture degraded when programs scaled from 120 to 500 people/year — personal transmission broke down at scale.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N029] **John Gutfreund** could "smell death at a hundred paces" — read trader anxiety to assess portfolio health before looking at numbers.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N031] Salomon was "comfortable with risk or with losing money, as long as the trade idea made sense."

### Cross-Domain Competitive Advantage

- [MUST KNOW][N040] **Unique convergence**: Lipschutz was the only person on the FX desk who knew what a put or call was — everyone else from fixed income. Currency options and the FX department grew up together.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N039] **Relative knowledge advantage matters more than absolute expertise** in early markets — Lipschutz leveraged the perception gap between his options knowledge and the FX market's baseline.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N035] **Entering a new market at its inception** — even without expertise — can be more valuable than entering an established market with credentials, because you grow with the domain.

### Salomon FX Department Origins

- [SHOULD KNOW][N032] Department founded with no business plan, no outside hires. Gil from bond arbitrage had zero currency experience. "That's not how Salomon did things. Everything was homegrown." Lipschutz didn't know what a Deutsche mark was when he joined.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N045] **First person at Salomon with a home terminal** — management thought it was insane. "Foreign exchange is a twenty-four-hour market. It doesn't go to sleep when you leave at 5 P.M."
- [MUST KNOW][N046b] **Information network superiority via interbank acceptance**: traders who stayed aloof couldn't make money — ended up calling CME floor clerks while Lipschutz talked to bankers in Tokyo, London, Frankfurt simultaneously.

### The Philadelphia Specialist Trade

- [MUST KNOW][N050b] **Long-term business decision**: specialist was quoting options 100 points off. Lipschutz bought, locked profit. Then BROKE all trades after the first 50 — holding him to the full position would have put the specialist out of business, destroying the marketplace Lipschutz needed. Goldman refused to break any trades → years later, specialist-turned-head-trader made Goldman's life difficult. **Short-term maximization can destroy long-term relationship infrastructure.**

### The Hard-Ass Philosophy

- [SHOULD KNOW][N051b] "These are the rules of the game. I don't give any quarter, and I don't expect any quarter." Never accommodated late exercises on expired OTC options. Budgeted ZERO for back-office errors at new firm.

### Stop-Gunning in Exchange-Traded Markets

- [SHOULD KNOW][N053] Floor traders gun for stops — but the direction of maximum pain is not always the obvious one. Story: everyone positioned for upside stop run → floor went for stops 200 points BELOW instead.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N054b] **"A fast market gives floor brokers a special license to steal, above and beyond their normal license to steal."**

### The Dual-Currency Bond Arbitrage

- [MUST KNOW][N055] **First major trade**: Salomon-issued bond redeemable in sterling OR dollars, grossly mispriced by Salomon itself. UK rates lower than US → forward sterling at huge premium → bond priced as if the option had no premium. Locked-in arbitrage profit. Acquired $135M of $150M outstanding over 1-2 years.

### G-7 Plaza Accord Trade (Sept 1985)

- [MUST KNOW][N058] **G-7 Plaza Accord**: first coordinated currency policy statement. Market initially underestimated it — conditioned by prior ineffective interventions. Paradigm-shift events are initially underestimated.
- [MUST KNOW][N060] **Andy Krieger** sold $60M in New Zealand — first market to open — on wide two-big-figure spreads. Made $6M that day (~25% of annual profits). Pre-established relationships determined who could act.

### The $800M Yen Option Poker Game (1987)

- [MUST KNOW][N062] Bull call spread: long 23,000 yen 54 calls, short 23,000 yen 55 calls. If between 54-55 at expiration: net long ~$800M over the weekend.
- [MUST KNOW][N064] **Information warfare**: counterparty tipped their hand Friday by buying yen cash. Lipschutz was unhedged long but let them believe he had hedged. Weekend news broke in his favor. ~$20M profit. "The mental chess game was more satisfying than the money."

### The $3B Gorbachev Trade (Fall 1988)

- [MUST KNOW][N065] **Low volatility breeds oversizing**: tight range → position creep → $3B short against D-mark. Gorbachev UN speech → dollar up 1% in eight minutes = $30M loss.
- [MUST KNOW][N043] **Failed attempt to cap the market**: sold $300M — "market went right through it." Couldn't cover in illiquid late-afternoon New York.
- [MUST KNOW][N044] **"Tommy, we have a problem"**: Strauss said "we" not "you" — Salomon's culture of shared ownership under pressure. Final loss: $18M (vs potential $70-90M). "Seemed like a major victory."
- [MUST KNOW][N046] **"I'm a much better trader from a bad position"** — fear from a bad position can sharpen decision-making by forcing total concentration on what is actually true.
- [MUST KNOW][N047] Key lesson: misjudged **liquidity**, not direction. The liquidity assumption embedded in the position size was wrong for exactly the conditions that would create urgency to exit.

### Personal Account Blowout

- [SHOULD KNOW][N048] $12K → $250K → blown out in days. September 23, 1982 Granville reversal. Pyramiding puts all the way down. "I just saw it as one major mistake."
- [SHOULD KNOW][N049] Never traded own account again — all attention to Salomon work. Elite performance requires undivided attention.

### Risk Control Framework

- [MUST KNOW][N050] **Lipschutz's risk principles**: (1) Always know exactly where you stand. (2) Don't concentrate on one big trade or correlated trades. (3) Understand risk/reward as it NOW stands, not when entered. (4) "House money" is the most ridiculous concept — all money has equal value.
- [MUST KNOW][N051] **Losing streaks**: impaired confidence → analytical distortion → more losses. Solution: **cut back trading size** to break the feedback loop.

### Fundamental Analysis Framework

- [MUST KNOW][N047b] **What the market is focusing on matters more than what's objectively correct.** Berlin Wall: initial consensus was DM-bullish, then reassessed. "Assess what the market is focusing on at the given moment."
- [MUST KNOW][N054] D-mark view illustrated fundamental analysis: German unification problems, Bundesbank uncertainty, weak Kohl government = bearish DM. How he'd know he was wrong: "essentially the converse."
- [MUST KNOW][N055b] **Scale-in/scale-out**: never full position at once. "You have to figure out how to make money being right only 20 to 30 percent of the time."
- [MUST KNOW][N056] **Exit when fundamentals change even if price moves in your favor**: "You don't want to hold a position when you don't understand what's going on."

### Large Size as Advantage in FX

- [MUST KNOW][N053c] **Counterintuitive**: large size in FX is an advantage — a big buyer pushes market 4%, changes market psychology for days, doesn't need to exit all at once. By 1990, Salomon had $80B in credit lines. **Caveat**: size is catastrophic liability when news creates illiquidity and urgency to exit.

### How Lipschutz Forms a View

- [SHOULD KNOW][N055c] **Scaling in and out**: "I am definitely a scale-in type." Builds as market confirms; exits the same way. Enables staying with winners far longer. "You have to trade at a size such that if you're not exactly right in your timing, you won't be blown out."

### Bank Trading — The Structural Secret

- [MUST KNOW][N057] **Citibank study**: if only trading bid/ask spread with no directional positions, would make ~$600M/year. Actual profit $300-400M implies they LOSE ~$200M on directional trading.
- [MUST KNOW][N058b] **"Frontrunning" legal in interbank FX**: customer sells $2B, bank sells $2.1B — first $100M is the bank's. Ride the coattails.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N059] **Moving $500M+ in FX**: dealer stands up, shouts "I need calls!" — 40 people making 120 calls total in minutes. "A large bank can move an amazing amount of money in a few minutes."

### Dreams, Intuition, and Gut Decisions

- [MUST KNOW][N062b] **Expert intuition = subconscious information processing**: Lipschutz dreamt the exact trade figure and price sequence the night before a release. Didn't trade it ("I don't trade on dreams"). Schwager: suppressed-but-valid trading thesis "comes out in a dream."
- [MUST KNOW][N063] **Gut decisions appropriate only in time-compressed/information-overloaded situations** where prior experience creates faster-than-conscious pattern recognition. Deliberate situations require deliberate analysis.
- [MUST KNOW][N064b] **"Gut feel" = crystallized pattern recognition from vast experience**, operating faster than conscious deliberation.

### The Pain of Trading

- [SHOULD KNOW][N069] "It's the middle of the night, everyone else is asleep, and you're sitting in front of a machine with glowing green numbers, with a pain in your psyche because the market is going against you."
- [SHOULD KNOW][N070] **Trading as a game**: "I'm thirty-six years old, and I almost feel like I have never worked." Would trade for free. "On the other hand, I've been vastly underpaid."

### Characteristics of Superior Traders

- [MUST KNOW][N065b] **Intelligence AND extreme commitment** — both required. "In trading you need BOTH." Obsessive self-interrogation loop: "What am I doing right? Wrong? How can I do better?"
- [SHOULD KNOW][N066] **Great traders never truly "turn off"** — even on a golf course, still processing market information. Interview red flag: "What time do I have to come in?"
- [MUST KNOW][N067] **Courage is the third essential trait** — insight without action is worthless. Many traders have the right analysis but lack nerve to act on out-of-consensus positions.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N068] **Trading is situational, not rule-based** — "it isn't about always at all; it is about each situation."

### Schwager's Postscript on Lipschutz

- [MUST KNOW][N071] Generated over **$500M in profits over eight years** at Salomon. Paper trading: $100K → $29M. Real account: $12K → $250K → blown out.
- [MUST KNOW][N072] **Scale-in/scale-out as practical framework**: ensures at least partial participation, never get the worst outcome. Restrict FULL positions to instances where confidence is greatest.
- [MUST KNOW][N073] **Marty Schwartz rule**: if a feared scenario doesn't materialize as badly as feared, DON'T get out — strong underlying forces must be present.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N074] **Schwager on Lipschutz's blow-up**: subconscious may have resolved the conflict between personal and firm trading by eliminating the personal account. "Everybody gets what they want out of the market."
- [SHOULD KNOW][N075] Buy on extreme strength after confirming news event — don't wait for pullback when fundamental thesis is confirmed.

## Randy McKay — Veteran Trader

### Track Record and Background

- [MUST KNOW][N080] **$2,000 → $70,000** in first seven months. Profitable **18 of 20 years**. Seven consecutive million-dollar-plus years. Each of two oldest managed accounts ($10K start) generated cumulative earnings exceeding $1M.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N083] **Vietnam transformed him from rule-follower to independent thinker**: "When I realized that the leaders didn't necessarily know what they were doing, I became much more independent."
- [SHOULD KNOW][N086] Drawn to markets by mathematical aptitude, competitive game orientation, tolerance for uncertainty hardened by Vietnam.

### The British Pound 1976 — Career-Defining Trade

- [MUST KNOW][N068] Government announced ceiling at $1.72 — market converged just below it. Consensus: sell, no-risk trade. **McKay's read**: converging oscillations = "locked limit-up" = tremendous underlying demand. Went long 200 contracts (5-7x prior max). Rode to $1.90. **P&L: $1.3M personal** — "propelled me into being a hundred-lot trader."
- [MUST KNOW][N069] Exit at $1.90 based on psychological round number AND prior chart point. Had 1,400 total contracts, accidentally sold 400 extra, made money on those too. "The business was just too damn easy and couldn't possibly last forever."

### Core Methodology

- [MUST KNOW][N070] **1970s vs 1990s shift**: "In the 1970s prognostication was 90% and execution 10%, whereas today prognostication is 25% and execution 75%."
- [MUST KNOW][N071b] **Market response to fundamentals, not the fundamentals themselves**: "I watch how the market RESPONDS to fundamental information." Stock market resilient despite recession/war/real estate bust = bullish. Gold broke $400 on Iraq war news then closed lower = bearish.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N072b] Soybeans: drought threat + Soviet exports + low prices → failed to rally → "What could possibly rally the market?" = sell.

### The 1982 Stock Market — Applying Currency Intuition to Equities

- [SHOULD KNOW][N082] Walked into Merrill Lynch with $1M cashier's check, asked for the **least experienced broker** — "I wanted somebody without any opinions." Instructions: invest ¾ in stocks at or near all-time highs. "He followed my instructions exactly, and I did very well." **The expert with wrong methodology is more dangerous than the novice who executes your methodology correctly.**
- [SHOULD KNOW][N084] **Why bullish in 1982**: market up every day without news; real valuations extremely low (nominal prices unchanged for 20 years while inflation skyrocketed); bearish expert consensus (Granville got MORE bearish at new highs). Classic contrarian setup.

### Canadian Dollar Short and Government Intervention

- [SHOULD KNOW][N073b] Short Canadian dollar from 85¢ to under 70¢ over five years (1,000-1,500 contracts). **Halfhearted intervention = trade reinforcement; committed intervention = exit signal.** Exited when PM Mulroney: "We will not allow Chicago speculators to determine the value of our currency."

### "Catch the Easy Part"

- [MUST KNOW][N074] **"I want to catch the easy part"** — the meat of the move. Beginning is hard (direction uncertain), end is hard (choppy profit-taking), middle is easy. "I never try to buy a bottom or sell a top."

### Carter Dollar Rescue — Deceleration as Warning

- [MUST KNOW][N075b] Liquidated over half his position a week before the surprise announcement. **"The upmove was decelerating instead of accelerating."** Market weakness visible even at new all-time highs.
- [MUST KNOW][N076] **Exit rule when hurt: "I get the hell out. It doesn't matter where the market is trading."** Once hurt, decisions are far less objective.

### FOMO — Missing Gold 1979

- [MUST KNOW][N077] Watched the greatest commodity move from sidelines. "Finally ended up buying gold on the exact day it made its high." Lost $750K next day. "Was so relieved the torture was over I couldn't have cared less."
- [MUST KNOW][N078b] **"Be more concerned about moves you're IN than moves you're NOT in."**

### Canadian Dollar 1988 Disaster

- [MUST KNOW][N079] Second-to-last play toward $50M goal. Long 2,000 contracts. Saw 100-point drop in Far East, rationalized as data error, walked out. Lost $7M total, exited at exact bottom. Net: -$5M instead of planned +$15M.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N081] Worst loss was NOT most anxious experience — the 1978 Carter plan loss hit harder because it was his **first big loss**. Psychological impact calibrated to novelty, not just magnitude.

### Market Structure Change

- [MUST KNOW][N085] **True cost of not exiting immediately**: not just the dollar loss — also the opportunity cost of **impaired mental state and reduced position sizing** during recovery. A loss that takes 2 months to recover from means 2 months of sub-optimal trading. Real cost is 2x (direct + opportunity).
- [MUST KNOW][N086b] **Breakouts became traps**: professional dominance removed sideline retail fuel. "False breakouts probably outnumber valid signals." Now buys breaks and sells rallies — exact opposite of 1970s.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N091] On risk of missing moves by always waiting for reactions: "So what? I've got thirty-eight markets. If I miss ten, there will be ten others." The risk of chasing is worse than the risk of missing.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N092] **Markets as chess**: "When your opponent is a farmer or a dentist, you play one way; when your opponent is a professional, you play another." Strategy must match who you're playing against.

### Systematic Trade Review and Position Sizing

- [MUST KNOW][N094] **Daily trade review**: reviewed every trading card at home, classified winners/losers by type with causal attribution. Became more selective.
- [MUST KNOW][N095] **Drawdown protocol**: normal 5-10% risk per trade → after loss drop to 4% → after second loss 2% → keep reducing. Ranged from 3,000 contracts to 10 contracts.
- [MUST KNOW][N096] **"Treat McKay as a trend"**: trading well → better attitude → "think" trades. Trading poorly → "hope" trades. Position size varied 100:1 between hot and cold streaks.

### Winners vs. Losers

- [MUST KNOW][N097] **Trading style must match personality** — "virtually every successful trader I know ultimately ended up with a style suited to his personality." ANY personality can win in the right arena. McKay's brother: meticulous, detail-oriented → became a spreader and thrived.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N098] McKay's own integration: "I take the risk-oriented part of my personality and put it where it belongs: trading. And the conservative part where it belongs: money management."
- [SHOULD KNOW][N099] **Selectivity as the cure**: analyze all trades, identify which types consistently profit, concentrate on those, abandon the rest. "If a trader analyzes his trades and still can't make money, then he probably should try another endeavor."
- [MUST KNOW][N100] **Personality-style mismatch is the norm** — traders adopt the diametrically opposite style to "fulfill some emotional need." Common: system developers who override systems, long-term thinkers who trade short-term.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N101] **McKay's unconventional use of fundamentals**: doesn't try to gauge whether fundamentals are bullish/bearish — focuses on the market's RESPONSE to fundamental news.

## William Eckhardt — The Mathematician

### Background and Track Record

- [MUST KNOW][N102] Partner of **Richard Dennis**. Eckhardt bet trading COULDN'T be taught; Dennis bet it could. The Turtles resolved the wager. Average return 62% on managed accounts over 5 years. Better than 60%/year in own account since 1978.

### Floor Trading

- [MUST KNOW][N105] **Floor trader success = bid-ask spread, not skill**: buy on bid = buying below theoretical value. Many floor traders with "bogus systems" profit from structural advantage alone. Off-floor traders MUST have genuinely valid ideas.
- [MUST KNOW][N106] Small traders fail "not because they're so inevitably wrong, but simply because they can't beat their own transaction costs."

### Statistics and Trading

- [MUST KNOW][N107] **Price distributions are "pathological"** — more variance than normal distribution predicts. Mandelbrot's conjecture: possibly infinite variance. Most standard statistical techniques are invalid; classically-derived risk estimates are significantly understated.
- [MUST KNOW][N108] **October 19, 1987**: 8,000-point S&P drop. Normal distribution says this should happen "a few times in a millennium." Happened within a decade.

### System Design

- [MUST KNOW][N109] **Binary indicator weighting**: if good enough to use, weight equally (1); otherwise don't use (0). Same for trade sizing. Counterintuitive but robust.
- [MUST KNOW][N110] **Chart angles (Gann, 45-degree) are mathematically invalid** — dependent on arbitrary scale choices. Trend lines connecting price points ARE scale-invariant.
- [MUST KNOW][N111] **Postdictive errors**: using information only available after the fact. Subtle form: rules depending on where peaks were — works historically only because you know where the peak was.
- [MUST KNOW][N112] **Overfitting**: 3-4 degrees of freedom fine; 7-8 too many. "Bad" degrees of freedom affect only a few outlier events.
- [MUST KNOW][N113] **Optimization dilemma**: too little = vastly inferior; too much = tells you about the past, not the future.

### System Validity and Falsification

- [MUST KNOW][N114] **"Sock you in the eye" test**: results must be outstanding, not statistically marginal. **Karl Popper** applied to trading: "try to kill your little creation." The better it looks, the harder you should try to disprove it.
- [MUST KNOW][N115] **98% of chart patterns don't work** — human mind creates patterns in random data. "Too fine an eye for pattern will find it anywhere."
- [SHOULD KNOW][N094b] Even careful hand testing is infected by confirmation bias. Eckhardt discounted hand results by 20-50%.
- [MUST KNOW][N095b] **Stochastics hand-test illusion**: looked "terrific" on a flight; computer-tested and lost money. Visual alignment was off by one day.

### Mechanical Systems vs. Discretionary

- [MUST KNOW][N098] **Override only when system clearly violates design intent** or exposure becomes dangerously large. "Don't finagle the system day in and day out."
- [SHOULD KNOW][N099] 1987 crash: system short Eurodollars, but they fell only 5 points when S&Ps fell 8,000. Covered — Eurodollars opened 300 points higher next day. Override was correct.
- [SHOULD KNOW][N101] **Missing an important trade is worse than a bad trade** — bad trades have backup protections (stops); missed trades have nothing.
- [MUST KNOW][N103] **Empirical test**: mechanical system outperformed discretionary trading. "Clearly, my overriding was costing me money, even though I thought otherwise."

### Entry Philosophy

- [SHOULD KNOW][N105b] **Against buying retracements**: psychologically seductive but provides false comfort. "Avoid those things that give you comfort; it's usually false comfort."
- [SHOULD KNOW][N106] **Why trend-following works**: people focus on broad probable outcomes, ignore low-probability scenarios. As neglected scenarios "pop into view," market discounts them discontinuously — creating trending moves.

### Commercial Systems and Contrary Opinion

- [SHOULD KNOW][N107] **~50 commercial systems reviewed — only 1 had any value** (and only as an element). Root cause: overfitting. "If a system truly had good profitability, a developer who invested years building it would want to use it, not sell it."
- [SHOULD KNOW][N108] **Contrary opinion**: theoretically sound, empirically flawed. Consensus numbers model "a very nonrepresentative group of traders." Research finding: high bullish consensus is marginally profitable to BUY — opposite of standard application.

### RSI, Stochastics, Popular Indicators

- [MUST KNOW][N109b] **RSI and stochastics: "nearly worthless"** — what they make during consolidations, they lose during trends.
- [MUST KNOW][N110b] **Prior/posterior probability confusion**: P(top | property X) ≠ P(property X | top). "If 85% of tops have property X but X occurs often elsewhere, using it as a signal will rip you to shreds."
- [MUST KNOW][N111b] **Cyclical analysis definitively fails** — Fourier analysis finds nothing systematic. Alleged cycles are "practically random."

### System Proliferation

- [MUST KNOW][N115b] **Red Queen Hypothesis**: "run as fast as you can just to stay in place." System traders must continuously innovate.
- [MUST KNOW][N113b] **Evidence against random walk is "staggering"** — hundreds of traders profited from mechanical systems year after year; taught to others who also succeeded.

### Money Management

- [MUST KNOW][N117] **Money management is more important than the price model — and easier.** Most traders optimize the wrong thing (signals) while neglecting position sizing.
- [MUST KNOW][N118] **Logarithmic utility function problem**: unbounded utility eventually tells you to "bet the ranch." **St. Petersburg Paradox**: no sane person would gamble a billion-dollar net worth on a coin flip for ANY prize. Therefore use only bounded utility functions.
- [MUST KNOW][N119] **The "whale graph"**: plot performance vs position size — nearly linear, then plummets past optimum. "An average position size only modestly larger than the theoretical optimum gives a negative performance." Never plan to risk more than 2%.

### The Turtle Program

- [SHOULD KNOW][N120] **Intelligence is not the key**: "I haven't seen much correlation between good trading and intelligence. Average intelligence is enough. Beyond that, emotional makeup is more important."
- [MUST KNOW][N121] **Trading CAN be taught** — Eckhardt was proven wrong. "The answer has to be an unqualified yes."
- [MUST KNOW][N123] **BUT: "it's much easier to learn what you should do than to do it."** Good systems violate normal human tendencies. "To win you have to act like the minority."
- [SHOULD KNOW][N122] Whether Turtle systems degraded with proliferation: with hundreds of millions under management, hard for that not to affect performance. Tied to Red Queen point.

### Human Nature as the Enemy

- [MUST KNOW][N124] **Two instinctive biases**: prefer sure gain over lottery (take profits) but prefer lottery over sure loss (hold losers). "You can't go broke taking profits — that's precisely how many traders DO go broke." **Success rate is the least important performance statistic.**

- [MUST KNOW][N131] **Large profits destabilize MORE than large losses** — breed overconfidence and complacency. "Losses make you strong and profits make you weak."

- [MUST KNOW][N133] **Asymmetric thinking rule**: if a thought about market behavior is COMFORTING when in a bad trade, it's wrong. If DISCOMFORTING when in a good trade, it's probably right.
- [MUST KNOW][N134] **Trading addiction**: intermittent reinforcement (random pleasure + pain) is the MOST addictive alternative. "Addictiveness is the reason why so many players who make fortunes leave the game broke."

### Fundamental vs. Technical and Options

- [MUST KNOW][N297] **Richard Dennis exchange**: Dennis: "I use zero percent fundamental information." - [MUST KNOW][N302] **Why mispricings ALWAYS exist in options**: "The notion that the market will trade at its precise theoretical fair value implies that someone will hold it there without getting paid. - [MUST KNOW][N303] **Black-Scholes flaw: fat tails**: Standard model underweights probability of extreme moves. Actual distributions have fatter tails. Result: standard models underprice out-of-the-money options. "Knowing that the distribution isn't log-normal only opens a can of worms."

- [MUST KNOW][N306] **Implied volatility beats historical volatility
  as a predictor**: "The implied volatility is a statement of what
  all the players in the market, having cast their votes, believe is
  a fair price for future volatility. Historical volatility is
  nothing more than a number representing past volatility." Using
  historical vol to identify "overpriced" options may be completely
  misleading if the market correctly anticipates higher future vol.

- [SHOULD KNOW][N307] **CRT never compared options to historical vol
  — only to other options**: Their method: make a bid/ask spread
  where you can find another trade to lay off the volatility risk
  and still leave a profit margin. Pure relative value arbitrage —
  exploit inconsistencies within the options surface, not absolute
  price level vs. a model.

## Chapter: Blair Hull — Getting the Edge

### Blackjack to Trading

- [MUST KNOW][N316] **Blair Hull's background**: Founded Hull Trading Company. Came to trading through blackjack — consistent winning in both requires strategy and discipline, not luck. After casinos banned his team, found option mispricings matched the same principles.

- [MUST KNOW][N317] **HTC performance record**:
  - Started with $25,000 in late 1976 → multiplied 20x by start of

  - Subsequent years: averaged ~100% per year (excluding sabbaticals)
  - HTC launched 1985 with $1 million → $90 million by mid-1991

  - Profitable in 58 of 69 months; only 5 months had net trading

### Team Blackjack Principles

- [MUST KNOW] **Team approach multiplies edge**: Edge × number of opportunities to apply that edge. More players → closer to mathematical certainty. Each player can size bets based on combined capital base without increasing individual risk. Same logic behind fund pooling and managed accounts.

- [MUST KNOW] **Accuracy vs. complexity trade-off**: More complicated counting methods → more errors under casino conditions. A more sophisticated model that a trader cannot execute faithfully is worse than a simpler model executed with discipline.

- [MUST KNOW] **Ego as operational risk**: Team collapsed because a member (Ken Uston) wanted to write a book. "The people who want to be recognized as the greatest traders are probably not the greatest traders. Egos get in the way of the process. You never want to be the largest player in the pit."

- [MUST KNOW] **Casinos tolerate counting because losers outnumber winners**: Only ~1 in 500 has the discipline. The market profits from the gap between people who know the method and people who can apply it.

- [MUST KNOW] **Edge + money management formula**: "All you need is a mathematical advantage and the money management controls to assure that you stay in the game. Everything else takes care of itself."

- [SHOULD KNOW][N329] **Strike pinning at expiration**: Hull's
  statistical research: stocks are about twice as likely to finish
  within one-quarter of a point of the nearest strike price at
  option expiration vs. what would be expected by chance. Mechanism:
  speculators tend to hold slightly in-the-money calls; as
  expiration approaches, they sell; market makers must sell stock to
  hedge → pushes stock toward the strike. Hull actively plays this.

- [SHOULD KNOW][N326] **Hull's "less obvious offsets" strategy**: He
  was one of the slowest floor traders — forced to look for hedges
  in less correlated markets. His slowness became an institutional
  competitive advantage — he learned to find less-contested offset
  markets. Now automated.

### Gulf War and Market Evolution

- [MUST KNOW][N323] **Hull's Gulf War S&P trade**: January 15, 1991.
  Consensus: market would fall 150 points if war started. Hull's
  thesis: uncertainty had to diminish regardless of outcome,
  therefore long. Put on half the position before the deadline,
  other half after war started. Never added the second half because
  market opened sharply higher. Result: ~$4 million in about thirty
  minutes. His head trader booked it in the "LTG account" — Hull
  had previously said "What do you think, you have a line to God?"
  Directional trades account for only about 5% of the firm's total
  profits.

- [MUST KNOW][N325] **The arms race in arbitrage**: "This is always a horse race, and unless you're running very fast, they're going to catch you." Intermarket trades now required for meaningful returns.

- [MUST KNOW][N327] **Zero delta portfolio with 2-second rebalancing**: Net delta reevaluated within two seconds every time any of 25 traders makes a trade. One designated trader acts as "air traffic controller" — responsible solely for hedging. "The beauty of this system is that the cost of hedging is very small."

- [MUST KNOW][N328] **Covered call = short put — "fraud" on the public**: Mathematically identical to selling a put. Brokers promote covered calls because they generate double commissions. "If you want to guarantee an inferior strategy, do covered calls."

### Money Management and Edge

- [MUST KNOW][N332] **Money management myth destroyed**: "All that good money management will do for a poor strategy is to assure that you will lose money more slowly." With a negative-edge strategy, your best chance is to risk everything on one trade — the longer you play a negative edge, the more certain ruin becomes. The correct formulation: "Even a good system can lose money with poor money management."

## Chapter: Jeff Yass — The Mathematics of Strategy

- [MUST KNOW][N334] **Susquehanna Investment Group — market as poker game**: "The market is like a giant poker game, and you have to pay very close attention to the skill level of your opponents." "If you're the sixth best poker player in the world and you play with the five best players, you're going to lose."

### Poker-Options Parallels

- [MUST KNOW][N338] **Poker strategy = betting strategy, not odds memorization**: Susquehanna trains all option traders using poker strategy. The transferable skill: what information do you get when your opponent bets? What do you give up when you bet? What do you give up when you don't bet?

- [MUST KNOW][N339] **Checking with the best hand**: When opponent has >50% probability of betting, checking is superior — inducing the bet then raising extracts double. Options analogy: maximize gains, not number of wins. Worked example: option worth 3¼, buying at 2¾ with 60% fill probability has higher expected value ($3,000) than certain fill at 3 ($2,500).

- [MUST KNOW][N340] **Four aces and the informed counterparty**: Poker — you have four aces showing, opponent shows potential straight flush. Correct answer: PASS. If he can't beat you, he folds and you win nothing extra; if he can beat you, he raises and you lose more. Options analogy: if a broker with suspected superior information is bidding significantly more than your model, don't take the other side.

- [MUST KNOW][N341] **Monty Hall problem**: Always switch — 2/3 probability by switching vs. 1/3 by staying. 100-door version makes it intuitive. Trading application: conditional probability routinely confounds even expert intuition.

- [MUST KNOW][N342] **"There is never any money to be made in the obvious conclusions."**

### Options Below Intrinsic Value

- [MUST KNOW][N343] **When an institution sells options below intrinsic value**: Nine times out of ten they know something you don't. The institution may have 1 million shares to sell, knows it will push price below your "locked-in" profit level. Conditional probability: given they want this trade, which is more likely — they're writing you a risk-free check, or they know something?

### Evolution of the Options Market

- [SHOULD KNOW][N344] **Three phases of edge**:
  - Phase 1 (1981-early 1980s): basic Black-Scholes + common sense
    sufficient; buy at low implied vol, sell related option at higher
    vol. Many traders didn't understand you could buy an underpriced
    call while bearish by simultaneously shorting the stock.
  - Phase 2 (mid/late 1980s): everybody understood volatility
    relationships; cross-option vol discrepancies eliminated.
  - Phase 3 (now): major battle has shifted to skewness — which
    direction vol is skewed and why.

### Skewness and Efficient Markets

- [MUST KNOW][N345] **Skewness: OTM puts priced higher than OTM calls — rational**: Much greater probability of financial panic on downside than equivalent upside euphoria. Market did NOT price puts higher until after the 1987 crash — even though asymmetry always logically existed.

- [MUST KNOW][N346] **No option pricing model adequately describes the real world**: "There's no way you can construct a model that can come close to being as informed as the market." Pricing decisions are always judgment calls — model output is the starting point, not the conclusion. "Traders are made, not born."

- [MUST KNOW][N347] **Takeover situations expose the deepest flaw in standard models**: The 50/50 tick assumption collapses in binary outcomes. NCR/AT&T example: OTM calls near $110 essentially worthless; OTM 90 puts have significant value. Mathematical traders who sold puts "because they appeared overpriced" got taken to the cleaners.

- [MUST KNOW][N348] **When a knowledgeable market maker bids away from your model**: Assume 9 times out of 10 he's right. The signal in his price tells you something about the stock — update everything, not just that one strike.

- [MUST KNOW][N349] **Information flows to the options market first**: "Frequently, the information will show up first in the option market." Leverage vastly higher than stock. Marion Labs employee: bought 500 July 25 calls at $1 → worth $10 next day ($500,000 from $50,000). Santa Fe 1981: floor traders lost ~$1 million overnight on 1,000 calls that went from $1/16 to $10.

- [MUST KNOW][N352] **Yass does zero directional trading**: "The market's wisdom is far greater than mine." "My guess about where interest rates will be in the next twenty years is better than that of almost any economist, because all I have to do is look at where the bond market is trading."

- [SHOULD KNOW][N350] **Combustion Engineering — picked off by a
  board member**: Options rarely traded; order to buy several hundred
  options. Susquehanna sold 300 of the 25 calls at ~$2.50; 10
  minutes later trading halted; company taken over at $39. Loss:
  over $350,000. Buyer was a board director of acquiring company.

- [SHOULD KNOW][N351] **Perverse economics of SEC enforcement**:
  Every option trade carries a risk premium for the possibility the
  other side has inside information. "It's really the average
  investor who ends up paying for insider trading through the wider
  bid/ask spreads." Large option volume almost always precedes

- [MUST KNOW][N353] **Diversification is the only free lunch**: "If
  you invest and don't diversify, you're literally throwing out

- [SHOULD KNOW][N333] **Schwager's inference — Hull's rules for

- [MUST KNOW][N396] "Scared money never wins." Undercapitalization is not just financial — it's psychological, corrupting every decision downstream.

**Observation 33: Think Twice When the Market Lets You Off the Hook**
- [MUST KNOW][N397] If you held through a feared event and get a favorable open instead, that price strength is bullish information. Before dumping in relief, ask why the market isn't doing what everyone feared.

**Observation 34: A Mind Is a Terrible Thing to Close**
- [SHOULD KNOW][N398] Blake entered trading specifically to prove
  prices were random — when he realized he was wrong, he became a
  trader. Driehaus: "The mind is like a parachute; it's good only
  when it's open."

**Observation 35: Markets Are an Expensive Place for Excitement**
- [SHOULD KNOW][N399] Larry Hite: "I don't trade for excitement; I
  trade to win." Faulkner describes a trader who blew out because
  profitable trading was "too boring" — he needed excitement, so he
  abandoned his profitable method.

**Observation 36: The Calm State**
- [MUST KNOW][N400] Basso: "If instead of saying, 'I'm going to do
  this trade,' you say, 'I'm going to watch myself do this trade,'
  all of a sudden you find that the process is a lot easier." The
  observer-self framing removes ego from the outcome.

**Observation 37: Identify and Eliminate Stress**
- [SHOULD KNOW][N401] "Stress in trading is a sign that something is
  wrong." If greatest stress source is indecision about exiting
  losers → solution is to enter a protective stop order every time.
  Key insight: the solution to trading stress is usually structural
  (change the system) not psychological (try harder).

**Observation 38: Pay Attention to Intuition**
- [SHOULD KNOW][N402] "Intuition is simply experience that resides in
  the subconscious mind." The conscious mind's analysis can be
  corrupted by current position, resistance to changing prior
  forecasts, ego. The subconscious is not inhibited by these
  constraints.

**Observation 39: Life's Mission**
- [SHOULD KNOW][N403] Many exceptional traders feel trading is what
  they were meant to do. John Grinder's definition of mission:
  "What do you love so much that you would pay to do it?" Intrinsic
  motivation may be a prerequisite for sustained effort to reach
  elite performance.

**Observation 41: Markets Are Non-Random**
- [MUST KNOW][N405] "It is not the magnitude of the winnings but the consistency that underpin my belief." Blake's 25:1 ratio of winning to losing months. Markets are getting harder as professionals account for a growing proportion of activity.

**Observation 42: Keep Trading in Perspective**
- [NICE TO KNOW][N406] "There is more to life than trading." The
  simplest of the 42 observations — standing alone as a deliberate
  contrast to the book's intensity.

### Schwager's Personal Reflection

- [MUST KNOW][N407] **Most important lesson from both books**: Personality-method fit is non-negotiable. "No trader revealed to me any great market secrets." Schwager loves the puzzle-solving but does not like the emotional experience of intraday decisions. Committed to system trading — "my progress accelerated and I felt that the approach fit like a glove." "It took me over a decade to figure out my natural direction."

## Bill Lipschutz Supplementary Material

### FX Trading Edge

- [MUST KNOW] **FX is a 24-hour market**: First person at Salomon Brothers with a Telerate terminal at home. "Foreign exchange is a twenty-four-hour market. It doesn't go to sleep when you leave at 5 P.M."

- [MUST KNOW] **Interbank network access is the primary differentiator**: "Those of us who did well were generally the ones who were accepted by the interbank circle." Traders who stayed aloof "couldn't make any money."

- [MUST KNOW] **"Being right early is the same as being wrong"**: "What is important is to assess what the market is focusing on at the given moment." Early contrarian identification of a thesis does not create edge; edge comes from identifying WHEN the market shifts to pricing that thesis.

### Key Trades

- [MUST KNOW] **1983 Philadelphia currency options arbitrage**: Specialist quoting deep-ITM option ~100 points off fair value. Bought 50 contracts, immediately hedged, locked in risk-free profit. Kept buying in lots of 50. Goldman Sachs followed. Specialist sold 300-400 contracts into a market with 200-300 daily volume.

- [MUST KNOW] **Letting the specialist off the hook**: Broke all trades after the first 50 — forfeiting a large windfall. Reasoning: holding him would put him out of business, bad for the exchange and the currency options product. Goldman refused to break their 150 contracts. Years later, the specialist became head trader at the largest market maker and "always made it very difficult for Goldman."

- [MUST KNOW] **Zero-error back-office standard**: "Zero. We don't have back-office errors." Never missed an options expiration. "If you put in enough fail-safes, you don't make errors."

- [MUST KNOW] **Plaza Accord**: G-7 September 1985 — first coordinated multilateral policy statement to lower the dollar. Markets initially failed to react correctly. Dollar rebounded modestly in Tokyo. Crowd skepticism from years of ineffective intervention made this the contrarian setup.

- [MUST KNOW] **Andy's New Zealand trade**: After Plaza Accord, Andy went into the NZ dollar market — first to open. Salomon had established relationships through consistent $20-50 million Monday morning volume. Sold $60 million into spreads "literally two big figures wide." Hit bids six big figures below Friday's close. Lipschutz: "I wouldn't have done that."

- [MUST KNOW] **1987 yen bull call spread**: Long 23,000 yen 54 calls, short 23,000 yen 55 calls. Each side represented nearly $800 million. Between-strikes risk at expiration created an $800M overnight poker game with a single counterparty. Bluffed the counterparty into believing he'd hedged (hadn't). Weekend news moved dollar lower against yen. ~$20 million profit. "The thing that was so great about the trade was not the money but the mental chess game."

- [MUST KNOW] **Gorbachev D-mark short — low-vol trap**: In fall
  1988, currency markets were quiet; D-mark in tight range. Trader
  scaled up position size to capture smaller moves and maintain
  same dollar P&L — short approximately $3 billion against the
  D-mark. Gorbachev announced troop reductions → dollar
  immediately started moving up with no liquidity → ~$30 million
  loss in one afternoon. Pattern: low-vol → bigger size → same-
  sized adverse move = catastrophe. Position size should be anchored
  to volatility-adjusted risk, not dollar P&L targets. Scaling up
  in quiet markets because "nothing is happening" is a known path
  to blow-ups.

### Market Structure Insights

- [MUST KNOW] **Stop-gunning is real but unpredictable in direction**:
  Setup: large cluster of stops accumulated ~40-50 ticks above the
  market. Clerk tip: floor would "gun for the stops above." Trader
  planned to sell heavily once upside stops hit, expecting artificial
  breakout to reverse. What happened: market moved DOWN 50, then
  100, then 200+ points — floor went for stops BELOW. Even advance
  intelligence doesn't guarantee direction.

- [SHOULD KNOW] **Low-liquidity sessions amplify stop-run risk**: The
  episode occurred on a cash market holiday. Thin markets require
  less capital to move prices to stop levels. Be especially cautious
  holding stops near clustered levels on holidays, early closes,
  and option expiry thin periods.

- [MUST KNOW] **"Fast market" designation as execution shield**: When
  an exchange declares fast market conditions, floor brokers are
  legally exempt from being held responsible for failing to fill
  orders within the day's traded range. A trader bidding at multiple
  price levels during a sharp break may get filled on only a
  fraction even though thousands of lots traded at those exact
  levels. "A fast market gives the floor brokers a special license
  to steal, above and beyond their normal license to steal."

- [SHOULD KNOW] **Crowded-long setup → downside vulnerability**:
  Everyone positioned long in anticipation of upside stops being
  taken out. Because the long side was crowded, direction of greatest
  price vulnerability was actually to the downside. Consensus
  positioning reveals where the market is most fragile.

## Supplementary: Hull/Options Market Insights

### Fat Tails and Short OTM Options

- [MUST KNOW] **Selling deep OTM options = slow profits, catastrophic
  ruin**: "That kind of strategy only leads to consistent profits
  until a catastrophe arises. Then you lose it all, plus some." Two
  1981 takeover trades wiped out approximately half of cumulative
  profits built over 4.5 years of thousands of trades. First trade:
  financed a trader on AMEX selling OTM options in a takeover
  situation → lost ~one-third of capital. Second (Kennecott):
  overnight move so enormous the loss was still substantial despite
  small position. The second hit came only ~a week after the first.

- [MUST KNOW] **Market maker role = risk transfer, not speculation**:
  Profits from exploiting mispricings between speculator and
  institutional flows, not directional bets. Speculators cluster on
  one side (buying OTM calls); institutions on the other (selling
  long-term calls against long stock). Smart market maker inserts
  between both flows, balancing the book so net risk approaches zero.

- [MUST KNOW] **Relative value as the real edge**: Doesn't matter
  which pricing model you use, as long as you apply it consistently.
  Concern is not whether the market is mispriced vs. the model in
  absolute terms, but whether individual options are mispriced
  relative to each other. Adjust model so at-the-money implied price
  matches market; any other strikes that don't fall in line are
  relative mispricings to trade.

- [MUST KNOW] **Implied vs. historical volatility bias**: Implied
  volatility consistently higher than realized — structural evidence
  options are systematically overpriced. But the implied risk of
  consistent selling is too great. "The buyer of options has the
  disadvantage." Having an edge and having an acceptable risk/reward
  are two separate questions.

- [SHOULD KNOW] **Takeover clues in options markets**: Option trading
  significantly beyond where it should be; unusual price moves in
  the underlying; sudden increases in volume; jump in implied
  volatility for OTM calls. These are warnings that informed buyers
  may be active — "there may be a wolf out there."

- [MUST KNOW] **Infrequent directional trades = higher hit rate**:
  Gets a strong directional idea only "maybe a couple of times a
  year"; crucially, "these types of trading ideas are infrequent,
  they're usually right." Selectivity filters out marginal ideas.

- [MUST KNOW] **DC-10 crash scare as contrarian opportunity**: Bought
  thousands of calls in McDonnell Douglas during multiple DC-10
  crashes. His wife: "Those planes are never going to fly again.
  We're going to be broke." Trader's read: maximum negative
  sentiment = maximum mispricing of calls. "When nobody wants to
  touch the market, that's the time you have to step up." "You
  could say my wife taught me to be a contrarian."

- [MUST KNOW] **1987 crash — going long during peak panic**: October
  20, 1987 (day after 508-point Dow crash). Due to fear + increased
  capital requirements, couldn't find anyone to execute orders.
  Went to the pit himself. Heard CME considering trading halt on
  S&P futures. "The fear was all out of proportion to reality. I had
  to be a buyer." Bid 285 → bought 100 lots; then another 50 at
  285 — the ONLY trades at 285. Market closed at 400 that day.

### Turtle Traders

- [MUST KNOW] **Core Turtle psychological lesson**: "Don't worry about what the markets will do; worry about what YOU will do in response." Markets are uncontrollable; the only controllable variable is your own response.

- [MUST KNOW] **Howard Seidler — most ebullient Turtle**: 34% compounded annual return since 1984. During interview, Schwager assumed he was on a hot streak; actually down 16% — his second worst six-month period. Emotional resilience decoupled from recent performance.

### Erroneous Quotes and Professional Conduct

- [SHOULD KNOW] **"Honest attempt" convention for off-market quotes**:
  In fast-moving interbank markets, dealers sometimes quote a price
  one full big figure off. Convention: receiving party must give
  dealer a chance to correct. If the dealer confirms, the quote
  stands. Lipschutz enforced this strictly — refused to cancel or
  split when counterparties came back wanting to undo the trade.
  Courtesy (checking the price) is required; charity (giving back
  windfall gains) is not.

### Mispriced Bond Arbitrage

- [MUST KNOW] **Classic arbitrage: mispriced dual-currency bond**:
  Bond issued by Salomon Brothers redeemable in either sterling or
  dollars was "grossly mispriced." Priced as if sterling's redemption
  assumed no premium over spot, despite forward sterling trading at
  a huge premium. The trade: buy the bond, sell sterling forward at
  the large premium — locked in profit regardless of rate moves.
  When flagged as mispriced, institution spent nine hours running
  analysis past every "quant jock in the house" before accepting.
  Spent 1-2 years patiently bidding in secondary market, ultimately
  acquiring $135 million of the $150 million outstanding.

## Appendix: Options Basics

- [NICE TO KNOW] **Core mechanics**: Call = right to buy at strike
  price; Put = right to sell. Buyer's max loss = premium paid;
  seller's theoretical risk = unlimited. Option premium = intrinsic
  value + time value. Three factors driving time value: (1) strike
  vs. market price, (2) time remaining, (3) volatility. Future
  volatility is never precisely known — must be estimated. The
  future volatility implied by option premiums is called implied
  volatility.
